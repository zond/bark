# Mail held for a sleepy node that moved to another anchor

**Status:** research note, 2026-11-04, with roadmap D106 round five.
Spec references are to `protocol.md` by section name. A measurement
only: the user decided, 2026-11-04, that the mesh does not handle this
mail (below).

## Summary

Round four counted, in the city at four times the rate after four hours,
6 559 messages held at holders, a third of them for fixed sleepy nodes
whose beats the holder had not received, and read those beats as lost
while the holder was busy or colliding. The holder's beat records say
otherwise: nine in ten of those beats were sent on a channel the holder
was not listening on, or out of its reach. With the message log now
saying, at the end of the run, whether the holder is still the node's
anchor, most of the mail held for fixed sleepy nodes turns out to be at
a relay that is not, or is no longer, the node's anchor. The node moved
to another anchor, before the mail arrived or after, and nothing tells
the old holder or the new anchor. Mail held at the node's own anchor
because the anchor missed a beat while it transmitted or a frame met
the beat is a small share. Sending blind at the time of a missed beat
would reach that small share only, and cannot be timed: beats come at
random.

In the city at four times the rate after four hours (round five's
build, seed 1), 5 680 messages were held at holders; 2 961 of them for
fixed sleepy nodes, and of those 2 504, 85 %, at a relay that was not
the node's anchor at the end: 768 whose node switched after the mail
arrived (a median 6.5 min after), 770 whose node switched between the
text's writing and its arrival, 966 at a relay that was never the
node's anchor. At the node's own anchor 252 waited for a dark node, 159
had seen no beat since they arrived, and 45, 1.5 %, had lost beats to
another frame or to the anchor's own transmission.

## What the holder does

A holder sends a sleepy node its mail in the window after each beat it
hears (spec "Delivery"). Outside the windows it sends one message with
the long preamble if its bucket holds it beyond everything it has to
send; once such a delivery goes unanswered it waits for the next beat it
hears (`relay/delivery.rs` `try_deliver`). A beat comes a random half to
one beat period after the last (spec "Beats"), so a holder that missed
one has no time at which to expect the next: spec "Relay choice" keeps
an expected time for the anchor's heartbeat, which the sleepy node
waits for, not for the node's beat. Spec "Owner" has the owner queue
the mail whether or not it hears the node now: that is about queueing
mail for a node the owner does not know, not about sending to it.

## How it was measured

The simulator's message log (`--messages`) gains, per message, the node
that queued it for its destination, and at the end of the run whether
that holder is the destination's anchor, whether the destination is
still sleepy, when it last changed anchor and how many times, and
whether the holder and its new anchor hear each other (commit `f24bdc7`).
Mail held at the end is classed by those columns and, for mail at the
node's own anchor, by what became of most of the node's beats there:
the node was dark, the holder was off its channel, out of reach,
transmitting, or a frame met the beat at the holder. The builds are
round five's (`HOP_WAIT` = 30 s); the classing does not depend on the
gate.

A node that switched after its mail arrived did so a median 140 to 160 s
later (10th percentile 18 to 27 s, 90th 610 to 1 020 s). The nodes
whose mail is held elsewhere changed anchor two to four times in the
half hour, the first choice after switching on included. Of the mail
ever queued at a relay that was not the node's anchor when it queued it
(seed 1), 290 of 884 were delivered, against 10 811 of 11 626 at the
node's anchor.

The same classes at the design's rate, seed 1: 30 messages held at the
end, 19 of them at a relay not the node's anchor; at eight times 211,
110 of them so. So the share is not a creature of overload; overload
only adds to the mail in flight when a node moves.

## What it shows

- **The holder's missed beats are not the main loss.** At the node's
  own anchor, beats met by another frame or lost while the anchor
  transmitted hold 8 to 11 % of the mail held for fixed sleepy nodes in
  the district at 32 times. Sending blind at the time of a beat would
  reach these only, and a beat's time cannot be foreseen.
- **Most of it waits at a former anchor, or at the owner of an address
  that was never the node's.** The spec says so in "Delivery queue":
  nothing covers a node that has moved out of reach of every holder that
  has its mail. The mail waits there until its place is needed.
- **The node's new anchor never learns of it.** No packet carries it
  there. In a third of the cases the old holder and the new anchor hear
  each other.
- **Not the mesh's to handle**, the user decided, 2026-11-04. The mesh
  holds mail bounded by space, never by age (spec "Delivery queue");
  whether a text reached its reader, and sending it again when it did
  not, is the application's job, not the relay's.

## What it does not show

- **Why nodes switch so often.** Two to four changes in half an hour,
  the boot's first choice included, is more than spec "Relay choice"
  intends for still nodes; whether the checks after missed heartbeats,
  which load makes more likely, or the margin, or the simulator's
  fading cause it was not looked into.
- **Walkers and carried nodes**, whose mail at a former anchor is the
  same problem with a moving address, were left out of the counts.
- **A long run in the district.** Half an hour shows what is held, not
  what ends; the city's four hours do.
