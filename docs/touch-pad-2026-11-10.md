# The touch pad, from the sensor's datasheet (2026-11-10)

What the TTP223-BA6 on P0.11 does, from its maker's datasheet, what the
bench of `docs/touch-pad-2026-11-09.md` means in its light, and how the
firmware reads the pad because of it (roadmap D175). The circuit is in
that note: the sensor on the chip's own supply, its output Q on P0.11
with no resistor, its option pins TOG and AHLB left open.

## The datasheets

Two sheets from the sensor's maker, Tontek, copies fetched 2026-11-10:

- *TTP223-BA6, 1 key touch pad detector IC*, Preliminary, Ver 1.0,
  2008-08-15, 8 pages (the part's own sheet).
- *TTP223, 1 key touch pad detector IC*, Preliminary, Ver 1.0,
  2008-04-07, 11 pages (the family's sheet, with every option pin;
  <https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/Grove-Touch_Sensor/res/TTP223.pdf>).

What they say:

- **Output.** Q is a CMOS push-pull output (BA6, Pad description), 8 mA
  sink and 4 mA source at 3 V (BA6, DC/AC characteristics).
- **Option pins.** AHLB and TOG each have a 28 kΩ pull-low (BA6, DC/AC
  characteristics, R_PL) and read 0 when left open (BA6, Function
  description 4, Option pin). TOG 0, AHLB 0 is direct mode, active high
  (BA6, Function description 2, Output mode): Q is low while the pad is
  not touched and high while it is. The maker's pin table for the board
  agrees: "Touch Button, P0.11, High level active" (the T-Echo
  repository's `README.MD`, fetched 2026-11-10).
- **Fixed options.** In the BA6 package, LPMB is fixed to 0, MOTB to 1
  and SLRFTB to 1 (TTP223, Order information, 2. TTP223-BA6). LPMB 0 is
  low power mode (TTP223, Function description 4). MOTB 1 is "Infinite
  (Disable maximum on time)" (TTP223, Function description 3, Maximum
  key on duration time): the 100 s timer that would end a stuck
  detection is not in this part. A touch, or anything the sensor takes
  for one, holds Q at its touched level for as long as it lasts.
- **Power-on.** "After power-on have about 0.5sec stable-time, during
  the time do not touch the key pad, And the function is disabled"
  (BA6, Features). Q's level during that time is not given.
- **Calibration.** "Auto calibration for life": in low power mode it
  recalibrates about every 4 s, and after a touch is released about
  16 s after the release (BA6, Features). Nothing says it recalibrates
  while it detects a touch, and with no maximum on time, a detection
  that does not end is never recalibrated away.
- **Timing.** Response within 220 ms in low power mode and 60 ms in
  fast mode at 3 V (BA6, DC/AC characteristics, T_R). It samples about
  every 160 ms in low power mode and every 20 ms in fast mode, which it
  enters at a touch and keeps for about 12 s after the release (BA6,
  Function description 3, Low power mode, and its figure).
- **Supply.** "If the supply voltage drift or shift quickly, maybe
  causing sensitivity anomalies or false detections" (BA6, Application
  circuit, P.S. 2).

So the pad on this board rests low and is high while touched. Since
the sensor runs from the chip's supply, it calibrates once when that
supply comes up, a cell plugged in or the USB cable with no cell, and
not at a reset of the chip.

The maker's example (`examples/Factory/Factory.ino`) reads the pin with
the pull-down and acts on its rising edge, with no wait after boot and
no debounce.

## The bench, read again

Version 16 read the level at boot and logged it, then waited for the
pin to be high, waited 20 ms, its debounce, and logged `pad: touched`
if it was still high, then waited for low. On c367 and f4f5,
`pad: reads high untouched` and then `pad: touched` 20 ms later is that
code finding the pin already high: not an edge, the pin was high all
along. Nothing more on a real touch means the pin never went low: a
touch that took it low, active low, would have logged `pad: touched` at
its release. So on those two boards Q stayed high, which by the
datasheet is the sensor reporting a touch, and one it cannot end, its
maximum on time being disabled. A calibration made while something
covered the pad, a hand holding the board as the cable went in, or a
supply that moved during it, could leave it so; only taking its supply
away, the cell and the cable both, would make it calibrate again. This
is a reading of the datasheet, not yet a measurement.

On 3112 the pin read low with the pull-down and never moved. That is
either a sensor at rest that does not detect a touch, or no sensor
driving the pin at all, which a pull-down alone cannot tell apart.

## How the firmware reads it

`bark_screen::pad`, tested on the host, fed by `bark-fw/src/button.rs`:

1. Nothing is read for 1 s after boot, twice the datasheet's "about
   0.5 sec" of calibration, since "about" has no bound. The firmware's
   clock starts after the bootloader, so this is at least as long after
   the sensor's power-on.
2. The pin is read with the chip's pull up and then down (13 kΩ,
   nRF52840 Product Specification, GPIO electrical specification). Q
   drives 4 mA or more, so a driven pin holds its level against either;
   a pin that reads high pulled up and low pulled down is driven by
   nothing, and the pad is taken as dead and left disconnected. Else the
   level it holds is where it rests, and it is read with no pull from
   then on.
3. A level counts once it has held 40 ms, two of the sensor's fast-mode
   samples.
4. A touch is a change from the resting level that lasts at least 40 ms
   and at most 10 s, counted once the pin has been back for 40 ms. 10 s
   is over three times the longest hold the node asks of a person, the
   user button's 3 s.
5. A change that lasts longer than 10 s is not a person's touch: the
   level it holds becomes the resting level. A sensor that starts
   reporting a touch it cannot end then gives no touch, and a pad
   covered for a while answers again once it is uncovered for 10 s.

The resting level is learned rather than taken from the datasheet: the
rules are the same either way, and a board whose sensor rests high,
by a fault or by a revision that ties AHLB high, still answers a touch
if its pin moves.

The boot log says what the probe found: `pad: rests low`, `pad: rests
high`, or `pad: follows the pull, nothing drives it: taken as dead`; and later `pad: touched` and `pad: held 10 s, rests
high now`. An open build logs every edge, `pad: edge, high`.

What the bench found, and looks for, on each board:

- **f4f5, 2026-11-10, the first boot of this rule.** `pad: rests high`,
  and each touch gave `pad: edge, low`, `pad: edge, high` and `pad:
  touched` at the release, with no power cycle. So on this board the
  output rests high and a touch pulls it low: the sensor works and is
  active low, the opposite of what the option pins read as open would
  give, so the board ties AHLB high or the part differs. The earlier
  "touched" at boot was the old code taking a high rest for a touch,
  not a stuck detection. c367 reads the same at boot and is expected to
  behave the same.
- **3112.** `follows the pull` means nothing drives P0.11: the sensor
  is missing, unpowered or not connected, a fault of the board. `rests
  low` with no edge at a touch means the sensor runs but does not
  detect; the same power cycle is worth a try, and then the pad itself.
- **Any board.** `pad: touched` once per touch, at the release, and a
  finger left on the pad for more than 10 s gives `held 10 s, rests high
  now`, then at its release `held 10 s, rests low now` 10 s later.
