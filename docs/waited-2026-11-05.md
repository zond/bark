# A message that waited goes first once

**Date:** 2026-11-05. **Status:** measured, not adopted; roadmap D114.
Spec references are to `protocol.md`. Built on main `2a91b24`, whose
code main `e2e2761` changes only in comments; the fix is commit
`A message that waited for a ban goes first once` on the branch
`worktree-agent-waited`.

## What the flag did

The spec, "Overload waits": when bans end, the messages that waited for
them go first, the most travelled first; the rest of the queue keeps its
order, since ordering it all by count would starve messages that have
just entered.

In the core, `Outgoing::waited` (`tables/send_queue.rs`) was set by the
routing pass whenever an entry's next hop was banned
(`relay/forward.rs` `pick`) and never cleared, and `next_due` also
counted any entry whose own wait, `not_before`, had ever been set. So a
message that had once waited for any ban was ranked by hop count ahead
of every message that had not, for the rest of its stay: after its
offer, after a refusal sent it round to a free neighbour, and on a next
hop other than the one it waited for.

## The rule, as built

`waited` means: the entry waits, or has waited and not been offered
since, for the ban of the next hop it is routed to now. It is cleared
when its hand-over starts (`prepare`, `Routed::To`) and when it is
routed to another next hop (`pick`). Its own wait, `not_before` (a
sleepy node's or the application's message refused or retrying), is no
ban of a next hop and gives no place ahead: when it ends the message
takes its place in the queue's order, and is marked again if its next
hop is banned then.

Two tests: `relay/tests.rs`
`a_message_that_waited_goes_first_once`, in which a message that went
first at a ban's end, was refused and went round to a free neighbour no
longer goes ahead of one that arrived before it and waited for nothing
(main offers it first); and `send_queue.rs`
`its_own_wait_is_not_a_ban`.

## How it was measured

Release builds of `bark-sim`, main and the fix, the same seeds; the
scripts and columns of round five of `pushback-2026-11-03.md`. The
district at 32 and 64 times the rate, seeds 1–3, settled half hour,
mean (range), within 10 min; the city, `dense-power.toml` seed 1, at
four times for 14 400 s, the last hour, and at the design's rate, the
settled hour, within 20 min; seeds 1–10 of S4, the ring, the corridor,
the mesh burst and the spread mesh at the design's rate. For the
mechanism, scratch builds of both counted, at every choice of the next
hand-over (deliveries aside), whether it was other than the first
sendable entry in order, and how many sendable entries had `waited`
set (district seed 1, the whole run).

## What it shows

- **The flag was sticky as found**, and the fix makes it the spec's
  rule: under overload two in five to three in five sendable entries
  carried a mark, against one in four to two in five with it cleared at
  the offer, and main went out of the queue's order a third more often.
- **The sticky order was better under overload.** With the rule as
  written, at capacity and past it, every load measured delivers a
  smaller share of what was accepted (0.5 to 1.7 points), the median
  text is 1 to 16 s later, and each delivered text costs 2 to 6 % more
  air, with 5 to 10 % more refusals. The fix's one gain is the tail at
  32 times in the district (p90 20 s, p99 59 s shorter) and fewer texts
  held back there; at 64 times and in the city the tail is the same or
  longer. At the design's rate nothing moves.
- **Why.** The argument of "Why the most travelled first" is not
  limited to the end of a ban. The gate lets a higher count in whatever
  the wait on its way, and refuses a lower one once the way's wait
  passes the budget, so under load any offer of a less travelled message
  to a loaded way is likely refused, costs a DATA frame and a refusal,
  and bans the neighbour for the rest. The sticky mark, set on every
  message that has met a ban, sorts by count exactly the messages that
  travel loaded ways; the messages that never waited, the ones it would
  starve, mostly travel free ones and go in order. The extra refusals
  are the fix offering the lower count first.
- **No starvation showed in main.** Its p99 is longer than the fix's
  only in the district at 32 times, by 6 %; its held back share is
  higher there too (2.63 against 2.38 %). At 64 times and in the city
  main's tail is as short or shorter.

## What it does not show

The district is three seeds and the city one; the differences at 32
times are about the seeds' spread, those in the city and at 64 times
one way in every run. Whether ordering the whole queue by count, which
the spec rules out, does better or worse than the sticky mark was not
measured; nor was a mark kept for the life of the entry but cleared on
a change of next hop alone.

## Verdict

Not adopted: the fix is the rule as written, and the rule as written
measures worse than the code it was meant to correct. The fix stays on
the branch. If main's behaviour stands, the spec's rule should say what
it does: a message that has waited for a ban goes ahead of those that
have not, the most travelled first, for as long as it is queued; and
"Why the most travelled first" should say that the argument holds at
every offer to a loaded way, not only at a ban's end.
