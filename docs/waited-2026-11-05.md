# Which messages go ahead in the send queue

**Date:** 2026-11-05. **Status:** measured; the spec's rule rewritten to
say what main does; roadmap D114. Spec references are to `protocol.md`.
Built on main `2a91b24`, whose code main `e2e2761` changes only in
comments. Three rules were built on the branch `worktree-agent-waited`
and measured against main: the spec's rule as written (commit `A
message that waited for a ban goes first once`), and two research
builds, the bark-proto features `all-by-count` (commit `Research build
all-by-count: every due entry by hop count`) and `mark-until-reroute`
(commit `Research build mark-until-reroute: the offer keeps the mark`).
The branch ends with main's code for the order, its tests and the
spec's new text.

## What main does

The spec, "Overload waits", said: when bans end, the messages that
waited for them go first, the most travelled first; the rest of the
queue keeps its order, since ordering it all by count would starve
messages that have just entered.

In the core, `Outgoing::waited` (`tables/send_queue.rs`) is set by the
routing pass whenever an entry's next hop is banned
(`relay/forward.rs` `pick`) and never cleared, and `next_due` also
counts any entry whose own wait, `not_before`, has ever been set (the
node's own message, refused or with no neighbour to take it). So a
message that has once waited goes ahead of every message that has not,
the most travelled first, for the rest of its stay: after its offer,
after a refusal sent it round to a free neighbour, and on a next hop
other than the one it waited for.

## The rules measured

- **main**: as above.
- **fix**, the spec's rule as written: the mark means the entry waits,
  or has waited and not been offered since, for the ban of the next hop
  it is routed to now. It is cleared when its hand-over starts
  (`prepare`, `Routed::To`) and when it is routed to another next hop
  (`pick`); an own wait gives no place ahead.
- **all-by-count**: no mark; after a due delivery, every due entry goes
  by hop count, the most travelled first, the earliest of equals. The
  order the spec ruled out.
- **mark-until-reroute**: the mark is set when the entry's next hop is
  banned and cleared only when it is routed to another next hop, not at
  the offer; an own wait gives no place ahead. A refusal bans the next
  hop, and the routing pass marks the entry again under both, so it
  differs from the fix only for a message that is offered, not taken
  and not refused, and stays on the same next hop: a retry after
  silence or a busy channel.

## How it was measured

Release builds of `bark-sim`, the same seeds; the scripts and columns
of round five of `pushback-2026-11-03.md`. The district at 32 and 64
times the rate, seeds 1–3, settled half hour (900 to 1 800 s), mean
(range), within 10 min; the city, `dense-power.toml` seed 1, at four
times for 14 400 s, the last hour, and at the design's rate, the
settled hour, within 20 min; seeds 1–10 of S4, the ring, the corridor,
the mesh burst and the spread mesh at the design's rate. Main and the
fix in the city and the design-rate sets are the runs of the first
round; the district runs of all four were made again with scratch
instrumented builds, whose output matched the first round's runs byte
for byte.

New columns, for starvation:

- *latency p99.9 and max*: of texts delivered in the window. In the
  district the max is bounded by the run's half hour.
- *on its way at the end, older than 10 or 20 min*: texts accepted
  before the window's end, neither delivered nor ended by then, written
  more than 10 or 20 min before it, as a share of the texts accepted in
  the window.
- *longest stay in one queue*, *queue stays over 10 and 20 min*: from a
  scratch build, how long a forward stayed in one relay's send queue,
  from the first routing pass that saw it.
- *choices out of the queue's order*: at every choice of the next
  hand-over, deliveries aside, whether it was other than the first
  sendable entry in order; *sendable entries marked*: of the sendable
  entries at those choices, how many had the mark.

## What it shows

- **The fix**, the rule as written, is worse than main under overload:
  in every load measured it delivers a smaller share of what was
  accepted (0.5 to 1.7 points), the median text is 1 to 16 s later, each
  delivered text costs 2 to 6 % more air, with 5 to 10 % more
  refusals. Its one gain is the tail at 32 times in the district.
- **Mark-until-reroute** is the fix within the seeds' spread in the
  district, and so below main there in the same way; in the city at
  four times, one seed, it is the best of the four (p90 187 s, 95.6 %,
  4.39 s of air). Keeping the mark through the offer changes only
  the retries after silence or a busy channel; why that helps in the
  city and not in the district was not looked into.
- **All-by-count** is the clear best far past capacity: at 64 times it
  delivers 6 points more of the accepted than main, 5 more within
  10 min, with 16 % fewer refusals and 7 % less air per text, since
  there nearly every way is loaded (four in five texts are told to
  wait) and the gate's argument holds at every offer. Nearer capacity
  it starves what the spec said it would. At 32 times it delivers fewer
  texts per 10 min than main in every seed, its p90 and p99.9 are
  longer, and messages of count 1 to 4 stay over 10 min in one queue
  two and a half times as often as under main, over 20 min seven times
  as often; in the city at four times its p99 is 25 % longer (1 137
  against 913 s) and fewer are delivered within 20 min, while its median
  is the shortest. On free ways a higher count gains nothing from going
  first, since the gate takes any count there, and the lower counts it
  puts behind wait for nothing.
- **Main** is never the worst and rarely far from the best: its p99
  is within 7 % of the shortest in every load, the shortest in the
  city; it delivers the most of the accepted but for all-by-count at
  64 times and mark-until-reroute in the city; and no starvation showed
  in it. Its
  mark, kept while the message is queued, picks out the messages that
  have met loaded ways, where the gate refuses lower counts, and leaves
  the order of the rest; the fix and mark-until-reroute put 25 to 40 %
  of the sendable entries under the mark, main 46 to 62 %, and
  all-by-count every one.
- **The four lose about the same** of what was accepted: about 1 % at
  the hop cap in the city, within 0.1 point of each other.

## What it does not show

The district is three seeds and the city one; the city's ranking of
mark-until-reroute rests on that one seed. The stays in one queue were
counted in the district only. Whether a rule between main and
all-by-count, ordering by count every message whose next hop is loaded
now rather than every one that has waited, does better was not built.

## Verdict

The measurements support main's rule: a message that has waited, for a
ban or a wait of its own, goes ahead of those that have not, the most
travelled first, for as long as it is queued. Against the spec's goals:
the four lose about the same of what was accepted; main pushes back
to the origin with fewer refusals than the fix and mark-until-reroute;
all-by-count, which pushes back with the fewest and spends the least
air, starves the least travelled near capacity, as the spec said it
would; and main's tail is within 7 % of the shortest everywhere. The
branch keeps main's code, with tests that the mark outlasts the offer
and the move to another next hop, and the spec's "Send queue" and
"Overload waits" say main's rule and its reason. All-by-count far past
capacity is the one result that argues for more ordering by count; the
rule between it and main, under "What it does not show", is the thing
to build if that is wanted.
