# The next hop's margin estimate: counting what was sent, not only what got through

**Status:** research note, 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03. Roadmap D105.
Spec references are to `protocol.md` by section name. Follows
`margin-2026-11-02.md` (roadmap D103). Three candidates built and
measured in the simulator; the first two are adopted, the third is not.

## Summary

A relay judges whether a neighbour has margin from the average of what
it heard from it. That average is of the frames that got through, and in
the dense city it stood 5.4 dB above the link's mean level for the DATA
lost to noise (D103). Three candidates were built to make the estimate
an average of what was sent:

1. a heartbeat due on this channel and not heard is a reading at the
   noise floor;
2. a carried neighbour is a next hop only while its entry is fresh;
3. the ACK reports how well the DATA was heard, and an attempt left
   unanswered is a reading at the floor.

On the three-failure base (D102), candidate 2 halves the airtime per
delivered text in the dense city and ends the overload of the city at
twice the rate: 88.8 → 97.6 % delivered, 19.2 → 2.8 s of airtime per
text, relays ever at their cap 532 → 28. Most of that overload was
frames chasing walkers that had left. Candidate 1 on top of it takes
another 13 to 16 % of the airtime per delivered text off in the dense
places, with delivery flat; it leaves the ring and the mesh burst as
they were.
Candidate 3 on top of both is within one seed's noise of them. 1 and 2
are adopted; 3 is not, and its code is on a side branch (see "Where the
code is").

## The question

D103 found that the sender's estimate of its receiver, the averaged
RSSI of the frames it heard from it (spec "Neighbour table"), stood
5.9 dB above the level the DATA met, for the DATA between relays lost
to noise alone in the dense city; 5.4 dB of that was the average itself
above the link's mean level, and 0.5 dB the fade at the frame. The
average is of the heartbeats that got through: a neighbour at or below
sensitivity is heard on its good moments and averaged on those, a
walker is heard outdoors and nearer than it will be, and the next-hop
rule takes, among the faint neighbours, the one whose estimate is
luckiest. A higher threshold, and an order by expected progress, on
that estimate did not pay (D103, `margin-2026-11-02.md`); this note
does not repeat them. It asks what each of three ways of removing the
bias from the estimate itself buys.

## Candidate 1: a heartbeat due and not heard is a reading at the floor

**When a heartbeat is due here.** A relay's heartbeat steps one channel
a slot, every T of its own, whether or not the heartbeat went
(spec "Heartbeat"). A neighbour that heard one in a slot on its own
channel at t expects the next there at t + 13 T, and the table keeps
that slot (`last_beat`). A heartbeat is never early and at most
872.8 ms late behind its slot (`node.rs`, `heartbeat_lag`: a hand-over
attempt it may find under way and two backoffs). The one heard may
have been that late itself, so the next may start up to 872.8 ms before
the time reckoned from it, and up to 872.8 ms after; the two clocks may
part by 2 × 50 ppm of the rotation, 13 ms; and the heartbeat is 14.4 ms
on air. The window is 2 × 872.8 + 2 × 13 + 14.4 = 1.79 s. A heartbeat
of a burst, a relay's first rotation, is in no slot and says the slots
start anew; a heartbeat heard in a slot sets them afresh. A heartbeat
outside the slots (the one after a change of flags or a move, or an
answer to an anchorless node) is taken as a slot heartbeat, and costs
one window reckoned wrong.

**What the reading is.** A heartbeat sent and not heard had a packet
RSSI under the level at which half of all heartbeats arrive, 3.1 dB
below a full frame's (spec "Neighbour table"): 5.2 dB of signal below
the noise, which packet RSSI reads as 10 log10(1 + 10^−0.52) = 1.2 dB
above the floor. The floor itself, what the radio reads of an empty
channel, is the reading an absent frame gives; the two differ by 0.6 dB
in an average that counts each reading half, and the floor is taken. A
heartbeat the neighbour skipped for a busy channel (three checks busy,
one time in a thousand on a channel busy a tenth of the time) or
deferred for its budget is counted the same, wrongly, and the next
frame heard halves the error.

**When the radio could not have heard it.** The core knows when its
radio is away from listening: an excursion (a listen-before-talk, a
reading of another channel, a transmission) from the action to its
completion event, and a frame it received, from its start to its end
(`core-interface.md` "Radio", rules 1 to 3). A frame it did not decode
it does not know. The radio reads another channel once a second
(spec "Busy estimate") and sends its own heartbeats and ACKs, so a
window of 1.79 s with the radio listening throughout is rare, and a
rule that read only those windows would read few in the dense city,
where it matters. A heartbeat that came at some moment of the window
was lost to the radio's absence with the probability of the absence's
share of the window, and otherwise to the link; so the reading at the
floor enters with the weight of the share the radio listened. A
channel reading, 1.9 ms, costs a thousandth of the weight; a hand-over
attempt on another channel, about 145 ms, a twelfth; a full frame
received in the window, 139 ms, the same. The core notes how long its
radio has been away in all when a window opens and when it closes, and
wakes for both. The entry keeps the first, 4 bytes, and whether its
slot is known: 8 bytes with padding, 80 B per entry from 72
(`core-interface.md` "Memory"). A sleepy node, which hears only in its
windows, counts nothing.

**What it does to the 4.1 dB bar.** The bar covers a fade of 4 dB, the
standard deviation of the simulated fading over time and channel,
because a DATA goes out on the neighbour's channel while the neighbour
is heard on this one (spec "Neighbour table", D38). The survivor bias
was not in its derivation; it was a second error on top. With the
average of what was sent, the derivation stands as written. That the
bar is still right was measured, not derived again: with all three
candidates on the first base (see "How it was measured") and the bar
lowered to 0.4 dB, the fade taken as zero, the dense city delivered as
many texts, 97.6 %, at more than twice the airtime, 4.08 s a text
against 1.83 s, and the median text 7.0 s against 3.7 s; the walkers
2.74 s against 1.78 s; S4 at the design rate 1.19 s against 1.01 s.
The estimate then stood 0.9 dB above the truth for the frames lost to
noise, and a third of the DATA between relays were lost to noise: the
bias was gone and the fade was not. The bar stays.

**As built.** `tables/neighbours.rs`: `Neighbour::slotted` and
`away_base`; `beat_heard`, `burst_heard`, `slot_passed`,
`window_opened`, `missed`. `relay/heartbeat.rs`: `slot_window`,
`next_slot_edge`, `count_missed_heartbeats`, run before every event is
applied, so that a frame or an excursion ending now counts as away
until now. `relay.rs`: `Radio::away_since`, `away_total_us`,
`away_us`, `back`. Spec "Heartbeat" (a heartbeat due and not heard),
"Neighbour table" (`rssi_ema`, `last_beat`). Test: `relay/tests.rs`
`a_heartbeat_due_and_not_heard_is_a_reading_at_the_floor`.

## Candidate 2: a carried neighbour is a next hop only while its entry is fresh

**The age.** A carried neighbour's entry says where it was when it was
last heard, and it walks at 1.4 m/s (`node.rs`, `WALKING_SPEED_M_S`).
In its own heartbeat interval, at most 15 s, it walks 21 m, within the
50 m a position is good to (spec "Position", "Owner"). Its next
heartbeat then tells its place to another channel, and by the time one
comes back to this channel, a rotation later, it has walked 180 m on
average and 270 m at most: a hop's length in the dense city (255 m at
the median of the hops taken, D103) and most of the 300 m a link
reaches indoors (spec "Physical layer"); it may also have gone indoors,
14 dB down. So within its interval the entry is where it says, and
after it the entry is where it was. D103's frames by the entry's age
agree: the frames that reached nobody were 1 % under 15 s, 9 % at 15 to
60 s and 24 % at 60 to 130 s. Its next heartbeat heard, or its ACK to a
frame sent to it, makes it fresh again, so a walker in conversation
stays a next hop.

**As built.** A carried neighbour that is not sleepy and has not been
heard for its interval is a last resort, as a sleepy neighbour with an
anchor is (spec "Next hop"): a candidate only when no other is left and
none is only banned. `relay/forward.rs` `last_resort`, `tier`. Spec
"Next hop", "Neighbour table" (`carried`). Test: `routing.rs`
`a_carried_neighbour_is_a_next_hop_only_while_fresh`.

## Candidate 3: the ACK reports how well the DATA was heard

**What the receiver can report.** After a reception the SX1262 gives
the packet's RSSI, signal and noise together, in half-dB steps; its
SNR in quarter-dB steps; and the RSSI of the signal alone after
despreading (datasheet §13.5.3, GetPacketStatus). The firmware reads
the first two (`bark-fw/src/radio.rs`, `read_frame`). The sender's
rule compares a packet RSSI with a noise floor
(spec "Neighbour table"), and the receiver's floor is its own,
measured where it stands (spec "Busy estimate"). So the report is the margin at the receiver:
its packet RSSI less its floor, in whole dB. A raw RSSI would need the
receiver's floor beside it; the chip's SNR is on a different footing
from every other reading in the average and saturates a few dB above
the noise.

**Where the bits are.** A 19th byte in the 18-byte ACK would make it
168 bits, one more block of symbols: 16.96 → 18.24 ms (`radio.rs`,
`time_on_air`), 7.5 % of every ACK and one refusal fewer in the reply
reserve (spec "Duty cycle cap"). But a taken ACK's first byte has six
bits at zero, `aux`, which carries a refusal's wait and is 0 for a
take. As built, the top bit of that byte, until now the seen flag,
means "not taken": clear, `aux` is the reading, 0 to 63 dB above the
receiver's floor; set, `aux` is a refusal's wait, or 0 for seen
(spec "Packet formats", "The ACK"). No byte, no airtime, one ACK
length; a change to format 1, which nothing in the field speaks yet.

**How it entered the estimate.** As one more reading in the same
average, counting half like any other: a frame heard here at its packet
RSSI; a report as the floor here plus the margin reported there; a
silence as the floor here. A report is of the frame a DATA is, on the
channel it took, in the direction it went, against the floor it met.
The sender listens for the ACK after each attempt (spec "Hand-over"),
so it knows which went unanswered; an unanswered attempt to a neighbour
that listens all the time was a reading at the floor. A silence is the
link's fault about three times in five in the dense city (of the DATA
between relays that had a link, 34 % were lost to noise alone and 23 %
otherwise, D103; 16 % of ACKs are lost); the answered attempts pull
the average back. A refusal or a seen answer carried no reading.

**As built, and removed.** `wire.rs`: the ACK's first byte
`0 1 h h h h h h` taken, `1 1 w w w w w w` not taken; `Ack::heard`. `relay/forward.rs` `data`: the reply's reading is the
DATA's packet RSSI less the floor, clamped to 0..63; `forward_timers`:
an attempt that the ACK timeout ends unanswered enters a reading at the
floor. `relay.rs` `received`: a taking ACK addressed to this node
enters the floor plus `heard`. `tables/neighbours.rs` `reported`,
`unanswered`. Tests: `wire::tests::ack_round_trips`, `routing.rs`
`an_unanswered_attempt_is_a_reading_at_the_floor_and_an_ack_reports_the_link`.

## How it was measured

Two rounds, release builds of `bark-sim`, settled hour.

**The first round** measured each candidate alone, 3 with 2, and all
three, against main as it was before D102 (`af695ca`), on
`dense-power.toml` seeds 1, 2 and 7, the same city at twice the rate
(`dp-x2.toml`) seed 1, `s4-capacity.toml` seed 1, `ring.toml`,
`dense-movers.toml` and `mesh-burst.toml` seed 1, and seed 1 of the
dense city with the simulator's margin log (`--margin`) for the bias.
It found candidate 3 alone harmful under load (below) and was used for
the bias table and the bar check.

**The final round** measured the combinations that matter on the
three-failure base, main with D102 (`f94a7cd`): 2 alone, 1 and 2, and
all three, against that base, on the dense city seeds 1 and 2, the city
at twice the rate seed 1, the walkers, S4 at the design rate, the ring
and the mesh burst. The tables below are this round's; the figures are
those of the JSON summaries and the message logs, settled hour.

## What the measurements show

- **Candidate 2** removes the frames sent to walkers who had left. In
  the dense city a quarter of the DATA between relays reached nobody on
  main (D103); with 2 the walker that is stale is passed over for a
  fixed neighbour. In the city at twice the rate this was the overload:
  2.6 million failed hand-overs, 16.5 hops a text, 532 relays at their
  cap; with 2 the city delivers 97.6 % at a seventh of the airtime per
  text. Its cost is more texts lost at the hop cap (209 → 280 in the
  dense city), the search walking round a hole it no longer crosses
  through a walker, and the slowest tenth a few seconds later in the
  dense city; delivery is within 0.1 point.
- **Candidate 1** on top of 2 takes another 16 % of the airtime per
  delivered text off the dense city, 16 % at twice the rate and 13 % on
  the walkers, with a quarter fewer failed hand-overs and delivery
  within 0.1 point. In S4 it moves airtime 2 % and delivery 0.2 point,
  within one seed's noise. It leaves the ring and the burst to the
  byte: their links are strong enough that a reading at the floor,
  counting at most half, leaves them margin.
- **Candidate 3** on top of 1 and 2 moves the airtime per delivered
  text by at most 1 % and delivery by at most 0.3 point, either way, in
  the dense city, the walkers and S4: within noise. It costs a change to
  the ACK's meaning, and on the burst, where a silence is a collision,
  it adds half again to the failed hand-overs and 4 % to the airtime.

Candidate 3 takes half the bias away, 5.4 → 2.5 dB: the answered
attempts report the link in the direction a DATA goes, and the
unanswered ones pull the average down. Candidate 1 takes little,
5.4 → 5.0 dB: a heartbeat missed for a link at the floor is rare
against one missed for the radio being away or a collision, and is
weighted down by the share the radio was away; its gain is on walkers
gone, whose estimates fall to the floor, more than on faint links held.
Candidate 2 takes a third, not by a better estimate but by choosing
fewer walkers, whose estimates were the worst. The two adopted rules
were not measured together for the bias; each alone leaves 3.7 to
5.0 dB of it. So the bias stays, as a known property of the estimate;
what it costs is smaller, since the frames sent to walkers who have
left are gone. With all three, what is left, 2.3 dB, is half bias and
half the fade at the frame.

## What it does not show

- The air. The simulator's links are reciprocal and every node's floor
  is −117 dBm, so a report in an ACK says what a heartbeat would have
  said but for the fade on the other channel and the survivor bias. On
  the air the floors differ by board, channel and place: two boards by
  5 dB on one desk, one board by 10 dB between channels
  (`bench-2026-10-30.md`). There the report is the only reading the
  sender can have of the margin at the receiver, and candidate 3's gain
  here is a lower bound. The 4 dB fade, its 60 s correlation and its
  independence across channels are the simulator's (D38).
- Collisions the radio does not decode. In candidate 1 a heartbeat lost
  to a frame the relay did not decode counts as the link's; the core
  cannot know of such a frame.
- The scenario. `dense-power.toml` keeps every walker a relay through
  its walk under the hour's grace, and 85 % of the DATA between relays
  have a walker at one end (D103); candidate 2 acts on that, and a city
  where carried nodes are sleepy would show less of it.
- A resting relay's search for a move. A relay on battery past its
  grace searches with its GPS when the heartbeats of its fixed
  neighbours differ from their averages by more than fading explains
  (spec "Position", roadmap D82). Candidate 1 pulls a faint
  neighbour's average down between the heartbeats heard, so the
  differences grow and the test may search more often. No measured
  scenario has a resting relay (0 searches an hour in every run); the
  outage scenario, `grid-down.toml`, would show it.
- Seeds. The final round has two seeds of the dense city and one of
  each other scenario; the bias is measured on one seed.

## Verdict

- **Candidate 2, adopted.** The largest gain of the study, at no cost
  in delivery: the city's overload was mostly frames chasing walkers
  who had left.
- **Candidate 1, adopted.** On top of 2 it cuts another sixth of the
  airtime per delivered text in the dense places with delivery flat,
  and it never fires where links are strong.
- **Candidate 3, not adopted as built.** On top of 1 and 2 its gain is
  within noise, and alone it was harmful under load, through the
  unanswered attempts counted at the floor. A report-only variant, the
  ACK's reading entering the average with no reading for a silence, is
  worth measuring later, on the air above all, where the receivers'
  floors differ.
- **The survivor bias** remains, 3.7 to 5.0 dB in the dense city with
  either adopted rule alone. Rules that read the biased estimate differently, a
  higher threshold and an order by expected progress, were measured
  and not adopted (D103, `margin-2026-11-02.md`).

## Where the code is

Candidates 1 and 2 are on main: spec "Heartbeat", "Neighbour table" and
"Next hop", `core-interface.md` "Memory". Candidate 3's code stays
reachable on the branch `worktree-agent-estimate-c3-code` at `8abb562`,
which holds all three candidates on the three-failure base, the build
the final round measured as "1+2+3"; candidate 3 alone is its commit
`186696d`.
