# The retry window grows with the busy estimate

**Date:** 2026-11-02. **Status:** measured; roadmap D100, not adopted;
the code removed after the decision, as measured and rejected code is,
and kept at commit `f75da94` on the branch `worktree-agent-ac9782f965af870d2-code`
so that it can be rebuilt. Spec references are to `protocol.md`. Built
on main `8ff3a74`, where D93 is adopted, and measured against it; all
release builds, the same scenario files and seeds. The build with the
rule reproduces nothing of main to the byte, since every retry draws a
different wait; main's own runs are reused where another measurement of
the same day made them on the same base, after this note's build of that
base reproduced one of theirs to the byte on the ring.

## Summary

- **The rule.** The wait before the retry after the k-th unanswered
  transmission is uniform in [0, 2^k × 155.5 ms × (1 + G)], with G the
  contenders the receiver's channel holds as the sender's busy table has
  it, G = −ln(1 − b) at busy fraction b: the frames in flight per frame
  time on a channel whose frames start at random. At b = 0 it is today's
  window; at 0.6 it is 1.9 times it; what the busy fraction adds stops
  at the shortest ban, 1.58 s. A node that does not sweep, and a
  delivery, draw today's window. Nothing changes for the first attempt
  or the ACK.
- **Measured: neutral.** The dense city at the design rate delivers
  97.8 / 98.1 / 98.1 % of the settled hour on main `8ff3a74` and
  98.0 / 98.3 / 98.1 % with the rule, seeds 1, 2 and 7, with 1 to 3 %
  less airtime and 2 to 9 % fewer hand-overs that failed with the
  receiver holding the message. At twice the rate 88.8 / 89.1 →
  88.7 / 88.8 %; S4 at three times the rate 63.6 / 61.1 →
  65.1 / 60.2 %, the median latency 83 / 94 → 87 / 96 s; S4 at the design
  rate 93.6 → 93.5 %; the walkers 96.5 → 96.4 %; the
  ring 92.6 → 94.1 % with the median 41.5 → 48.9 s; the mesh burst loses
  two texts of a hundred. Every difference is within what the seeds
  spread.
- **Why nothing moves.** The windows drawn are today's: in the dense
  city 99.5 % of the retries drew a window within a tenth of the idle
  one, at twice the rate 91 %, at three times the S4 rate 95 %. The
  busy estimate behind them was under 10 % for 99 % of the retries at
  the design rate and for 79 to 88 % under load, and never over 43 % at
  any relay on any channel. The estimate is a 30-minute average taken at
  the sender; the collisions that lose ACKs are seconds long and hidden
  from it by definition. And where load is high the bucket binds before
  the channel fills, as spec "Duty cycle cap" says it does: at twice the
  rate 1.9 million hand-overs waited for the bucket and 800 relays
  reached their cap while the channels read 19 % busy.
- **Recommendation: not adopted.** The rule is sound and bounded but has
  nothing to act on in this design: the sender's busy estimate is too
  slow and too low to see the contention that loses ACKs, and the
  regimes where channels would fill are the bucket's first. The spec is
  left as it is. A retry window that answers contention needs a signal
  on the hand-over's own time scale, the receiver's busy reading in its
  ACK or heartbeat, or the sender's listen-before-talk results over the
  last seconds; the doubling per attempt is such a signal already.

## The question

A hand-over's attempts are spaced by the retry wait: uniform in
[0, 2^k × 155.5 ms] after k unanswered transmissions, the unit a full
frame and its ACK (spec "Retry wait", roadmap D22). The window was
derived for one case, two senders that cannot hear each other and
collided at one receiver: a window of two units parts them with
probability 1/4, one of four with 9/16. It does not look at the channel:
on a channel found busy 60 % of the time a sender draws the same wait as
on an empty one. In the dense city the storm note found four hand-overs
in ten failing at 5 % busy already, and the retries of every sender
whose frame or ACK was lost landing on a channel that the retries of the
others fill (`storm-2026-10-30.md`, `storm-ack-2026-10-31.md`).

Every node keeps a busy estimate per channel, the fraction of the sweep's
readings that found the channel busy, used to choose a home and to move
(spec "Busy estimate"). The question of this note is whether the retry
window should be scaled by the busy estimate of the channel the
hand-over goes on, by what rule, and what it does to the dense city and
to the loaded S4.

## The rule

The wait before the retry after the k-th unanswered transmission is
uniform in [0, W], W = 2^k × unit × (1 + G), with G the contenders the
receiver's channel holds as the sender's busy table has it: G = −ln(1 − b)
at busy fraction b. The unit is as before, a full frame and its ACK,
155.5 ms, or 389.8 ms with the long preamble. What the busy fraction
adds is bounded by the shortest ban, 1.58 s; the idle channel's window
is never shortened. The busy estimate is of the channel the frame is
addressed to, the receiver's home as the sender's neighbour table has it
at the attempt. A node that does not sweep draws the idle channel's
window, and so does a delivery to a sleepy node. Nothing changes for the
first attempt, which waits only for the channel, or for the ACK, which
goes at once (roadmap D93).

As built, the rule was one function of the unit, the attempts and the
busy fraction beside the retry wait's constants in the core's tables,
the contenders G in the busy table's fixed point (−ln(1 − b) by an
integer log2, exact to 1e-4), the busy fraction read from the frame's
channel where the relay draws the wait, a trace of each window drawn
with the busy estimate behind it, and the simulator's count of those by
half-units and by tenths. Unit tests held the idle window to today's,
the window at 60 % busy to the derived 596 and 1192 ms, and the bounds.
The firmware built unchanged. That code was removed with the decision;
it is at `f75da94` on `worktree-agent-ac9782f965af870d2-code`.

## The derivation

**Stability of random access.** On a channel where n senders contend,
each retrying after a collision with probability p per frame time, the
expected number of retries per frame time is np. Throughput is highest
and the backlog bounded when np is about one: each contender retries
about once in n frame times. Retrying more often, the retries collide
with each other and breed more retries; less often, the channel idles
while senders wait. This is the argument behind every stabilised
random-access scheme: the retry rate must fall as the contenders grow.
A uniform wait in [0, W] frame times retries at a rate of about 1/W per
frame time over the window, so W should be about n.

**Contenders from the busy fraction.** A sender does not count its
contenders; it has the busy fraction b of the channel, the share of the
sweep's readings that found energy on it. On a channel whose frames
start at random, Poisson with G frames per frame time, the channel is
idle when no frame started in the last frame time, a fraction e^−G of
the time, so b = 1 − e^−G and G = −ln(1 − b): the expected number of
frames in flight per frame time, with the frame time the unit. A busy
fraction of 0.2 holds 0.22 contenders, 0.6 holds 0.92, 0.8 holds 1.61
and 0.9 holds 2.30. Carrier sense keeps apart the frames of senders that
hear each other, so a channel with it holds fewer contenders than the
Poisson relation says for the same b; the estimate errs towards more
contenders, so the window errs long, the stable side. The estimate counts
foreign transmitters, which contend as peers do.

**The window.** The sender retrying is one more contender than the
channel holds, so W = 2^k × unit × (1 + G). At b = 0 this is today's
window: the doubling stays, since it is what parts the correlated
senders, the two that collided and retry together, which the busy
fraction says nothing about. The two factors do different work: the
doubling counts the senders the failure itself reveals, the busy factor
the ones the channel hides.

**The bound.** A sender refused by a relay comes back after the shortest
ban, 1.58 s, the time the refuser's bucket takes to refill a full frame
and its ACK (spec "Bans"); a sender unanswered has no reason to wait
longer than one told to wait, and a node at its cap sends a full frame
only every 1.58 s in any case (spec "Duty cycle cap"). So what the busy
fraction adds stops at 1.58 s: with a window of four units the busy
factor is bounded at 2.54, reached at b = 0.79; with two units at 5.08,
at b = 0.98. The idle window is never shortened, with the long
preamble's unit either, whose four units are 1.56 s. With the bound a
hand-over's three attempts and two waits take at most
3 × 738.6 ms + 2 × 1.58 s = 5.38 s, under 1 % of the 600 s time to live
(spec section "Time to live" (before D113)); a message with the worst wait at every one of its
hops would still have time for 111 of them.

**What keeps the idle figures.** Three figures of the design are derived
from a hand-over's retry waits: a sleepy node's listening window after
its beat, 3.39 s, and after a message, 3.15 s (spec "Delivery"), and
the reply reserve, the 13 refusals the bucket refills while a sender
retries (spec "Duty cycle cap"). They keep the idle channel's waits. A
delivery draws the idle window whatever the channel, since a longer wait
would push its retries past the window its node listens for. The
refusals a relay owes a sender retrying are at most two, one per retry,
whatever the waits between them, so the reserve's refusals are not fewer
for the longer waits; what changes is that a sender refused and one
unanswered on a busy channel come back on the same time scale.

**Stale and missing estimates.** A relay sweeps every channel once in
13 s, so its estimate of any channel is at most 13 s old and averages
30 minutes; a hand-over's retries use what the table has at the retry.
A sleepy node does not sweep after its boot (spec "Busy estimate"), so
its estimates stop at its first rotation and go stale; rather than draw
from a measurement that may be hours old, a node that does not sweep
draws the idle channel's window. A channel with no reading yet has an
estimate of 0, the idle window too.

## What it shows

**Delivery is the same everywhere.** The dense city at the design rate
gains 0.2 points on two seeds and none on the third; at twice the rate it
loses 0.1 and 0.3; S4 at three times the rate gains 1.5 on one
seed and loses 0.9 on the other; S4 at the design rate loses 0.1; the
walkers lose 0.1, with 2 % less airtime and 4 % fewer splits. Main's own seeds of the dense city spread 97.8 to
98.1 %, and the two S4 seeds 61.1 to 63.6 %, so every difference is
within the spread.

**The windows are today's.** The count of the windows drawn says why: 99.5 % of
the dense city's 1.1 million retries drew a window within a tenth of
the idle one, 311 or 622 ms, and 0.1 % reached the bound; at twice the
rate 91 % and 0.0 %; at three times the S4 rate 95 % and 1 %. The busy
estimate behind the retries was under 10 % for 98.6 % of them in the
dense city, where a busy fraction of 10 % lengthens the window by a
tenth, and for 79 % at twice the rate; it was 30 % or more, which
doubles the window at least, for 0.1 % and 1.1 %. The relays' estimates
themselves, read at the end of the run on every channel, average 5 % in
the dense city (the 99th percentile 10 %, the largest 14 %) and 19 % at
twice the rate (the 99th percentile 38 %, the largest 42 %), the same on
main and the branch.

**What did move, moved the right way, a little.** In the dense city
airtime fell 1.4, 3.2 and 0.7 %, hand-overs that failed fell 2.2, 5.5
and 1.5 %, and of those the ones whose receiver held the message, the
splits, fell 2.2, 9.3 and 0.7 %; hand-overs held back by the bucket fell
16, 19 and 10 %. The frame fates on seed 1 are unchanged to a tenth of a
point: 43.7 → 43.9 % of DATA received, 8.8 → 8.7 % lost while the
receiver received another frame, ACKs received 84.0 → 83.7 %. The 1 % of
retries that drew a longer window did not change what the channel does.

**What it costs.** Latency where the bucket binds: the S4 median at
three times the rate 83.0 → 87.3 and 94.0 → 96.0 s, the ring's 41.5 →
48.9 s (its p90 159 → 140 s), the dense city at twice the rate 46.5 →
48.7 s. The longer waits are paid by the messages that wait, and buy
nothing where the channel was not the constraint.

**The dense city at twice the rate does not stabilise, and was not
unstable.** Seed 1 delivers 88.8 % on main and 88.7 % with the rule, with
the same airtime, 1 118 451 against 1 118 743 s, the same 2.8 million
failed hand-overs and the same 560 000 splits; seed 7 89.1 → 88.8 % at
1 % more airtime and 2 % more splits. It is not a storm: 1.9
million hand-overs waited for the bucket, 800 relays reached their
cap, and the channels read 19 % busy. The limit there is
the duty cycle, as spec "Duty cycle cap" says: a busy relay runs out of
budget before its channel fills.

## What it revealed

- **The busy estimate cannot see contention.** It is the fraction of
  the sweep's readings that found energy, one reading of each channel
  every 13 s, averaged over 30 minutes (spec "Busy estimate"), made for
  the move rule, which wants a slow, steady figure. The collisions that
  lose a frame or an ACK last a frame time and come in bursts of
  seconds; a 30-minute average of a 5 % channel reads 5 % through them.
  And it is the sender's reading: the hidden terminal is the sender the
  first cannot hear, so what collides at the receiver is, by definition,
  what the sender's estimate leaves out. A contention signal for the
  retry window must be fast and from the receiver's side: the receiver's
  own busy reading carried in its ACK or heartbeat, or, at the sender,
  the share of its listen-before-talk checks that found the channel busy
  over the last few seconds, which it has for nothing. The doubling per
  attempt is already a receiver-side signal: each unanswered attempt is
  one more vote that the receiver's channel is contended.
- **The design is bucket-bound where it is loaded.** At the design rate
  the channels sit at 5 % and the losses are the links': 43 % of the
  DATA addressed to relays are lost while the receiver listens and is
  free, 9 % to another frame. At twice and three times the rate the
  bucket binds first: 1.9 million hand-overs held back at twice the dense
  rate, 700 000 at three times the S4 rate, with the channels at 19 % and
  6 %. The spec sized it so (spec "Duty cycle cap": 10 % of airtime per
  relay is about 180 B/s, and a busy relay runs out of budget before its
  channel fills), and the measurement bears it out. A rule for channel
  contention has no regime to act in until the cap is lifted, by more
  relays or less airtime per text.
- **−ln(1 − b) was never exercised.** With the estimates where they are,
  the factor 1 + G stayed within 1.1 for 98 % of retries; the choice of
  relation between busy fraction and contenders, Poisson or linear,
  made no difference here, and its bound was reached by 0.1 % of
  retries. The one place the bound was met on purpose is the long
  preamble: a hand-over to an anchorless node draws four units of
  389.8 ms, 1.56 s, and any busy fraction over 1.4 % puts it at the
  1.58 s bound, so the rule barely lengthens the long preamble's waits
  at all. That is right, since its unit already holds the receiver that
  long, but it is a corner the derivation did not see.
- **The ring is the one gain**, 92.6 → 94.1 % with the p90 159 → 140 s,
  at a median 7 s longer. Its relays run at their cap (the largest
  settled airtime 9.9 %) and 2 269 texts waited at their origin; 4 % of
  its retries saw a busy estimate over 10 %, more than any other run.
  One seed of 3 000 texts is not a result; it is the direction the rule
  should work in where the channel is the constraint.

## Recommendation

Not adopted. The rule is derived, bounded and cheap, and it does no harm
at the design rate, but the measurement says it has nothing to act on:
99 % of the dense city's retries draw today's window because the busy
estimate behind them is under 10 %, and where the load is high enough
to fill a channel the bucket binds first and the rule only lengthens
the waits of messages the bucket already holds, 2 to 4 s on the S4
median. Adopting it would add a logarithm and a rule to the core for a
change within the seeds' spread.

What to do instead, if contention is to be answered at the retry:

1. Take the signal from the receiver's side and the last seconds, not
   the sender's 30-minute average: the receiver's busy reading in its
   heartbeat (one byte), or the share of the sender's listen-before-talk
   checks that found the channel busy over the last hand-over or two.
2. Measure in a scenario where the channel, not the bucket, binds:
   fewer relays per km² at the same offered load, or the cap lifted for
   the measurement, so that the channels pass 30 % busy.

Until then the retry wait stays as D22 left it, and the doubling per
attempt remains the only contention signal, which this measurement
found sufficient for the loads the design carries.
