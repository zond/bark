# A node that moves: carried

**Date:** 2026-10-21, roadmap D70. **Status:** measured; decided
2026-10-21 (roadmap D70): adopted, fixed again after four hours; the
boards' GPS wander indoors is a Phase 2 bench item (spec O16).
Spec references are to `protocol.md`. The base is main `c4b83cd`, run as its own binary on the
same scenario files.

The question, from the user's ruling on D68: a relay that moves strands
its neighbours' routes to it and its sleepy nodes' mail. A node that moves
is to route, take each GPS fix and hold no mail, and say so with a
heartbeat flag that tells others what it is, not what it wants from them.
Becoming carried is to need strong evidence, and being fixed again even
more, so that a node that stops for a while does not take mail it must
then deliver all at once with the long preamble; and at its first far fix
a node is to flush what it holds, since once it has left, its sleepy nodes
cannot hear it.

## Summary

- **Carried relays fix what D68 found.** On `dense-movers.toml`, 5317
  relays of which 1004 walk, seeds 1 / 2, delivered of the texts sent in
  the settled hour: all 91.46 / 91.94 % on main against 96.87 / 97.03 %;
  to the walking relays 58.5 / 57.1 % against 99.35 / 99.55 %; airtime
  per delivered text 6.89 / 7.23 s against 3.15 / 3.07 s. D68's figures,
  on main before its candidate for the near relay, were 91.12, 58.1 and
  7.2 s.
- **Texts to the walkers' sleepy nodes** go 60.7 / 62.1 % on main and
  91.6 / 90.5 % with the rule, but they are not the same texts: with the
  rule a sleepy node does not anchor to a walker, so only 680 / 691 texts
  in the hour go to one whose anchor walks, against 1280 / 1254, and
  those are to nodes that anchored before their anchor first walked.
- **The flush does little, because there is little to flush.** At a
  first far fix after the settle time a walking relay held 21 / 16 texts
  in all over the hour: 2 / 1 went by the flush, 1 / 0 at a later beat,
  18 / 14 never reached their node. Most were already waiting for nodes
  that could not be reached. Its airtime is 23 frames, 9.7 s, an hour over
  the domain.
- **Mail to a node heard as a relay** is the rule's other gain: 170 / 192
  texts an hour go to their node directly (201 / 204 frames, 13.8 / 14.1 s
  of airtime), mostly to walkers whose texts reached a stale owner.
- **The run of far fixes is 72 s** and takes 77 s at the median (p90
  82 s, at most 101 s) at the walkers' one fix a heartbeat. No still relay
  turned carried without a GPS error, as none can: the simulator gives
  still relays no fixes, and exact ones (below).
- **With a wandering GPS** (an assumed 15 m on each axis, correlated over
  300 s, a fix a second for every relay) 193 / 194 of the 4313 still
  relays turned carried, 4.5 %, most in the first half hour. Their texts
  still arrive (98.9 / 96.2 %), since they route; their sleepy nodes'
  texts sent while they were carried arrive 0 of 11 and 2 of 18 times,
  since the nodes left for other anchors and the senders kept the old
  address. All delivered moves by less than 0.1 point: 96.86 / 96.96 %
  against 96.87 / 97.03 %. The flush then costs 199 / 229 frames, 84 /
  97 s an hour over the domain, and 883 / 935 sleepy nodes leave an
  anchor for the flag against 542 / 478.
- **1 h or 4 h** cannot be told apart in the settled hour of
  `dense-movers.toml`: within 0.1 point everywhere, since the walkers
  stop for an hour on average and the run ends first. Over four settled hours (seed 1)
  they part: at 1 h 1158 turns of a walker to fixed, and of the texts
  held when such a walker walked on, 30 of 36 never reached their node,
  about eight an hour; at 4 h 12 turns and none. All delivered is 97.90 %
  either way, airtime per text 2.96 against 3.00 s, flush frames 69
  against 45. A still relay carried by mistake stays carried longer at
  4 h, and costs no more: texts to its sleepy nodes while it was carried
  2 of 46 at 1 h, 5 of 38 at 4 h, since the nodes anchor elsewhere at
  once. **4 h is recommended**, and is the default on the branch. Main
  gets worse with time instead: 90.47 % over the four hours, 35.1 % to
  the walkers, 11.4 s a text.
- **S4 repeats main to the byte. `dense.toml` does not:** a holder that
  hears the relay it holds texts for now hands them over, 6 texts in the
  settled hour (9 frames, 0.62 s), and the run diverges from there, all
  delivered 97.43 → 97.31 %, to still relays 99.52 → 99.74 %. With that
  hand-over switched off the branch repeats main to the byte on `dense.toml`
  too.

The driver's checks were the D63 way to keep a relay still: a relay's fix
farther than 50 m from its first was never handed over. Now the core
decides, so the driver hands every fix a walker at the carried speed
could reach, and so the same core can run on the board, where the fix
input is the same command (the firmware has no GPS driver yet).

Found and fixed on the way, before any run counted: a sleepy node whose
anchor turned carried while it listened to choose its anchor, with no
other relay, left its choice due and woke for it again and again at the
same instant (`an_anchor_carried_while_its_node_chooses`); and a holder
heard a relay it was not keeping in a full table and sent to its own
channel instead of the relay's, so the hand-over now keeps the channel
the heartbeat named.

"To their sleepy nodes" are texts to a sleepy node whose anchor, when the
text was sent, was a walking relay. "Carried by mistake" counts texts sent

## Why

A relay's position is two things to others: where its neighbours route
to it, and the address of its sleepy nodes and of its own texts. Kept at
its first fix (D63) both are wrong once it walks; taken from each fix,
routes follow it but the addresses go stale. Carried, it routes where it
is, which is what neighbours need, and it holds nothing that relies on
staying, so there is nothing for an address to go stale on: its sleepy
nodes are anchored elsewhere, and its own texts reach it at its position
now, which the simulator's senders know. The walking relays' texts go
from 58 to 99 %, and the search for them, which on main spent most of
the airtime, ends: half the airtime per text.

The flush matters less than expected because a walker holds little when
it leaves. It was a relay among people at home and anchored few sleepy
nodes, and the texts it held were mostly for nodes it could not reach
then either: dark, or heard below sensitivity. The flush sends each once
and they stay. It is cheap at the true rate, 10 s of airtime an hour
over the domain.

The run of far fixes and the way back trade two costs. A relay that
turns carried by mistake gives up its sleepy nodes, which re-anchor: what
was sent to their old address, the carried relay's position, finds no
owner (0 of 11, 2 of 18). A walker that turns fixed again too early takes
new sleepy nodes and texts, and strands them when it walks on. In the
settled hour neither weighs: 2 texts were held at a walker's far fix
after it had turned fixed again, 11 to 18 texts went to the sleepy nodes
of a still relay carried by mistake. Over four hours the walkers' stops,
an hour on average, often outlast one hour: at 1 h 1158 turns to fixed,
each a walker that takes new sleepy nodes and texts and then walks off,
and 30 of the 36 texts it held then never arrived. At 4 h a stop rarely
lasts so long. The other side is a relay carried by mistake, which at
4 h stays carried until four hours of fixes within 50 m of one place
pass; its sleepy nodes have left for other anchors by then, at the cost
of the few texts already sent to the old address (5 of 38 arrived at
4 h, 2 of 46 at 1 h), and its own texts arrive (99.9 %), since it
routes. So the longer wait loses nothing measured and the shorter one
loses texts.

How fair the error model is: the simulator gives exact fixes, and none
to a relay that does not walk, so the scenario as committed cannot show a
still relay turning carried. The error is a model with assumed numbers.
15 m on each axis is a radial error of 21 m RMS, 37 m at the 95th
percentile, from a windowsill with half the sky; the L76K gives 2 m CEP
under open sky (its specification), and nobody has measured the boards
indoors. A fix lies beyond 50 m 0.4 % of the time, and correlated over
300 s such a fix stays out long enough for the 72 s run. So 4.5 % of
still relays turned carried in the run, nearly all in the first half
hour, where every relay starts with its first fix exact. It is a stress
test of the rule, not an estimate of how often it will happen.

## What this does not show

- The senders here address a moving relay at its current position, read
  from its core when the text is sent. A real sender has the position the
  app last told it, so the walking relays' 99 % is an optimistic bound:
  the app layer must tell senders the new address, and how often it does
  sets the real figure.
- Two seeds for each comparison, one for the long runs. The differences
  that decide, 58 → 99 % and 6.9 → 3.1 s, are far beyond the spread.
- Walkers only, at 1.4 m/s, with a fix at each heartbeat; nobody drives.
  A relay in a car passes the 72 s run in a few hundred metres and is
  carried as a walker is.
- A spoofed fix that drifts slower than a node can move makes a relay
  carried within minutes (the D63 test of the simulator now shows it): it
  then loses its sleepy nodes and is routed to the spoofed place, where
  D63 kept it out. Not measured on the D63 scenarios.
- The GPS error model is assumed (above); the real error at a windowsill
  is a Phase 2 measurement.
- A sleepy node never sets the flag in this core, since the sleepy
  setting does not change at runtime; the runtime switch of a node's role
  when it is unplugged is still to come (D68).

## Recommendation

1. **Adopt the carried flag and its rules** (spec proposal, D70): the
   walkers' texts go from 58 to 99 % and the domain from 91.5 to 96.9 %,
   at half the airtime per text, and nothing measured gets worse.
2. **Keep the run at 72 s** of far fixes, from the walking pace and the
   L76K's rate. With the assumed error it lets 4.5 % of still relays turn
   carried at least once in the first two hours; whether that is real
   depends on the boards' indoor error, to be measured in Phase 2 before
   it is lengthened.
3. **The way back: four hours** (the default on the branch), as the
   user asked for a strong bias: one hour lets walkers that stop for
   an hour turn fixed, take mail and strand it, about eight texts an
   hour here, while four costs nothing measured.
4. **Keep the flush**, cheap and bounded, though it saved few texts here.
5. **Keep the hand-over to a node heard as a relay**: 170 to 220 texts an
   hour reach their node by it.
