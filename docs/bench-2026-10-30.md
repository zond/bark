# First board on the bench

**Date:** 2026-10-30. **Status:** measurements, one board, no decision.
The boards arrived a week early; this is the first night with one of
them on USB, the measurement console (`bark-fw --features console`)
and then the node firmware, driven from the host without hands on the
board. Spec references are to `protocol.md`; the bench list is
`roadmap.md` Phase 2 and `bark-fw/scripts/measure.md`.

## The board

Serial 133C25F161D73112, stock antenna, on a desk beside the laptop,
indoors.

| | |
|---|---|
| bootloader | UF2 Bootloader 0.6.1-2-g1224915, SoftDevice S140 6.1.1, Board-ID nRF52840-TEcho-v1 (`INFO_UF2.TXT`) |
| application start | vector table at `0x26000`, stack at the top of RAM; the factory image occupied `0x26000..0x89000` (its `CURRENT.UF2`) |
| factory firmware | LILYGO's test build, not Meshtastic: it reports an L76K, an SX1262, a PCF8563, a 2 MB external SPI flash (JEDEC 0xBA6015), and no BME280 |
| entering the bootloader | a double tap within half a second of the top-left button gives the `TECHOBOOT` drive; opening the factory firmware's serial port at 1200 baud enters the bootloader's serial update mode instead, which has no drive |
| our firmware's USB identity | 1209:0001 "bark T-Echo" |

So D6 and D79's layout hold: the loader at `0x26000` is where the
bootloader jumps, and both image slots lie where the factory image lay.

The 2 MB external flash was not in the pin research. It is a candidate
home for the staged image and the mesh update's coded pieces, which D79
put in internal flash. Not pursued here.

| battery pin on USB, `status` | 4805 to 4790 mV | unreliable on USB | the charger's voltage, as `board.rs` warns |

Retune is seven times the spec's figure and the sweep reads nothing
useful: the busy estimate's sweep (spec "Busy estimate") needs a dwell
of at least 150 µs a channel after a 680 µs retune, about 11 ms for 13
channels, and the RSSI it reads at that dwell is 10 dB below the
settled reading. Either the sweep dwells a millisecond a channel, 24 ms
in all, or the threshold is set against readings taken the same way.

The noise floor is the open question. −79 dBm in 125 kHz is 40 dB above
kTB. The receiver's RSSI may be offset, the desk may be that noisy at
433 MHz (the laptop, its charger, the USB hub), or the LNA setting
(`rx_boost: false`) may read differently from the datasheet's
sensitivity figures. A second board transmitting through attenuators
settles it (bench list, "Noise floor and RSSI calibration").

## The loader, on the board

`scripts/image.py stage` pushed three images through the console, each
swapped in by the loader at the reboot that followed, each running
afterwards:

| Image | Version | Size | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| console, 2 ms oscillator wait | 1 | 106 072 B | runs; CAD 4.64 ms |
| console, 1 ms oscillator wait | 2 | 106 080 B | runs; CAD 3.65 ms; uptime reset confirms the reboot |
| node firmware | 3 | 179 360 B | runs; see below |

A staging over USB takes about a minute. The first attempt failed on the
host side: a second reader on the serial port took the console's
answers, and the script timed out before sending a chunk. One reader
per port.

Whether each image confirmed itself (D79: the loader swaps back at the
next boot if not) could not be read from the log, because the boot
lines are lost (below). The node image ran on past the 600 s watchdog,
to 660 s and counting, so it did confirm; the console images were
replaced before their watchdogs ran out.

## The node firmware, first minutes

The node booted, ran its first rotation's burst (D73) and settled to
listening. In 660 s the driver handled 17 603 events, its longest
handling 976 µs and its longest time away from the radio 245 µs,
against D37's 3.6 ms bound; ten sends were held by a busy channel. No
frame was heard: there is no second board.

Five of the burst's thirteen heartbeats were skipped (`HeartbeatSkipped`
on channels 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11): their listen-before-talk found the
channel busy at every attempt. CAD on channel 5 gave no false alarm in
20 tries from the console, so either those channels carry real signals
at the desk (channel 6 is 433.92 MHz, the ISM band's centre; the noise
readings were highest on 8 to 12) or CAD right after a transmission on
the neighbouring channel behaves differently from CAD from rest. Two
boards and a quiet place decide it.

## Found on the way

- **The boot log is lost.** `radio up in N us`, `node id=…`,
  `update: version N confirmed` and `console ready` are printed before
  the host opens the port and never reach it; the README says the log
  shows them once opened. Either the firmware keeps its first lines
  until a host connects, or the README says what happens. The boot time
  and the confirmation line are what we want from them.
- **The first command after a reconnection is garbled** (`unknown
  command`): bytes left from before, or the host's line settings at
  open. Harmless on the bench.
- **README sizes are stale**: the node image is 179 360 B after D81 and
  D82, the README says 171 644.
- **Entering the bootloader from the factory firmware** by the 1200-baud
  touch gives the serial update mode, not the drive; only the double
  tap does. Worth a line in the README's flashing steps.

## The night alone, and two boards the next morning

The node image ran alone on the desk for 10.5 hours. It moved its home
channel 47 times, every 10.6 to 15.5 minutes, the fastest the move rule
allows (spec "Channel moves": 10 minutes of hysteresis and a wait of 0
to 5), each move a write of the stored state. The cause is the RSSI
settling above: the driver reads another channel 300 µs after the
retune, −91 dBm, and the home channel settled, −79; the noise floor,
the 10th percentile of all readings, lands at −92, the busy threshold at
−86, and home is busy at every sample while no other channel ever is.

The second board, flashed the same way (bootloader 0.6.1, board ID
nRF52840-TEcho-v1), gave a transmitter. Both on the desk 20 cm apart,
the beacon a 255-byte frame every 1.4 s (the console holds the 10 %
duty cycle), read by the first board; 120 to 300 readings a dwell.

**RSSI against dwell, no signal**, four runs alike, 1 to 2 dB spread
at each step:

| Dwell after the receiver starts | Reads |
|---|---|
| under 150 µs | −128, nothing |
| 150 to 400 µs | −90 |
| 400 to 700 µs | −83 |
| 700 µs and later | −80, the settled home channel's −79 within 1 dB |

The steps are one symbol period apart, 256 µs at SF5.

**RSSI against dwell, the beacon on**, settled level −28 dBm at −9 dBm
out, −16 at +10 (the receiver is near its top):

| Dwell | Reads |
|---|---|
| 400 to 640 µs | −55, the same for a true level of −15, −20 or −28 |
| 700 to 900 µs | −38 to −63, scattered |
| 1 ms and later | the true level, within 3 dB |

A short dwell does not report a level; it reports a fixed value for
anything strong. A reading is good from four symbol periods after the
receiver starts, and the settled home reading equals it, so home needs
no restart. One correct reading of another channel is a retune (668 µs),
four symbols (1.02 ms), the read (155 µs) and the retune back: 2.5 ms.

**CAD under the beacon**: busy 6 to 10 % of attempts on the beacon's
channel, the beacon's own duty, so CAD catches data symbols too. On the
channels one, two and three away it reported busy 8 to 15 % of attempts,
as often. The beacon read −66 dBm one channel away at −9 dBm out, −46
three away, −35 three away at +10: at 20 cm the receiver's front end is
overloaded and the neighbouring channels see the blocker, not a clean
leak, so no weak signal can be made on the desk by turning the power
down. The adjacent-channel figures of the bench list need distance or
attenuators.

**The two boards' settled floors** on the same desk, same minute: −81
and −86 dBm.

**The oscillator wait** of 1 ms on the second board: 40 CADs (3.9 ms
each) and 10 transmits without an error, as on the first.

**The boot log was not lost this time**: after a staged image's
reboot the host saw `image version 2`, `radio up in 19454 us` and
`update: version 2 confirmed`. The loss above may be the USB
enumeration after a cold plug, not the firmware.

**What the retune is made of, an experiment.** A console image built
with the SPI bus driven blocking inside the asynchronous device, so a
command is a few microseconds of busy-wait instead of one DMA interrupt
and executor wake per bus operation, staged on the first board:

| | Interrupt-driven bus | Blocking bus |
|---|---|---|
| RSSI read, one 3-byte command | 155 µs | 49 µs |
| retune, any hop | 668 µs | 461 µs |
| transmit prepare, 9 to 255 bytes | 1.49 to 1.73 ms | 0.91 to 1.15 ms |
| transmit excess | 510 µs | 356 µs |
| return to listen after transmit or CAD | 1.30 ms | 0.84 ms |
| CAD prepare, at the 5 ms oscillator wait | 0.75 ms | 0.49 ms |
| sweep of 13 channels, no dwell | 11.0 ms | 7.2 ms |

About 35 µs of every bus operation was the interrupt path. The 461 µs
that remain in a retune are five commands (standby, frequency, receive,
symbol timeout, receiver gain) with the chip's busy line after each,
and the chip's own transitions; the last two commands repeat settings
that do not change between retunes. The readings were the same as
before. Not committed: a change for a decision of its own.

**A listen-before-talk with fewer commands, an experiment.** The same
image, with the oscillator wait at 2 ms, sending to the chip only what
changes: a CAD is standby, frequency, interrupt mask, CAD parameters
and the CAD; the receive after it is the interrupt mask and SetRx,
which starts the oscillator itself from the standby a CAD ends in; the
retune is standby, frequency and SetRx. Modulation, packet parameters,
the symbol timeout and the receiver gain, which the driver rewrote at
every step, are set once. The RSSI was also read during the CAD, as
the chip does not forbid it. Medians on the first board, the second
beaconing 255-byte frames every 1.4 s on the channel at −9 dBm for the
busy rows:

| | Every command, blocking bus | Only what changes |
|---|---|---|
| retune | 467 µs | 355 µs |
| CAD prepare | 501 µs | 464 µs |
| CAD, from SetCAD to its interrupt read | 4.65 ms, to standby with the oscillator restarted | 2.02 or 2.28 ms, the chip left in standby |
| back to receiving on the same channel | 846 µs, from the restarted oscillator | 2.25 ms, the oscillator's 2 ms start inside |
| RSSI after the CAD | a second excursion, 2.06 ms | four symbols and a read, 1.12 ms, no retune |
| retune home | | 336 µs |
| the whole, CAD and RSSI, home to home | 8.06 ms | 6.19 ms |
| CAD busy of readings overlapping a beacon | 22 of 22 | 20 of 20 |
| RSSI read 1.2 ms into the CAD, quiet channel | | −77 to −78 dBm, the settled reading |
| RSSI read 1.2 ms into the CAD, during the beacon | | −28 to −32 dBm (−35 settled in receive) |
| RSSI read 0.6 ms into the CAD, quiet | | −81 to −83 dBm, still climbing |
| RSSI read 2.0 ms into the CAD | | −128 dBm: the CAD had ended |

The reading during the CAD is right from four symbol periods in, as in
receive. With it, a listen-before-talk is the CAD's preparation and
sampling, 2.5 ms, and the oscillator's restart is paid once by whatever
follows: a transmit from the standby the CAD ends in, or the return to
listening. Of the 6.19 ms, 2 ms is that restart, 2 ms the sampling,
1.1 ms the dwell the reading inside the CAD makes unnecessary, and
1.1 ms commands. Not committed: a change for a decision of its own.

**The oscillator's start, probed.** The same image, with a probe: standby
with the oscillator off for a chosen time, the delay programmed anew,
the error word cleared, a receive, which starts the oscillator, and the
error word read. First board, room temperature, 10 to 15 probes a cell:

| delay | off under 1 ms | off 1 ms to 3 s |
|---|---|---|
| 500 µs and above | no error | no error, 95 probes |
| 200 µs | no error | about half flag an error |
| 100 and 50 µs | no error | about half flag an error |
| 0 | the chip hangs, busy line high until a reset | |

A probe costs the delay plus 0.44 ms. Restarted within a millisecond of
stopping, the oscillator is still running and no delay is too short;
after a millisecond off it is a cold start, so the restart after a CAD,
a few milliseconds in standby, is one. The flag is not a clean
threshold: between 50 and 200 µs about half the probes fail whatever
the off time. With the flag set the chip carries on: eight transmissions
of eight after a second off at a 50 µs delay were received by the third
board with the CRC right, at −28 to −34 dBm where the 2 ms delay gave
−26, so a start too short costs a few dB at the start of the frame, not
the frame. So this board's oscillator starts in 200 to 500 µs at room
temperature, and the 2 ms of roadmap D87 holds a factor four. Not
committed.

## What this does not show

Two boards 20 cm apart: no range, no sensitivity, no adjacent-channel
rejection, since the receiver is overloaded at that distance; no weak
signal, since −9 dBm out is still 50 dB above the floor there. One
place, one night: the noise floor may be the desk's. The oscillator's start was probed on one board at room temperature; a
cold board is not measured. Of the 355 µs retune with three commands, how much is the chip's
transitions is not measured. A transmit from the standby a CAD ends in
is not timed.
