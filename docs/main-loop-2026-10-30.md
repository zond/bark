# The core's main loop: what it is and what it should be

**Date:** 2026-10-30. **Status:** agreed 2026-10-30; done 2026-10-31
("Done" and "Done, step two", at the end). The storm note (`storm-2026-10-30.md`) is the reason:
its candidate rules do not fit the loop as it stands.

## The question

The core is sans-IO: `handle` takes one event, fires what is due, and
starts radio jobs until the radio is busy. That shape is right. What
grew around it is the problem. The node has about fifty fields, twelve
of them bare timers; one hand-over object serves the whole node; one
owed reply is all a node can hold; and the storm's candidate, a wait
that is never idle, a take final only with its ACK out, cannot be
written into it without another layer of special cases. Can the loop be
restructured without changing what the node does, and would the
candidate then fit?

## In pictures

**The firmware around the core.** One executor, five futures joined;
the driver's loop is the only thing that touches the core, in one
`step` at a time: wait for the earliest of the core's next wake-up, the
radio's interrupt or the app, deliver one event, carry out the actions.

**The core's loop today.** One pass per event. The radio slot holds
one excursion and its owner; four owners, each with its own step
machine; one hand-over object, one owed reply, and four side slots a
packet can be in.

```
  handle(event)
    |
    |-- apply ---------------------------------------------------.
    |   Received -> classify (new / held / handed / done) -> ACK, |
    |               take, merge or ignore; records, neighbours    |
    |   LbtDone / RssiSample / TxDone -> radio.complete() -> job  |

          || start_handover || sweep_due && start_sweep           |
                                                                  |
  radio: Radio<Job>  [ one excursion, owner: Sweep|Heartbeat|    |
                       HandOver|Reply ]                           |
  Transfer (one):   Idle -> Wait -> Lbt -> Tx -> AwaitAck -> ...  |
  Replies (one):    Idle -> Lbt -> Tx ; dropped at the deadline   |
  Heartbeat:        its own Check steps                           |
  Sweep:            its own channel step                          |
  side slots:       held | make_way | hold-for-reply | defer      |
  queue:            places, one marked current                    |
```

**The core's loop after.** The same pass; the hand-over state lives in
its place, one excursion machine serves every owner, timers live in
one struct, and the pump is a scheduler over ready jobs with the
in-flight bound as a rule, set to one.

```
  handle(event)
    |
    |-- apply: as today; classification unchanged
    |         LbtDone / TxDone -> excursion.complete() -> owner
    |
    |-- timers.fire(now)          one struct, one "what is due",
    |                             one "when next" -> the wake-up
    |
    `-- schedule(now):
          ready jobs, by priority:
            1 owed reply        (replies: short queue)
            2 heartbeat
            3 a hand-over       (any place whose wait has ended,
                                 up to IN_FLIGHT = 1)
            4 sweep reading
          start the first the radio can take

  excursion: Excursion { owner, step: Lbt(ch) -> Tx(frame) -> done }
             one machine; the owner's continuation after it
  places:    Outgoing { frame, kind, state: Idle | Waiting(why, until)
                        | Lbt | Tx | AwaitAck(until), neighbour,
                        attempts (count transmissions only), expect }
             held / deferred / waiting-for-reply are states of a place
  replies:   bounded queue of Owed; dropping at a deadline is a rule
  mode:      Relay{power} | Sleepy{anchor..}  instead of nine flags
  bound:     IN_FLIGHT = 1 reproduces today byte for byte;
             raising it is the candidate's fourth part
```

## The loop as it is

**One pass.** `handle` applies the event: a frame goes through `received`
and one classification against the records (`forward.rs` `classify`,
cases new, held, handed, done); a radio completion goes to whichever job
owns the radio; an app command to its handler. Then `timers` fires what
is due, and `pump` starts jobs while the radio is idle, in the order
reply, heartbeat, hand-over, sweep.

**The radio** is one slot, `Radio<Job>`, holding at most one outstanding
excursion and the job that owns it, one of `Sweep`, `Heartbeat`,
`HandOver`, `Reply`. Every excursion is a CAD, an RSSI reading or a
transmission; D88 adds one listen-before-talk excursion for the pair.

**The hand-over** is one object on the node, `Transfer`: a step (idle,
wait, CAD, RSSI, transmit, await ACK), the attempt count, when its
listen-before-talk began, the ACK it expects. The send queue marks one
place as current; `prepare` picks the next due place, routes it and
starts the `Transfer` on it. Because there is one `Transfer`, a relay
runs one hand-over at a time, and every wait inside it, the busy backoff
and the retry wait of up to 622 ms, idles the radio: 15 % of the hour
for the relays at their cap in the storm.

**The reply** is one owed ACK, `Replies`: the reply, its channel, a
deadline and a ready time, with its own step machine. A second owed
reply is refused, and an owed reply whose backoff would pass its deadline
is forgotten while the packet stays taken: the storm's 245 277 takes
without an ACK.

**The side slots** are the special cases the one-at-a-time rule forced:
`held`, one packet set aside for the budget; `make_way`, which cancels a
waiting hand-over so a delivery can go first; a hold of the transmit
when a reply is due; `defer_handover` for the budget. Each is a place a
packet can be that is not the queue.

**The timers** are twelve `Option<Instant>` fields (boot, rescan,
choosing, feed, window, app, sniff, watch, retry, promised, delivery
retry, the mover's), scanned by hand in `timers` and again in
`radio_due` for the wake-up.

**What is sound and stays:** the event and action interface
(`core-interface.md`), the tables (neighbours, records, busy, budget,
heard, delivery queue, send queue), the wire format, the pure routing
functions, the classification of a received frame, the priority order.

## The loop as it should be

**Four steps, no exceptions.**

1. **Apply the event** to the one piece of state it concerns, as now.
2. **Advance the clock.** Every timer belongs to the state it times, in
   one `Timers` struct with named deadlines; one function fires what is
   due and one answers when the next thing is due, and the wake-up is
   derived from that answer and nowhere else.
3. **Schedule.** Collect the jobs that are ready, each with its priority
   and channel, and start the first the radio can take. The order stays:
   an owed reply, the heartbeat, a hand-over, the sweep.
4. **Emit**, with the wake-up from step 2.

**The hand-over lives in its place.** Each send-queue place carries its
own hand-over state: the neighbour, the attempt count, the ACK it
expects, and what it waits for and until. The radio slot stays single,
the radio does one thing at a time, but records which place it serves.
"An attempt is a transmission" then falls out: the count advances when
the radio reports the frame sent, never on a busy channel. The ACK wait
is exclusive, the radio listens at home for it; a retry wait or a busy
backoff is only a state of a place, and another place may be served
meanwhile. The side slots become states of a place: held for the budget,
waiting for a reply to go first, deferred until the budget allows. There
is no place a packet can be except the queue.

**Replies are a short queue** of owed ACKs, bounded, served first.
Whether an owed ACK may be dropped at a deadline is a rule, not a
consequence of having one slot.

**The number of hand-overs in flight is a rule.** The restructure keeps
it at one, so the node does exactly what it does today. Lifting it is
the storm candidate's fourth part, a decision measured on its own.

## How it is done without changing anything

Each step is one commit, and after each the simulator's output on a
fixed seed is byte-identical to main's: the messages CSV and the summary
line, wall time aside, on `s4-capacity.toml` seeds 1 at the design rate
and at three times it, `dense-power.toml` seeds 1 and 7, `ring.toml`
seed 1 and `dense-movers.toml` seed 1. A run is deterministic by seed,
so this is a check, not a hope. The steps, in order: the timers; the
replies as a queue of one; the hand-over state into the places, still
one in flight; the scheduler. The tests that drive `Transfer` and
`Replies` directly are rewritten to the places; the timing tests keep
their bounds. The firmware builds at every step, since the driver's
interface does not move.

## Further unifications, proposed with it

**One excursion machine for the four jobs.** The heartbeat, the
hand-over, the reply and the sweep each carry a hand-written step
machine (CAD, RSSI, transmit; or the reading alone), with its own check
and done functions. With D88's single listen-before-talk excursion they
are all one shape: a check on a channel, clear or busy or given up,
then a transmission, then the owner's continuation (the heartbeat's
next channel, the hand-over's ACK wait, the reply's deadline, the
sweep's sample). One `Excursion` type with the owner as data replaces
three step machines, and the scheduler starts and completes one kind of
thing.

**A mode instead of flags.** The node carries `sleepy`, `leaving`,
`low`, `battery_sleepy`, `dark`, `renew`, `unplugged`, `sniffing`,
`anchor` and `stage` side by side, and most of their combinations
cannot occur. One `Mode` enum, a relay on external power, a relay on
battery in its grace, a sleepy node with or without an anchor, with the
data each mode needs inside its variant, makes the impossible states
unrepresentable and puts the sleepy node's watch, beat and sniff logic
behind one variant instead of across the whole file. The heartbeat
flags on the air are derived from the mode, not stored beside it.

**The relay module split by concern.** `relay.rs` is 3 800 lines with
its tests: the loop, the boot, the anchor and the sleepy side, the
sweep and the move rule, the heartbeat. Forwarding, delivery, motion,
power and GPS already live in their own files; the rest follows them,
and `relay.rs` keeps only the loop.

**Memory, measured before anything moves.** The node is about 170 KB on
a chip with 256 KB. The send queue is 82 KB of it: 39 regular places
and 254 hop places (one per hop count, D29 to D36), each a 280-byte
`Outgoing` holding a full 260-byte frame whether occupied or not; the
records are 63 KB, 1 571 at 40 bytes. The hop places are capacity the
design chose, so their frames cannot be shared away, but they can be
stored as frames only, in one fixed store the places index, with each
place a few bytes of state. That is a separate step from the loop, and
it waits for the firmware's stack headroom to be measured on a board
under load, which the bench list already asks for.

**Smaller things**, each cheap, none required: the `H` and `Q` size
parameters of `Node` could be plain constants, since one firmware and
one simulator share them, which takes a generic off every impl; and the
records' lookup is a scan of 1 571 entries per received frame, about
ten microseconds on the chip, fine today and worth an index only if the
records grow.

**Order.** The timers, then the mode, then the excursion machine, then
the replies, the places, the scheduler, and the file split last, each a
commit with the byte-identity check. The memory step is its own
decision after the board measurement.

## What it does not do

It changes no rule. The storm's candidate comes after, one part at a
time on the storming seed: absorb copies, which needs only the records'
`handed` set and touches no structure; a take final only when its ACK
has gone out, which is a rule on the reply queue; the attempt rule,
which the places make the only possible reading; and a wait never idle,
which is the in-flight bound raised above one. Each is a decision with
its own row.

## Risks

The relay module is 3 800 lines with its tests, and the tests assert
on the `Transfer` steps. The diff is large even though the behaviour
is not. The guard is the byte-identity check after every commit; a
step that changes any output is a bug found, not a step finished.

## Done

**Date:** 2026-10-31. Seven commits on the branch, in the order above,
each checked byte-identical to main (`e75bd97`) on the six reference
runs: the messages CSV, the summary without its wall-time line, and the
JSON summary.

1. `e3cf86a`: the timers in one `Timers` struct, fired by `Node::fire`, asked
   by `Node::due_at`, from which `next_wakeup` is derived.
2. `6e01d95`: `Mode`: a relay on external power, on battery since an
   instant or leaving since one; a sleepy node with its anchor, its
   battery flag, its anchor's misses, its dark check and where it chose
   from.
3. `b8568f5`: `mac::Excursion`: one check-then-transmit or one reading,
   with the job that owns it; the heartbeat's, the hand-over's and the
   reply's step machines gone.
4. `c31d49a`: `Replies`: a queue of `REPLIES` places, one; the drop at the
   deadline and the giving way of an unsent reply are rules of the queue.
5. `898c6d6`: the hand-over in its place: `Outgoing::state` (`Queued`,
   `Held`, `Waiting(why)`, `Radio`, `AwaitAck`), `tries` the attempt
   count, advanced only when the radio reports the frame sent, the ACK
   expected read off the frame; `transfer::Flight` keeps what the one
   hand-over in flight (`IN_FLIGHT`, one) needs that a place has no room
   for: until when it waits and since when its checks found the channel
   busy. The side slot for the application's message is a `Held` place.
6. `0a05c3f`: the scheduler: `JOBS` in priority order, `Node::start_job` and
   `Node::ready_at` per job, `Node::schedule` the loop.
7. `e913b75`: `relay.rs` split: `relay/boot.rs`, `relay/anchor.rs`,
   `relay/sweep.rs`, `relay/heartbeat.rs`, the tests in
   `relay/tests.rs`; `relay.rs` keeps the node and the loop.

**Size.** The `Relay` was 170 792 B before and is 170 464 B after
(328 B less): the side slot's full frame gone, against the
flight and the replies' length. `Outgoing` stays 280 B: the state fits
its one spare byte. The size tests' bounds are untouched.

**What it revealed.**

- A place has one spare byte in its 280. The hand-over's two instants
  (until when it waits, since when its checks found the channel busy)
  do not fit, so they live beside the place in `Flight`, one per
  hand-over in flight. Raising `IN_FLIGHT` means a flight per hand-over
  in flight, a small table, not a wider place.
- The old `Transfer` started every hand-over from the place's kept
  `tries`, and a forward's were always zero because every path that
  ended a forward's hand-over without removing it left `tries` alone.
  With the count live in the place, a forward is reset to zero where it
  is addressed afresh (`route`), which the module documentation already
  said. The routing tests caught the one place it was missed.
- `move_at` never needed its "while sweeping" guard: it is set only
  while the node sweeps and cleared when it stops.
- The renew flag (a sleepy node listens on after the reply that took a
  DATA for it) belonged to the replies, not the node.
- The simulator's wake-up is sensitive to the exact instant, even one
  in the past: a different value, however harmless, reorders the event
  heap. Every component of `next_wakeup` had to be kept to the
  microsecond, which the check enforces.

## Done, step two

**Date:** 2026-10-31. The node's fields grouped and the held place
counted: five commits, each built from its own tree and checked as
before against main (`9f1719d`) on the six reference runs.

1. `eca1925`: the battery (low, the readings' scale, the charge) one
   `Battery` in `relay/power.rs`; the application's message and its
   refusal count one `OwnMessage` in `relay/forward.rs`.
2. `29e9e5e`: what forwarding keeps beside the send queue, the
   hand-over in flight, the neighbour slots the current forward passed
   over and whether the send queue may have room for the delivery
   queue, one `Forwarding` in `relay/forward.rs`.
3. `7c3080e`: the radio's side, the excursion outstanding, the replies
   owed, the sweep, the heartbeat, the busy estimate and the airtime
   budget, one `Radio` in `relay.rs`, built by `Radio::new` from
   `Sweep::new` and `Beacon::new`, which replace the two copies of each
   literal; the node's fields ordered by part, what an event reads
   first, the tables after, as the layout comment says.
4. `8efc095`: the send-queue place in `Held` counted in
   `Load::send_queue`.
5. This commit: the notes.

The node has 21 fields where it had 31: the stage, the mode, the
battery, the configuration, the timers, the radio's side, the
forwarding's side, the application's message, the motion, the GPS, the
stored state, the ID, the home, the plane, the random source, and the
six tables. The stored state stays a field of its own: a part of one
field would be a name for nothing.

**Size.** `Relay` 170 464 B before, 170 480 B after; `SleepyNode`
151 448 B before, 151 464 B after: 16 B more each, the parts' own tail
padding (`Radio` holds 514 B in 520, `Forwarding` 33 in 40, `Battery`
13 in 16, `OwnMessage` 9 in 12), bytes that packed against neighbouring
fields in the node's C layout before. The bounds are untouched.

**The load.** Only the simulator's reporting reads `Node::load`, at its
two call sites in `bark-sim/src/sim.rs`: the per-minute series
(`--series`: its `sq`, and `send_queue_max` and `send_queue_sum` in its
`m.load`) and the per-node `LoadStats` that no summary prints; no
protocol rule reads it. Counting the held place changes those two where
a relay holds one, which it does only while its delivery queue is full
as it originates a message for a node it holds for. The six reference
runs' CSV, summary and JSON are byte-identical, and so is the series
on ring, on s4-capacity at the design rate and at three times it, and
on dense-power seed 7: no sampled relay held a place.

**What it revealed.**

- The script that builds a commit's simulator from its tree (the
  commit's archive extracted, then a build in a shared target
  directory) gave the extracted files the commit's time, older than
  the previous build's output, so cargo rebuilt nothing: every binary
  after the first in a batch was the first's. In the first step's
  batch, `c2` to `c6` were `c1`'s build, so its six-run checks of
  commits 2 to 6 were run on commit 1's code; the quick three at each
  step, built from the working tree, and the tip's six runs, built
  after its commit, were what they claimed. The script now touches the
  files it extracts, and the binaries of this step differ as they
  should.
- `Load::hop_places` and `Load::waiting` counted the held place all
  along; only `send_queue` left it out.
- The battery's reading, `battery`, would have shared its name with
  the new field: it is `battery_read`.
