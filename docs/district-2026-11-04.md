# The district: the city near and above its capacity in minutes

**Status:** research note, 2026-11-04. Adds `scenarios/district.toml`.
Spec references are to `protocol.md` by section name.

## Summary

The city of `dense-power.toml` (5 317 relays, 14 952 sleepy nodes, 28 km²)
takes 20 minutes to simulate its 6 300 s at the rate of
spec "Capacity and sizing", and two hours at eight times that rate. The district is a tenth
of it: 2.8 km², about 520 relays and 1 500 sleepy nodes at the same
densities, everything else unchanged, every node switched on at once, half
an hour. A run takes 17 s at the city's rate, 42 s at sixteen times, and
3 minutes at sixty-four times, in 0.4 GB.

Per relay, at the same rate, the district delivers what the city does,
since it has the city's people per relay. Everything that grows with a
text's path does not carry over: texts go to anyone in the patch, so a
path is a third of the city's (870 m against 2 750 m), latency and airtime
per text are lower, and the capacity per relay is 4.6 times the city's.
The district reaches its capacity at about 30 times the spec's rate where
the city reaches it between 4 and 8 times. Above capacity it fails the
same way the city does: the backlog grows without end and texts expire in
the network instead of being refused at their origin.

## The scenario

`scenarios/district.toml` is `dense-power.toml` with the square's side
divided by √10, 5 291.5 m to 1 673.3 m, the boot windows set to 0, and a
1 800 s run; a test checks that nothing else differs. The densities give
5 rooftop relays, 22 others and 2 000 people's nodes, of which a quarter
are relays at boot (the seed draws which; 452 to 511 in seeds 1 to 3).

The settled window is 900 to 1 800 s: the city's settle time, since a
relay's first channel moves come 795 s after it is switched on (spec
"Channel moves"). At the district's capacity the time from acceptance to
the destination or the node holding a text for it is 94 s at the 90th
percentile, so the backlog has levelled well before 900 s; the backlog
series below are flat in the window up to the capacity.

**Rate.** As for the city, a run at k times the rate is a copy of the
file with `per_leaf_per_hour` multiplied by k and the name set to
`district-xk`:

```
k=16; sed -e "s/^name = \"district\"/name = \"district-x$k\"/" \
  -e "s/^per_leaf_per_hour = 0.9583333/per_leaf_per_hour = $(echo "0.9583333*$k" | bc -l)/" \
  scenarios/district.toml > /tmp/district-x$k.toml
bark-sim /tmp/district-x$k.toml --seed 1 --json x$k.json --messages x$k.msgs.csv
```

**The border.** The patch has a border on four sides. So has the city: it
is a 28 km² square, not an endless one. Destinations are uniform within
the square in both, so no search needs to leave it; the border's effect is
the shorter paths, which are taken as they are rather than hidden by a
margin of relays that originate nothing. Such a margin would make the
patch's relays carry traffic they do not originate and would not lengthen
its paths.

Refusals at the origin are under 0.4 % of texts written in every row,
but the city's first hops tell more origins to wait, 13 % of texts at ×4
against 0.3 % in the district (see "Saturation" for the measure). The
rest of the accepted texts are still on their way at the end.

- **Delivered per relay** agrees within the seeds' spread: it is set by the
  people per relay, which the district keeps.
- **Losses** are HopCap in both, a search that ran out of its 255 hops
  (spec "Hop count"), in the city two times in three towards a carried
  node, one that moves with its person; the district loses a half to a
  third of what the city does, as its searches are shorter.
- **Transit backlog, latency, airtime per text** are a third to a fifth
  of the city's, and the gap widens with the rate: the city's relays carry
  each text three times as far, and its transit backlog per relay starts
  to rise at ×4 where the district's is still linear.

So the district at the city's rate is a lightly loaded city. The figures
that predict the city's are those set by the people per relay; the figures
set by load per relay are reached at a higher rate.

"Told to wait" counts a text once for each wait, so it can pass 100 %.

- **Capacity.** The district delivers at most about 8 500 texts per
  10 minutes, 16 per relay, at ×32; at ×64 it delivers less, 7 000. The
  city delivers 19 100 at ×8, 3.6 per relay.
- **×8 and ×16 are below the district's capacity**: nothing is held
  back, the backlog is level, the only loss is HopCap. With the capacity
  per relay 4.6 times the city's, they load the district as ×2 and ×3.5
  load the city.
- **Above capacity**, at ×64 on main, the backlog grows through the
  window and 7.7 % of accepted texts expire in the network: their time
  left ran out on the way (spec section "Time to live" (before D113)). That is a loss of a text
  the network had accepted, the loss the refusal at the origin should
  prevent. The city at ×8 shows the same, at 10.5 %, so the district
  reproduces the city's failure in 3 minutes instead of 2 hours.
- **The depth 8 candidate** at ×64 loses no text to expiry in the half
  hour, delivers 10 % more than main with the same seed, and refuses a few texts as Banned; its
  backlog still grows, from 4 900 to 6 700 over the window, so the half
  hour does not show whether it levels or expires later.

## How to read a run at saturation

Look at four things, in the settled window:

1. delivered per 10 minutes: flat with the rate once the capacity is
   reached; falling above it means the network spends airtime on texts it
   will not deliver;
2. the transit backlog: level means the origins refuse what the network
   cannot carry; growing means texts are accepted faster than they leave;
3. held back and told to wait: the refusals the people see; they should
   take up all the rate above the capacity;
4. losses by cause, as shares of accepted texts. HopCap is a search that
   ran out of hops, Unplugged a text held by a node that turned sleepy,
   ReturnFailed a hand-over back to the parent that failed; these are
   small and do not grow with the rate.
   Expired, Budget and QueueExpired that grow with the rate are texts the
   network accepted and could not carry.

A run's summary table prints the same causes; `--messages` writes one row
per text with its fate, cause and times, from which all the figures above
are computed.

## What the district cannot show

- **The city's numbers above its rate.** Paths are a third as long, so
  the load per relay at a given rate is lower and the capacity per relay
  4.6 times higher. A rate in the district is not a rate in the city; the
  mechanisms near and above capacity are the same, the rates are not.
- **Long paths.** Anything that grows with hops, such as searches that
  wander far, return paths, and how long a text's time to live lasts on a
  long path, is weaker in the district.
- **A spread boot.** Everything switches on at once; the city's
  half-hour boot is not in the district.
- **Long runs.** Half an hour shows a backlog growing; it does not show
  where it ends.
