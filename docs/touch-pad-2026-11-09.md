# The touch pad (2026-11-09)

What drives P0.11, the pad that flips the node's screen (roadmap D160),
and why the firmware reads it as it does.

## The bench

Version 16 on three boards, the pad read with the pin's pull-down,
high taken as touched:

| Board | At boot | Touched by hand |
|---|---|---|
| c367, f4f5 | `pad: reads high untouched`, then `pad: touched` 20 ms later with nobody touching | nothing more |
| 3112 | `pad: reads low untouched` | no edge at all |

So the idle level is not the same on every board, and on 3112 the pin
did not move.

## The circuit

From the maker's schematic, `schematic/T-Echo_Schematic.pdf` in
<https://github.com/Xinyuan-LilyGO/T-Echo>, fetched 2026-11-09, block
"Touch_Wake":

- U6 is a TTP223-BA6 touch sensor.
- Its VDD (pin 5) is `VDD_nRF`, the nRF52840's own supply, with 100 nF
  to ground. It is not on the switched rail (`PWR_EN`, `PWR_ON`) and no
  pin enables it: it runs whenever the chip does.
- Its output Q (pin 1) is the net `TP_SER_IO`, which goes to the
  nRF52840's P0.11. No resistor is drawn on it.
- Its sense input I (pin 3) goes to the pad P3, with 10 pF to ground.
- TOG (pin 6) and AHLB (pin 4) are drawn with no-connect marks.

The maker's examples name P0.11 `TTP223_TOUCH`
(`examples/Sleep_Display/pin_config.h`) and read it with the pin's
pull-down and an interrupt (`examples/Factory/Factory.ino`).

The TTP223's datasheet is not in the repository. As commonly published
for the family, and so to be checked against the datasheet itself:
TOG selects direct or toggle output and AHLB active high or low, each
with a default when left open (direct, active high); Q is a CMOS output
that drives both ways; and the chip calibrates to its pad at power-up,
taking about half a second during which a touch is not to be made.

## What follows

- No pin powers or enables the chip, so the firmware cannot have left
  it off.
- Q drives both ways, so the pin takes no pull: a pull-down would only
  draw current while Q is high, and could not hide an open output, of
  which the schematic shows none.
- The idle level is read at boot, when nobody touches the pad, and a
  touch is a change away from it that lasts 40 ms, then a return to it
  for 40 ms before the next (`bark-fw/src/button.rs`). Two boards
  idling high suggests their AHLB pads read high, or their chips
  calibrated against something at power-up; either way, a change from
  idle is a touch.
- 3112's pad gave no edge to a pull-down. With no pull and every edge
  logged in an open build (`pad: edge, High` or `Low`), the bench sees
  whether Q moves at all; if it does not, the chip or its pad is at
  fault on that board, not the firmware.
