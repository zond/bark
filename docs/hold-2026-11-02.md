# Holding on to a neighbour past its demotion

**Date:** 2026-11-02. **Status:** candidate, measured, the user decides;
roadmap D102. Spec references are to `protocol.md`. Built and measured
on main `c0ec923`, on which D96 is adopted and D97, D98, D100 and D101
are not. The branch's spec says three failures; the four-failure runs
are the same build with the scenario's `move_on_after_failures` set to
4.

## Summary

- **The candidate.** A message whose hand-over fails waits for the
  neighbour's ban and is offered to it again, demoted or not, and moves
  on only once the neighbour has failed three hand-overs in a row (N =
  3), or four (N = 4), where main moved on at the demotion, two. The
  waits are the ban ladder main already has, 1.58, 3.16, 6.32 s
  (spec "Bans"); the count is the neighbour's; the hold of one message
  on one neighbour is bounded by two bans and three hand-overs, 14.2 s,
  at N = 3 and 23.7 s at N = 4.
- **Why it was tried.** A quarter of the dense city's failed hand-overs
  leave the receiver holding the message; moving on then is the split
  the storm is made of (`storm-2026-10-30.md`). D98 found that more
  frames to the same receiver mostly meet one that cannot answer; a
  later offer, after the ban, costs no frames while it waits and comes
  after the receiver's reply reserve has refilled.
- **Measured.** The splits fall by a fifth at N = 3 and a third at N =
  4 on the dense city, 111 672 → 86 821 → 72 946 failed hand-overs with
  the receiver having the message on seed 1, and the split proper, the
  moves that left the receiver holding it, by two thirds and five
  sixths, 60 384 → 20 541 → 9 380, and the airtime with them,
  4 and 8 % less; delivery in the settled hour slips 0.3 and 0.4
  points, 97.9 → 97.6 → 97.5 %, and the median text comes 10.0 → 16.5 →
  19.9 s after it was sent, the p90 45 → 68 → 87 s. At twice the rate
  the city loses 0.6 and 1.5 points on seed 1 and 1.0 and 2.2 on seed
  7, for 14 % less airtime, the texts lost at the time to live 1 576 →
  1 734 → 2 167. Where the bucket binds, S4 at three times the rate, it
  gains 4.9 and 6.6 points on seed 1, 65.7 → 70.6 → 72.3 %, and 8.9 and
  7.8 on seed 2, with the median text 86 → 67 → 65 s, since a refusal
  now meets the offer again where silence sent the message the long way
  round. S4 at the design rate is level at 93.6 %; the ring gains 0.8
  points at a median 41.5 → 48.8 s; the walkers deliver 96.4 → 96.4 →
  96.0 % at a median 5.4 → 9.0 → 12.5 s for 7 and 12 % less airtime;
  the sleepy nodes' current does not move. The offers again say why: on
  the dense city 6.6 % of taken hand-overs are taken at the first offer
  again, 0.8 % at the second and 0.4 % at the third, and seven holds in
  ten end with the message moving on after the full hold.
- **Recommendation: N = 3 if the user takes the latency, not N = 4.**
  N = 3 buys the first offer again past the demotion, which is where
  the splits fall and the loaded network gains, for a median text 6.5 s
  later in the city and no airtime; N = 4 doubles that latency for half
  as much again in splits and loses more under channel load. Before
  adoption the hold should be bounded by the message's own count as
  well, see "what it revealed".

## The question

A hand-over makes three transmissions (spec "Hand-over" step 6,
`tables::HOP_ATTEMPTS`). When all go unanswered a failure is counted
against the neighbour, it is banned, and the message waits in its place
and is offered to it again; two failures in a row demote the neighbour
(spec "Neighbour table"), and on main the message chose its next hop as
soon as a failure had demoted the neighbour (roadmap D34, D42). The user
asked, 2026-11-02: keep the three transmissions, but hold on to the
same neighbour longer before moving on, the wait between failed
hand-overs doubling, 1.58, 3.16, 6.32 s, so that a receiver that took
the message and lost its ACKs, or one at its duty-cycle cap without
reply budget, gets another offer before the sender moves on and leaves a
split copy searching (`storm-2026-10-30.md`). The price is latency on
that hop.

One correction to the question's premise first. The ban after silence
already doubles: the k-th failed hand-over in a row bans the neighbour
for 1.58 s doubled k − 1 times, up to 61 s (spec "Bans", roadmap D42),
and the message's wait is that ban. What main did not do is hold on
past the demotion: the message's second failure, or its first at a
neighbour another message had already failed, moved it on. So the
candidate is not a new wait but a later move: how many failures in a
row before the message gives the neighbour up, and the message keeping
its neighbour through the demotion.

## The derivation

**What a failed hand-over leaves.** The receiver is in one of two
states the sender cannot tell apart: it never got the frame, or it took
the message and its ACKs were lost or could not be afforded (spec
"Duty cycle cap"). Three quarters of failed hand-overs on the dense city are
the first kind and a quarter the second (`attempts-2026-11-02.md`).
Moving on in the second case leaves two copies searching, the split;
offering the message to the same neighbour again heals it, since a
receiver that holds the message answers any later offer, as a
retransmission of the packet it took (spec "Receiving DATA" case 3) or
a copy merged with the one it holds (case 4), and one that has finished
with it answers seen (case 6) or returns it.

**The wait is the neighbour's ban, and nothing new doubles.** The ban
is pushback on the neighbour for every message (spec "Bans": silence
says the channel or the receiver is full, not how full, so the ban
grows), and a banned neighbour is no candidate for anyone (spec
"Next hop"). A message cannot be offered to its neighbour before the ban
ends, so a wait of its own shorter than the ban would be a dead letter,
and one longer than the ban would hold the message past the moment the
neighbour may be offered to, for nothing. So the message waits for the
ban, as on main, and keeps no clock of its own. At a neighbour whose
failures are all this message's, the waits are the ladder the user
asked for, 1.58, 3.16, 6.32 s; at a neighbour others have failed since,
they are the ladder further up, which is right, since the pushback is
the neighbour's.

**The count is the neighbour's, not the message's.** The neighbour's
consecutive failures, which demotion already reads, carry what a count
of this message's own failures cannot: a neighbour that has failed
three messages in a row is silent to everyone, at its cap, gone or
hearing nobody, and one more offer of this message learns nothing. A
count per message would also have no bound but the ladder's cap: a
message at a neighbour whose ban is 61 s would wait 61 s between each
of its own failures, N − 1 times, 122 s at N = 3 and 183 s at N = 4, a
fifth and a third of the time to live. With the neighbour's count, the
move at N happens at the latest when the ladder reaches N: a message
that finds the neighbour fresh waits the first N − 1 steps and no
longer; one that finds it at N − 1 waits nothing and moves on after one
hand-over, as on main after the demotion. The hold of one message on
one neighbour is then bounded by the ladder's first steps and N
hand-overs:

| N | waits, s | hand-overs, at most 3.15 s each | hold, s | of the 600 s time to live |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (main, at a fresh neighbour) | 1.58 | 2 | 7.9 | 1.3 % |
| 3 | 1.58 + 3.16 | 3 | 14.2 | 2.4 % |
| 4 | 1.58 + 3.16 + 6.32 | 4 | 23.7 | 4.0 % |
| 5 | 1.58 + 3.16 + 6.32 + 12.65 | 5 | 39.5 | 6.6 % |
| 6 | … + 25.3 | 6 | 67.9 | 11.3 % |

A hand-over is at most three attempts of 738.6 ms, listen-before-talk
until it gives up, a full frame and the ACK timeout (spec
"Message records"), and its retry waits, 0.93 s (`attempts-2026-11-02.md`).

**Which N.** Two bounds and one yield:

- *The receiver at its cap.* A relay below its reply reserve answers
  nobody (spec "Duty cycle cap"); once it stops spending, the bucket
  refills the whole reserve, 235 ms, in 235 / 9.83 % = 2.4 s. The second
  offer comes 1.58 s after the first failure, the third 4.74 s after it:
  the third is the first that certainly finds a receiver that was only
  out of reply budget able to answer. So N = 3 is the least count that
  covers the case the question names, and N = 4 a margin on it.
- *The receiver that holds the message.* It answers every offer, but
  the offer must reach it and its ACK come back: on the dense city 43.7
  % of DATA to relays arrive and 84 % of ACKs (`attempts-2026-11-02.md`,
  the fates), so one hand-over of three attempts gets an answer with
  probability 1 − (1 − 0.437 × 0.84)³ = 75 %, if the attempts are
  independent. Of the splits main leaves, one offer again heals three
  quarters, two offers 94 %, three 98 %: the second offer again, N = 4,
  buys 5 points, the third 1.6 for a hold doubled again.
- *The search.* A message that meets a silent neighbour at every relay
  of the design's eight-hop route (spec "Capacity and sizing") must keep
  at least half its life for the search beyond: 8 × hold ≤ 300 s, so
  the hold is at most 37.5 s and N at most 4.

So N = 3 by the reserve, N = 4 the largest the search allows, and both
are measured. The branch's spec and `tables::MOVE_ON_FAILURES` say 3.

**The demotion stays at two.** It orders the candidates: other
messages try a neighbour that has failed twice last (spec "Next hop"),
and this one is not moved by it. Moving the demotion to N would send
other messages to a dead neighbour N − 2 times more before they learn
to try it last, and would gain nothing, since the message that waits is
held by the count, not by the demotion.

**The message keeps its neighbour by state.** On main the offer again
was by choice: when the ban ended the message was routed afresh (spec
"Next hop") and the nearest candidate was the same neighbour, not yet
demoted. Past the demotion the choice would pass it over for any
neighbour that is not demoted, so a forward that waits for a ban now
keeps its neighbour (`send_queue::State::Holding`) and is offered to it
when the ban ends, unless the neighbour has left the table, been tried,
or failed N in a row meanwhile, in which case it chooses afresh. A
return has only its parent and needed nothing.

**The worst case for a one-way link.** A neighbour whose heartbeats
arrive here but which never hears us is never demoted by a heartbeat
(spec "Neighbour table") and leaves the table only 390 s after its last
heartbeat, which keeps coming. The first message to be offered to it
pays the whole hold, 14.2 s at N = 3 and 23.7 at N = 4, and its
failures bring the neighbour's count to N; every later message that
reaches it, as its last candidate since it is demoted, pays one
hand-over, 3.15 s, and moves on, as on main. When the demotion lapses
after 390 s the count starts again, so a relay pays one hold per
one-way neighbour per 390 s on top of what main paid, 7.9 s. Nothing
in the rule makes a message wait for a neighbour it has never reached
beyond that: the hold is bounded by the first N − 1 steps of the
ladder, whoever climbed it. One case main had stays as it was: a
message whose only candidate left is such a neighbour waits for each
of its bans and is offered to it again until its time runs out (roadmap
D95); that is the rule for banned candidates, not this one, and this
rule neither fixes nor worsens it.

## How it was measured

Every run is the scenario file at the seed named, with `--json` and
`--messages`, release builds. Main is `c0ec923`; its protocol behaviour
is that of the D96 runs made on `8ff3a74` the same day
(`storm3/d96b`), which this note's own main build reproduces on the
ring to the byte on the messages CSV, so those runs stand for main on
the dense city at both rates, S4 at three times the rate seed 1, the
ring and the mesh burst; S4 at the design rate, S4 at three times the
rate seed 2 and the walkers are this note's own main runs. The branch's
runs are one build, `ac88f23`, with the scenario files as they are
(N = 3) and copies with `move_on_after_failures = 4` under
`[protocol]`. Main's runs have no hold counters, those being this
branch's.

The scenarios: `dense-power.toml` seeds 1, 2 and 7; the same city at
twice the rate (`per_leaf_per_hour` 1.917) seeds 1 and 7;
`s4-capacity.toml` seed 1 at the design rate and at three times it
(2.875) seeds 1 and 2; `ring.toml`; `dense-movers.toml`;
`mesh-burst.toml` seed 1.

The figures are the JSON summary's as in `attempts-2026-11-02.md`, and
four new ones. A **hold** starts at a node's first failed hand-over of a
message to a neighbour and ends when a hand-over to that neighbour is
taken, the message moves on at the neighbour's last failure, the
neighbour refuses the message or answers seen, or the message is
dropped: the simulator counts the hand-overs taken at the k-th offer
again, the failures after which messages moved on, how the other holds
ended, and how long each lasted (`holds` in the JSON; `metrics::Holds`).
Per node, **moved on after a failure** is the failed hand-overs after
which the message chose its next hop or a return was dropped, and
**moved on with the receiver having it** those among them whose
receiver had the message's record: the split proper, where "failed with
the receiver having it" also counts failures the message waited out
and healed.

## What it revealed

**The hold's bound holds only while the ladder climbs, and the tail is
inherited.** The derivation
bounds a message's hold on one neighbour by the ladder's first N − 1
steps, since the message moves on when the neighbour's count reaches N.
Two things make a hold longer. An ACK from the neighbour to any other
message resets its count (spec "Bans"), so a neighbour that answers
others and never us starts the ladder again under our message, which
waits one or two short bans and fails again, as often as that happens;
main had the same, with one ban a cycle. And a message whose only
candidate left is the neighbour waits for its ban after it has moved
on, and is offered to it again (spec "Overload waits", roadmap D95),
which the hold counter spans too. The measured holds show the tail: p90
7.1 s on the dense city at N = 3, within the 14.2 s derived, but p90
181.8 s on S4 at three times the rate and a longest of 597 s, the time
to live, on most runs, and the same tail at N = 2, main's count, so it
is the second cause, main's, that makes it. The cure for the first is a count of the
message's own offers beside the neighbour's, moving on at N of either:
the hold is then at most N − 1 waits of at most the (N − 1)-th step and
N hand-overs, 15.8 s at N = 3 and 31.6 s at N = 4, whatever the
neighbour's ACKs to others; it needs a byte, or three bits, in the
send-queue entry. The second is main's rule for banned candidates and
not this one's.

**The offer again is a kept neighbour, not a fresh choice, and that
is a change of its own.** Main's offer again was whatever the next-hop
choice gave when the ban ended, which was the same neighbour only while
nothing had changed; a ban grown meanwhile by another message's failure
or a refusal sent the message elsewhere at once. The branch keeps the
neighbour and waits the grown ban out, and the N = 2 control shows
that alone moving delivery by −4.8 to +4.0 points where bans grow by
refusals, S4 at three times the rate and the ring, and nothing on the
dense city. Whether the kept neighbour should give way when its ban has
grown is a rule the spec now states one way (a kept neighbour, spec
"Hand-over" step 6) and main stated the other; it deserves its own
measurement.

**A wait costs no airtime, and that is the difference from more
attempts.** D98's six attempts and this N = 3 cut the splits by about
the same share on the dense city, a third and a fifth, and both make
the median text 60 to 70 % later; six attempts did it for 23 % more
airtime, 248 relays ever at their cap and 0.3 points less delivered,
the hold for 4 % less airtime, 7 relays at their cap and 0.3 points
less. What the receiver that cannot answer costs is time either way;
frames to it on top are a loss.

**Where silence is a full bucket, the offer again meets a refusal.** S4
at three times the rate is the one scenario where the hold gains
delivery, five to nine points, and its holds end with a refusal
22 715 times on seed 1 against 2 588 by a seen answer: a relay at its
cap that could not afford a reply to the first hand-over can to the
second, and the refusal's told wait keeps the message where it was,
which is what spec "Bans" wants of pushback. The dense city's silence
is collisions and weak links, which the second offer meets again.

Three things to read from it. **The split proper**, failures after
which the sender moved on while the receiver had the message, is
60 384 → 20 541 → 9 380 on the dense city seed 1, 53 523 → 18 970 →
8 299 on seed 7, 244 340 → 79 602 → 42 266 at twice the rate, 8 646 →

→ 18 052 for the walkers: two thirds fewer at N = 3 and five sixths at
N = 4, where "failed with the receiver having it", which counts the

the bans and hand-overs of a hold that goes the whole way; the tail is

neighbour by state moves some runs on its own**: against main, N = 2
delivers the same on the dense city, 97.9 → 98.0 and 98.1 → 98.1 %,

## What it does not show

- Other seeds of `dense-power.toml` than 1, 2 and 7.
- N = 5 or more: the derivation puts them past the bound, and the
  measured trend from 3 to 4 says where they would go.
- A count per message, with its wait capped: rejected on the bound
  above, not measured.
- The kept neighbour giving way when its ban has grown, as main's
  fresh choice did: not measured, and the N = 2 control says it matters
  where the bucket binds.
- D97, a wait never idle, on top of this.
- The board: nothing here was run on hardware.

## Recommendation

N = 3 is a trade the user has to take or leave, and N = 4 is not
worth its price. N = 3 does what the question asked: a receiver that
took the message and lost its ACKs, or could not afford them, gets one
offer more, past the demotion, and the split proper falls by two thirds
on the dense city and at twice the rate, with 4 to 14 % less airtime
and a tenth as many relays at their cap; where the bucket binds, S4 at
three times the rate, delivery gains five to nine points. The cost is
the median text 6.5 s later in the city, 3.6 s later for the walkers,
and under channel load, the city at twice the rate, 0.6 to 1.0 points
of delivery, since seven holds in ten end with the message moving on
after the whole hold. N = 4 doubles the latency, 10 → 20 s, and the
loss under channel load, 1.5 to 2.2 points, for half as much again in
splits, and the third offer again yields 0.4 % of hand-overs.

If N = 3 is adopted, two things first. The hold should be bounded by
the message's own count as well as the neighbour's, moving on at N of
either, so that a neighbour that answers others and never us cannot
hold a message beyond 15.8 s; that is a bit field in the send-queue
entry and a line in `wait_for`. And the kept neighbour's giving way
when its ban has grown, main's rule, should be measured against the
branch's waiting it out, on S4 at three times the rate and the ring,
where the N = 2 control says the difference lives.
