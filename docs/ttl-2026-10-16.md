# A message ends when its time does: measured

**Date:** 2026-10-16. Builds on `collapse-research-2026-10-16.md`, whose
draft decision has this as rule (2), and `backoff-2026-10-16.md` (D42),
which found that past the cliff messages still live 48 to 58 minutes in
the network. Measured against main `bddfb50`, paired by seed. Spec
references are to `protocol.md`.

## What was built

DATA carries one more byte, `time_left`: the time the message has left
of the 600 s delivery TTL, in whole units of 2.5 s, rounded down (spec
"Packet formats", "Receiving DATA"). The header grows from 21 to 22
bytes and the payload shrinks from 234 to 233 bytes, so that a full
frame stays at the radio's 255. A DATA without payload stays 18.24 ms:
at SF5 and coding rate 4/5 the 22nd byte fits in the 21st's block of
symbols. A 100-byte text, 122 bytes, stays 69.4 ms for the same reason.

- The leaf stamps each attempt with the TTL less the time it has held
  the message, and gives the message up once no more than one unit is
  left. A relay sending for its own application starts at the TTL.
- A relay that takes a packet to send on keeps when the message's time
  runs out: what the packet carried less the frame's time on air, which
  passed after the sender stamped it. Each attempt, retries included,
  carries what is left at that moment.
- With one unit or less left the message cannot be sent with any; the
  relay drops it (`Expired`) at once wherever it waits in the send queue,
  so its place frees. A hand-over whose frame is on air or waits for its
  ACK drops it at its next attempt. A packet that arrives with its time
  gone is taken, as no work, and the message ends there.
- A message at its owner is not affected: set aside for a full delivery
  queue or in it, the queue's own TTL, counted from its arrival, ends it.
- A return and a copy carry the time like any DATA, so a parent's copy
  ends by the same clock.

Commits `7c75335` (wire), `e8ba2c4` (the drop reason), `c0a3c46` (core),
`f91b681` (spec). `Relay` grows by one instant per send-queue place,
164 040 → 166 400 B on `bddfb50` and 169 160 → 171 520 B on the current
main; the leaf stays 776 B.

**The unit.** Rounding down loses up to a unit at each hand-over, and
a whole one at a hand-over that follows at once: a relay that receives
k units and sends a moment later has less than k left. So the unit also
caps the hand-overs a message has time for. 2.5 s is the finest unit at
which one byte holds the TTL (240), and leaves time for 239 hand-overs,
near the hop cap of 255. The draft's 10 s leaves time for 59. Both were
measured, and so was a third form, two bytes in units of 10 ms, which
loses at most 2.55 s over the whole hop cap. Rounded to the nearest
instead of down, a message that stays just under half a unit at each
relay would lose no time at all, so that was not built.

For the measurement only, the simulator split `Expired` by where the
message was: on receipt, in a hop place, waiting for a ban, waiting
its turn in the regular room, in a hand-over begun but not on air (a
listen-before-talk check, the bucket, or a retry wait), at an attempt,
or as a return. The split changes no result.

## What was run

S4 (`scenarios/s4-capacity.toml`, 13 channels of 125 kHz) at the texting
rate times 1, 2, 3 and 4, seeds 1 and 2. The loaded suites of D31 to
D36, 30 seeds each: the corridor at one text per leaf every 1 s and
0.5 s, the ring at 1 s and 2 s, mesh-burst at 64 times. The standard
suite: S1, mesh-burst, mesh-spread and the ring at 10 seeds, S1b, S2 and
the corridor at 60. "Delivered" and the other columns are as in the
backoff note: S4 counts the reachable texts sent from 900 to 3900 s. A
message's life runs from its origin's core taking it to its delivery
or drop; "in the network" leaves out the texts a leaf dropped for want
of a relay (`NoRelay`), and includes the wait in the owner's delivery
queue. ± is one standard error of the paired difference; with two seeds
it says little, and the S4 runs repeat to the frame.

**T** is the form built, one byte of 2.5 s; **T10** the draft's 10 s;
**T16** two bytes of 10 ms.

| S4 ×1, Expired a run | 1 | 8.5 | 0 |

T10 ends long searches that would have reached their owner: each of its
11 ends on receipt in mesh-spread is a message at its 59th hand-over,
5 to 50 s after it was taken, which main delivered, after 6 to 428 s
and 65 to 140 hand-overs. At S4 ×2 all 128 of its ends are on receipt. Under overload
the same cut helps, a little more than T at ×3 and ×4, because it ends
the long wanderers sooner; but it ends them by their hand-overs, not by
their time.

T loses one message in mesh-spread (seed 6): 122 hand-overs, so about
280 s of its time went to rounding, and it ended after 324 s, where main
delivered it after 428 s. T16 keeps that one; its own loss in that
suite is another message, which in its run wandered its whole 600 s.
T16 changes every frame by one byte, enough for a 100-byte text to cross
into the next block of symbols (122 → 123 bytes, 69.4 → 70.7 ms), and
it shrinks the record table it derives from the shortest DATA (1695 →
1642 on `bddfb50`), so its runs diverge everywhere. Under overload it
gains half of what T gains at ×3 and most of it at ×4: rounding no
longer shortens the lives of messages that make many hand-overs.

## Standard and loaded suites

T against main, paired. The corridor at 1 s and 0.5 s, the ring at
1 s and 2 s (30 seeds each), S1, S1b, S2, the corridor and the ring at
their own settings, and mesh-burst: **identical summaries**, to the
frame. No message there reaches 600 s, and their payloads gain no
symbol from the extra byte. Mesh-spread: 0.9989 against 1.0000 (−0.11 ±
0.11 points), one message, the one above; 2.9 ± 1.0 s less airtime a
run. Mesh-burst at 64 times: 0.932 against 0.943 (−1.0 ± 0.7 points),
airtime the same (+9 ± 21 s), longest life 1740 → 1167 s; it ends 1076
messages by time over the 30 runs, 635 of them in hop places, and the
spread between seeds there is wide (main's worst seed delivers 0.48).
No message was lost without a trace in any run of T.

**A neighbour that is gone** (S1, B removed at 1200 s, seed 1): identical
to main. The message that waits for B's bans is dropped for want of a
route 413 s after it was taken, when A's entry for B expires, 390 s
after B's last heartbeat (spec "Neighbour table"). That is before its
600 s run out, so the time does not end it; it would end a message only
where the wait is longer than the TTL, as behind a link that works one
way only (backoff note).

## What it does not do

1. It does not move the cliff. The collapse starts in the same bin as
   with main; the TTL stops the fall that follows it, not the loop of
   lost attempts that starts it.
2. A message's whole life is bounded by two TTLs, not one: the owner's
   delivery queue starts its own on arrival, since that is the wait for
   a dark leaf. Counting the queue's wait against the message's time
   would end messages for leaves dark for a few minutes; not measured.
3. Rounding costs a message up to 2.5 s a hand-over, so one that makes
   many quick hand-overs has less than 600 s: after 122, about 320 s.
   Measured, that ended one message in the standard suite.
4. A message that reaches its first relay in its leaf's last seconds,
   a unit or less left, is ended there, though its owner might have been
   a hand-over away.

## Recommendation

Keep it, as built: one byte, 2.5 s. At the spec "Capacity and sizing" rate and twice it, it
changes nothing beyond a message or two a run that would have arrived
in their last seconds; past the cliff it carries 1.3 and 1.6 times the
texts, uses 3 to 6 % less airtime, and bounds every life by two TTLs
where main's run to an hour. The draft's 10 s ends searches by their
hand-overs, which costs a point in mesh-spread; two bytes of 10 ms keep
the time exactly but give up half the gain at ×3, for a byte on every
frame.

## Decision

Not adopted (roadmap D44, 2026-10-16). The prototype was measured on a
research branch and not merged; the commit hashes above are not in the
repository. The user's reading of the numbers: a byte on every frame to
lift a domain past its cliff from a few per cent delivered to 16 to
40 % is not worth it, since a domain at that load is lost either way.
The capacity work goes into keeping a domain below its cliff.
