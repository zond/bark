# Rules the simulator had never exercised

**Status:** measured 2026-10-18, roadmap D59 to D65. Decided 2026-10-19:
D61 to D64 adopted (D63 with the relay keeping its first fix outright),
D60 and D65 not.

A review of the spec listed seven rules that no simulated run had put to
the test. For each this note says what the rule says, what was built in
the simulator to test it, what was run, what it showed, and what it does
not show. The models are scenario sections, off by default
(`docs/simulator-design.md` §3.7 and §3.8), so every earlier run and
test is as before.

## Summary

## The simulator for these

Six models, each described in `docs/simulator-design.md`: pairs of nodes
forced to share an ID; groups of sleepy nodes that go dark together;
sleepy nodes that ride in vehicles; interferers that reach walkers where
they are and may appear later; GPS fixes that jump or drift, with the
driver's plausibility checks; nodes held on one home channel. Heartbeats
lost at their receivers are now split by why, and the per-message table
has each message's destination anchor and the relays at its addresses.
Two small scenarios join the repository: `dark-groups.toml`, a district
of 4 km² at S4's densities with a relay at its centre beside a building
of 400 people, and `s3-interferer.toml`, the S3 of the catalogue.

Where an item did not need the whole domain it was run on the district
(40 s a run) or on a laboratory of a few nodes, and S4 only where the
item or its fix needed it. At most one S4 run went at a time.

## 1. Shared node IDs

**The rule.** Spec "Node identity": two nodes share an ID with
probability about n² / 2³³ among n nodes, one run in twenty for a domain's
20 000; it matters only among the nodes one node keeps in its tables.

**What was built.** `[[shared_ids]]`: pairs of relays, of a relay and a
sleepy node, or of sleepy nodes, that hear each other above sensitivity
both ways or have no link either way, drawn among the nodes that do not
move; the second takes the first's ID, or, for the comparison run, only
is marked. Each message's row says whether it reached a node with its
destination's ID that was not its destination.

**What was run.** S4 at the spec "Capacity and sizing" rate with ten pairs of each of the six
kinds, 120 nodes, seeds 1 and 2, against the same pairs marked only. The district of item 2, without
its building, with one kind at a time and many pairs, so that the pairs'
own texts can be counted: 5 pairs of relays, 10 of a relay and a sleepy
node, 100 of sleepy nodes, each in and out of reach, seeds 1 to 6.
Delivered counts the texts sent in the settled window.

**What it showed.** A shared ID costs the pair, and in one case stopped a
node.

| District, 6 seeds | To the pair | To the sleepy nodes of the pair's relays | From the pair | Reached the twin | Whole district |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 relay pairs in reach | | 87.9 % (91.4) | | 0 | 90.1 % (91.5) |
| 5 relay pairs out of reach | | 89.0 % (91.0) | | 0 | 90.6 % (91.5) |
| 10 relay–sleepy pairs in reach | 81.6 % (89.8) | 90.8 % (91.6) | 95.6 % (100) | 8 | 91.3 % (91.5) |
| 10 relay–sleepy pairs out of reach | 78.4 % (94.6) | 91.3 % (91.8) | 95.1 % (92.7) | 8 | 91.3 % (91.5) |
| 100 sleepy pairs in reach | 69.9 % (92.5) | | 87.9 % (89.4) | 216 | 89.7 % (91.5) |
| 100 sleepy pairs out of reach | 90.9 % (92.8) | | 90.9 % (90.1) | 15 | 91.3 % (91.5) |

(In brackets the same nodes marked only; 37 to 3 649 texts a cell.)
Two sleepy nodes in reach of each other lose a quarter of their mail,
nearly all of it to each other: a holder that hears both keeps one entry
for them, on the channel of whichever beat last, and sends to whichever
listens; the twin takes it as its own, being the node it names
(spec "Owner": `dst_id` is its own ID). A relay twinned with a sleepy node
takes the sleepy node's messages that pass it as addressed to itself.
Two relays with one ID ignore each other's heartbeats as their own, and
the relays that hear both keep one entry that jumps between their
positions and channels; their sleepy nodes lose 2 to 4 points of their
mail. Out of reach, with no relay hearing both, a pair costs nearly
nothing.

In S4 ten pairs of each kind cost the domain 0.3 points on seed 1
(90.8 against 91.1 %) and the 120 nodes' own texts within a few points,
too few to tell apart a run.

On seed 2 a sleepy node's core stopped the run: from 582 s it asked to be
woken at the instant it was woken, and the simulator did nothing else
for an hour. The node's anchor was a relay whose ID a sleepy node in
reach shared. That sleepy node's beats, which carry the sleepy flag,
removed the anchor's entry from the node's neighbour table (spec
"Neighbour table": a sleepy node's beat keeps it out of the table, before
D58); the rotation in which the node chooses after its first anchor then
ended without the anchor in the table, and that branch never cleared its
end, so the end stayed due. On the board this is a core that never
sleeps again, until its battery is flat. No message is lost for it
elsewhere, but the node is.

**Fix proposed, D64.** The choice after the first anchor is made when its
time comes, whether or not the anchor is still in the table (branch
`research/unproven-choosing`, `node/relay.rs` `reconsider_anchor`; a test
that is stuck without it). S4 seed 2 with the ten pairs a kind then runs
through: 90.7 % against 90.4 % with the pairs marked only.
S4 ×1 seeds 1 and 2 without pairs: identical to the last message (91.06
and 90.44 %).

**What it does not show.** Shared IDs among nodes that move; a pair one
of whose nodes is an anchor of the other. The loop was found through a
shared ID; whether anything else can empty an anchor's entry within the
first rotation was not searched for. Under D58, where beats fill the
neighbour table, the same sleepy twin marks the anchor's entry sleepy
instead of removing it; that path was not run.


## 2. Group dark spells

**The rule.** Spec "Delivery queue": nodes in one train or one basement
go dark together, so the queue holds what arrives in a TTL for every node
a holder can keep, its heard table's 256, at 23 texts a day each:
41 messages.

**What was built.** `[[dark_groups]]`: the given number of sleepy nodes
nearest a node go out of reach together for a spell, over their own
availability process, and come back to it.

**What was run.** Groups of 50, 200 and 400 sleepy nodes nearest the
centre relay of `dark-groups.toml`, dark for 5, 10 and 20 minutes from
1800 s while texts keep coming at the spec "Capacity and sizing" rate,
seeds 1 to 3, in two districts: as the file has it, with a building of
400 people within 30 m of the relay, and without the building, S4's
density alone, about 75 sleepy nodes a relay. In the building district
also with a delivery queue of four times the size, 164, a throw-away
build, to see whether the 41 places are what binds. Delivered counts the
texts to the group's nodes sent from a TTL before the spell to its end,
against the same nodes in the same window of the run without a spell.

**What it showed.** At S4's density the 41 places never bind. The group
spreads over the relays around, and the centre relay held at most 17
messages; nothing was refused for a full queue. The delivery TTL decides:

| Group | 5 min | 10 min | 20 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 82.1 % (96.6) | 75.0 % (95.1) | 42.1 % (94.8) |
| 200 | 83.9 % (93.5) | 68.2 % (94.3) | 42.6 % (93.4) |
| 400 | 84.7 % (95.2) | 70.4 % (95.2) | 44.6 % (94.2) |

(Delivered to the group, and in brackets the same nodes without the
spell; three seeds, 28 to 489 texts a cell.) Every message lost was
dropped by its holder when the queue's TTL ran out. A message that
reaches its holder at the start of a 20-minute spell has expired before
the node is back, and one that arrives in the spell's last minutes waits
up to a beat period after it: about half the window's mail is lost
whatever the queue's size.

In the building district the relay holds for 310 to 510 sleepy nodes,
more than the 256 its heard table keeps, and the table binds before the
queue, without any spell. A full table takes a newcomer only in place of
a weaker entry, and in a building all are about as strong: the nodes left
out are never in the table, so their holder never sends to them in the
windows after their beats, and their mail waits out its TTL. Without a
spell, per node of that relay, 82 and 115 of the nodes of two seeds got
none of their texts and the rest nearly all; delivered to the relay's
nodes 81.9, 67.0 and 54.0 %, against 92.6 % in the district as a whole.
The queue then fills with mail it cannot deliver, refuses (813 and 3041
refusals in two seeds) and the 41 places bind as well. Four times the
places deliver 0 to 6 points more to that relay's nodes and up to 15
more to a dark group (400 nodes for 10 minutes, seed 3: 53.1 → 69.0 %),
but leave the starved nodes starved.

**Fix proposed, D61.** When the heard table is full a newcomer takes the
place of an expired entry, else of the one heard longest ago: a node that
beats now listens now, so its holder can send to it in the window after
every beat, however many nodes it holds for (branch
`research/unproven-heard`, spec "Heard table", `tables/heard.rs`).
Measured:

| | weakest makes way | longest unheard makes way |
|---|---|---|
| Building district, to the centre relay's nodes, seeds 1–3 | 81.9, 67.0, 54.0 % | 92.7, 93.9, 93.5 % |
| ... whole district | 92.6, 87.4, 85.9 % | 93.9, 91.8, 94.5 % |
| ... refused for a full delivery queue | 0, 813, 3041 | 0, 0, 33 |
| District without the building, whole | 93.53, 91.60, 93.24 % | the same to the last message |
| Group of 400 dark for 20 min, building district | 39.1 % | 46.8 % |
| Group of 50 dark for 10 min, building district | 74.1 % | 84.5 % |
| S4 ×1, seeds 1 and 2 | 91.06, 90.44 % | the same to the last message |

**What it does not show.** The building's people all chose the centre
relay because it is the strongest they hear; a real building would also
hold relays of its own. Every group is static and comes back at once;
a train's people come back at different stations, to other relays. The
simulated application never sends again: a real one would, after a
group's spell, and fill the queues again.

## 3. Vehicles

**The rule.** Spec "Relay choice": vehicles cannot keep up and rely on
the delivery queue and a new anchor on arrival.

**What was built.** `[vehicles]`: a share of the sleepy nodes that do not
walk ride along one straight route. Each stops indoors at a point of the
route, a stop of ten minutes on average, then is driven to a point drawn
on the route and stops again; driven, its links are a walker's outdoors
less the car body's 9 dB (3GPP TR 38.901 §7.4.3.2), with the fading's
coherence time at its speed, and it takes a GPS fix before each beat, as
walkers do.

**What was run.** S4 as it is, a fifth of its people walking, and 5 % of
the others riding, 794 riders, on a route across the domain's middle from
west to east, at 10 and at 15 m/s, seeds 1 and 2; against S4 itself, in
which the same nodes stay where they were placed. A rider is driven 23 %
of the time at 10 m/s, 16 % at 15 m/s.

**What it showed.**

| | 10 m/s | 15 m/s | Same nodes in S4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivered, all texts | 88.9 % | 89.2 % | 90.8 % |
| ... neither end moving | 92.8 % | 92.9 % | 92.7 % |
| To a rider driven when sent | 7.4 % (271) | 5.5 % (200) | 91.7 % |
| To a rider at a stop when sent | 58.9 % (928) | 56.1 % (998) | |
| From a rider driven | 78.2 % | 83.0 % | 91.4 % |
| From a rider at a stop | 88.9 % | 87.6 % | |
| Anchor changes, a rider-hour | 4.9 | 4.7 | |
| Anchor checks, a rider-hour | 7.2 | 6.6 | |
| Time without an anchor | 0 | 0 | |
| Radio current, a rider | 1.95 mA | 1.85 mA | 0.33 mA |

Riders do not keep up, as the rule says, and the delivery queue does not
save their mail. A text goes to the anchor its sender knows; by the time
it arrives the rider has gone on, the old holder no longer hears its
beats, and the message waits out its TTL there (spec "Delivery queue":
nothing covers a node that has moved out of reach of its holder). Of the
texts sent to a rider being driven, 6 to 7 % arrive; of those sent to one
at a stop, under 60 %: the simulated sender knows the rider's anchor of
the moment, but early in a stop that is still one chosen on the way,
until a check finds the anchor of the stop.
Riders do not wait for their arrival to choose: each fix past the move
bound starts a check, a rotation's listening, and two missed watches
start another, seven an hour, which costs a rider six times a static
node's current. They are never without an anchor.

**Fix tried, not proposed (D65).** A node whose last two fixes are
farther apart than its move bound is on its way: an anchor chosen then
would be behind it at the next fix, so it checks once a fix shows it has
arrived. Built, measured and removed. S4 with riders at 10 m/s, seeds 1
and 2: checks a rider-hour 7.2 → 5.6, rider current 1.95 → 1.63 mA, but
texts to a rider at a stop 58.9 → 55.3 % and all texts 88.9 → 88.4 %.
S4 as it is, its walkers deferred the same way: 91.06 → 90.78 and
90.44 → 90.84 %, anchor checks −15 %, current 0.396 → 0.380 and
0.402 → 0.384 mA. And it takes S2 with every node walking below its
scenario test's bound, 55.8 % against 60 %: a walk of a few fixes keeps an
anchor left behind until the walker stops. It saves current and costs
mail, and the mail riders lose is lost in the old holder's queue, not to
checks. What would help a rider is a sender that learns its new address,
which the network does not provide (spec "Addresses").

**What it does not show.** One route, one speed per run, stops of ten
minutes indoors by the road; no traffic lights, no tunnels, no vehicle
stopping briefly. The application never sends again and never learns a
new address but from the destination's anchor; a real sender of a text
that got no answer may resend, to an address the rider's next message
gives it.


## 4. The answer heartbeat and the anchor watch

**The rule.** Spec "Heartbeat": a relay answers a node without an anchor
with a heartbeat outside its slots, with the long preamble, on the
node's channel; a receiver cannot tell an answer from a slot's
heartbeat, and one that takes it for its anchor's slot at worst checks its
anchor once. Spec "Relay choice", in its paragraph on sleep: the time a sleeping node
expects its anchor's next heartbeat is kept from the earliest heard.

**What was built.** Nothing for the rule itself: with D58, every sleepy
node receives duty-cycled between its windows and catches long frames, so
a sleeping node on the anchorless node's channel catches its anchor's
answers. D58 is built on `impl/sleepy-reach` and not yet on main, so this
item was measured on that branch's code (`c478530`), with `[[homes]]`
(a node held on one channel) and `[[dark_groups]]` carried over to its
simulator. A laboratory: a relay R with five sleeping nodes on its
channel, and a sleepy node A 300 m away that R hears, held on R's channel;
in one case A hears R too, and is dark through its boot listen so that it
is anchorless when it comes into R's reach; in one A does not hear R, a
link that works one way; in one A is on another channel; and one without
A. Three hours, true clocks drifting by up to 50 ppm, 20 seeds.

**What it showed.**

| Case | Answers | Checks per sleeping node | Watches missed | Listening, s an hour | Current |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No A | 0 | 0 | 0 | 55.6 | 0.282 mA |
| A hears R | 1.0 | 0.92 | 4.8 | 71.0 | 0.306 mA |
| A does not hear R | 45.6 | 19.9 | 203 | 399.0 | 0.817 mA |
| A on another channel | 45.4 | 0 | 0 | 55.6 | 0.282 mA |

One answer costs each sleeping node one check, as the spec says. But an
anchorless node that its relay hears and that does not hear the relay is
answered at once and a fade later each time its beat comes to the relay's
channel, about every seven and a half minutes, for as long as it stays
there. Each answer moves the time the sleeping nodes expect the anchor;
their next two watches miss, and they check: seven checks an hour, a
rotation's listening each, which triples their current.

**Fix proposed, D62.** A heartbeat of the anchor heard before the watch
opens, as early as the drift allows the slot to come, is no slot's: it
shows the anchor alive and moves nothing (branch
`research/unproven-watch`, on `impl/sleepy-reach`; spec "Heartbeat",
"Relay choice"; a test in `tests/sleepy.rs` that fails without it). In
the laboratory: no check in any case, the case where A does not hear R
58.1 s an hour of listening and 0.286 mA. In S4 at the spec "Capacity and sizing" rate on
D58's code, seeds 1 and 2, the domain's own anchorless spells make the
case rare: anchor checks 45 011 and 45 677 a run without the rule, 45 033
and 45 077 with it, delivered 91.21 and 91.09 % against 91.24 and
91.19 %, radio current 0.59 mA either way.

**What it does not show.** How often a relay hears an anchorless node
that does not hear it: in the simulator a link has the same loss both
ways, and only fading, a collision or a node's going dark makes one
direction fail. The laboratory forces the one-way link; the S4 runs show
what the domain's own anchorless spells do.

## 5. GPS plausibility checks

**The rule.** Spec "Position": a fix is not used if it jumps
farther than the node can have moved since the last accepted one (50 m
for a node that does not move), is inconsistent with the neighbours
heard, or has a C/N0 that is not sane.

**What was built.** `[gps]`: from a time on, chosen relays or sleepy
nodes get fixes that jump a distance in a direction of their own, or that
drift away at a speed. A relay with a fault takes a fix every second, the
receiver's usual output; a sleepy node one before each beat. With the
checks on, the driver hands a fix to the core only if it is within 50 m
of the last one handed over (a relay), or within 50 m and 15 m/s since
(a sleepy node, at the faster vehicle speed of item 3), and within 3 km,
the longest hearing radius, of at least one neighbour the core keeps.
The C/N0 check cannot be modelled: the simulator has no satellites, so a
spoofer that the C/N0 would reveal is here one it does not.

**What was run.** The district without its building, from 1800 s: 4 of
its 40 relays, or 300 of its sleepy nodes, with fixes 1, 5 or 20 km off,
or drifting at 0.5 or 2 m/s, with and without the checks, seeds 1 to 3.
Delivered counts the texts sent from 1800 to 4200 s to the sleepy nodes
anchored to a faulty relay, or to the faulty sleepy nodes.

**What it showed.**

| Fault | Unchecked | Checked | Without a fault |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relays, 1 km off | 90.8 % | 91.8 % | 91.8 % |
| Relays, 5 km off | 90.2 % | 91.8 % | |
| Relays, 20 km off | 28.2 % | 91.8 % | |
| Relays drifting 0.5 m/s | 63.2 % | 63.2 % | |
| Relays drifting 2 m/s | 11.1 % | 24.0 % | |
| Sleepy nodes, 1 or 5 km off | 93.4, 93.0 % | 92.6, 92.8 % | 93.4 % |
| Sleepy nodes, 20 km off | 34.4 % | 93.4 % | |
| Sleepy nodes drifting 0.5 or 2 m/s | 92.6, 91.1 % | 92.6, 92.2 % | |

A relay that believes itself 1 or 5 km off still gets most of its nodes'
mail: they address it by the position it heartbeats, it owns that
position, and the depth-first search of a district of 40 relays finds it
there. At 20 km the short position of its heartbeats, which repeats every
39 km of latitude (spec "Positions on the wire"), is rebuilt by its
neighbours half a period away from where it believes it is, so the
position its nodes address is one nobody owns. A sleepy node's own
position matters little, its address being its anchor's, except that it
rebuilds its anchor's position from the short one near its own: 20 km off,
it gives out a wrong address; and every wrong fix past its move bound
starts a check, two to five a node over the 40 minutes.

The checks refuse every jump of a relay, and of a sleepy node all that are
farther than 15 m/s can explain or farther than 3 km from every
neighbour. A drift does not jump: each fix is within 50 m of the last one
accepted, and the jump check, measuring from the last, lets a spoofer pull
a relay any distance, 50 m at a time. Only the neighbour check stops it,
at 3 km, which at 2 m/s comes after 25 minutes.

**Fix proposed, D63.** A relay, which does not move, checks a fix
against its first accepted fix, not its last (branch
`research/unproven-gps`, spec "Position"; the simulator's
`relays_keep_first`). Relays drifting 0.5 m/s 63.2 → 93.7 %, 2 m/s
24.0 → 91.8 %, jumps as before. In S4, 27 of its 269 relays drifting
0.5 m/s from the settle time, seeds 1 and 2, checks on: their sleepy
nodes' mail 30.6 and 39.5 % with the jump check as it is, 89.2 and
90.1 % with D63 (91.1 and 90.6 % without a fault), and the whole domain
70.8 and 74.1 % against 90.9 and 90.6 %: a relay pulled away draws its
neighbours' traffic toward where it believes it is, and the searches
for its nodes' addresses run out there.

**What it does not show.** A real spoofer moves every receiver in its
reach to the same false place, not each in its own direction; relays and
their nodes then agree with each other and the neighbour check sees
nothing amiss. Fixes of nodes without a fault are not handed to the
cores; a true fix changes nothing, but real fixes scatter by metres, which
the 50 m allows. Neither a relay that its owner moves nor a sleepy node
in a vehicle with a fault was simulated; a relay moved by its owner is
switched off and on, and starts from a new first fix.

## 6. Interferers with walkers

**The rule.** Spec "Channel moves": move only when the home is busier
than the quietest channel by 0.05 for ten minutes, then after a random
wait; the hysteresis keeps relays from jumping together. The realism note
could not run its interferer scenario with walkers, because interferers
reached nodes only where they were placed.

**What was built.** An interferer reaches the walkers where they are when
it comes on and again every 10 s while it is on; it may appear at a time
(`from_s`). S3 of the catalogue is now `scenarios/s3-interferer.toml`:
S2 with three rooftop relays that hear each other, and on channel 6 a
non-LoRa handheld at 10 mW, on a third of the time, that appears at
1800 s, after every relay has chosen its home.

**What was run.** S3 two and a half hours, 20 seeds: without the
transmitter, with it, and each with a fifth of the sleepy nodes walking,
and with half of them walking stops of ten minutes.

**What it showed.**

| | Delivered | Relay moves an hour after 1800 s | Off channel 6 a run | Moved twice, or onto 6 | Relay-minutes on 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No transmitter | 92.2 % | 0 | | | 8.5 % |
| Transmitter | 91.9 % | 0.035 | 0.90, after 836 s | 0 | 2.9 % |
| No transmitter, a fifth walking | 91.3 % | 0 | | | 8.5 % |
| Transmitter, a fifth walking | 91.4 % | 0.035 | 0.90, after 839 s | 0 | 2.9 % |
| Transmitter, half walking | 83.3 % | 0.035 | 0.90, after 822 s | 0 | 2.9 % |

Walkers change nothing in how the relays move: the relays on channel 6
when the transmitter appears leave it once each, 14 minutes after (the
ten minutes of measurement, the random wait and the sweep), none moves
again or onto 6, and the few relays left on 6 do not hear it. Half the
nodes walking costs delivery as the realism note found, with or without
the transmitter. Before it appears, the relays chose their homes at boot
without channel 6's busy time; a transmitter already there at boot is
avoided then, and nothing moves at all.

**What it does not show.** Pairs of relays that move within a minute of
each other, 0.3 a run, both left channel 6 for different channels; a
domain-sized test of joint jumps was not run.

## 7. Quiet slots around known heartbeats

**The rule tested.** Every node knows its neighbours' next heartbeat
times since D47 (spec "Heartbeat"). Proposed by the review and doubted
by the user: a node that would put a hand-over's frame on the air while a
known neighbour's heartbeat is due on that channel waits until the
heartbeat has ended, and then listens before talking again.

**What was built.** A research switch, off by default, since removed.
Before a hand-over's frame goes out, after its listen-before-talk, the
node looks through its neighbour table for a heartbeat due on the frame's
channel while the frame would be on the air: each neighbour that
heartbeats on its fixed interval has its slot on that channel a rotation
apart from the last one heard. If one is due, the hand-over waits until
that heartbeat's end and checks the channel again. ACKs, which must come
within the sender's 100 ms, and heartbeats, which may not come before
their slot, do not wait. Sleepy and anchorless neighbours have no slots.

**What was run.** S4 at the spec "Capacity and sizing" rate and at twice it, seeds 1 and 2;
the standard suites (S1 and the two meshes 10 seeds, S1b, S2 and the
corridor 60, the ring 10) and the loaded ones (the corridor at 1 s and
0.5 s, the ring at 1 s and 2 s, the burst mesh at 64 times its rate, 30
seeds each), each against the same seeds with the switch off. Heartbeat
loss counts the heartbeats of nodes that are not sleepy lost at receivers
that listened on their channel and heard them above sensitivity on
average, and of those the share lost to another frame.

It does nothing that can be told from the spread. In S4 at the rate a
heartbeat is lost to another frame at 1.1 to 1.2 % of its receivers, so
there is little to save; the frames that meet heartbeats at a receiver
are mostly sent by nodes that do not know that heartbeat's sender. At
twice the rate, where the domain slides into collapse at a time that
varies with the seed (realism note), one seed gains 2 points and the
other loses 6. In the suites only the burst mesh at 64 times its rate
moves beyond its spread, by 3.7 points, and the loaded corridors lose
what the rings gain. The row (D60) says so, and the code goes: it is not
on any branch.

**What it does not show.** The slot a node predicts is the earliest a
heartbeat can come; one held up behind a busy channel comes later, by up
to a second (spec "Heartbeat"), and is not avoided. A node knows only
its own neighbours' slots, while the heartbeats that its frames meet at
its receiver are also those of the receiver's other neighbours, which
the sender does not hear.
