# More attempts per hand-over

**Date:** 2026-11-02. **Status:** measured, not adopted; roadmap D98.
Spec references are to `protocol.md`. Built and measured on main
`8ff3a74`, on which D93 is adopted and D97 is not. The six- and
eight-attempt trees are kept on the branches `d98-six-attempts` and
`d98-eight-attempts`; main's spec is untouched.

## Summary

- **The candidate.** A hand-over retries its DATA up to six, or eight,
  times before it has failed, not three (spec "Hand-over" step 6). The
  retry wait would then double twice, to four units, 622 ms, and stay
  there; everything derived from the count is re-derived below.
- **Why it was tried.** An attempt is lost to another frame far more
  often than to a weak link, and three attempts, a second apart at
  most, can all be lost to the same busy stretch; when the receiver had
  taken the message on one of them, the sender gave it to its next
  candidate and two copies searched on, the split the storm is made of
  (`storm-2026-10-30.md`). A quarter of the hand-overs that fail on the
  dense city leave the receiver holding the message. Resending the
  whole DATA is simpler than a query and covers the DATA that was lost.
- **Measured.** The splits fall; delivery, latency, airtime and the
  sleepy nodes' battery pay for it, and under load the balance goes
  either way. On the dense city, seeds 1, 2 and 7, hand-overs that
  failed with the receiver holding the message fall by a third at six
  attempts (108 676 → 72 599 on seed 1) and little further at eight;
  the whole run's airtime rises 23 % and 50 %, the median latency 10 →
  18 → 28 s, the relays ever at their cap 73 → 248 → 442, and delivery
  in the settled hour 97.8 → 97.5 → 96.6 %. At twice the rate the city
  falls from 88.8 % to 78.6 % at six and 68.6 % at eight, with the texts
  lost at the time to live 1 741 → 5 153 → 8 710, though the splits fall
  three- and fourfold. S4 at the design rate gains 0.4 points for 15 %
  more airtime and a sleepy node's radio current 0.54 → 0.65 → 0.72 mA;
  at three times the rate, where the bucket binds, six gains 2.6 and
  3.9 points on seeds 1 and 2 and eight loses 3.8 and 0.4; the walkers
  deliver the same at a median latency 5.4 → 8.7 → 11.2 s. The attempt
  at which taken hand-overs succeed says why: on the dense city 70 %
  succeed at the first, 14 % at the second, 7 % at the third, and 4.1,
  2.8 and 2.1 % at the fourth to sixth, a yield that falls slowly
  because what the later attempts face is a receiver that is not going
  to answer, at its cap or out of reach, not a lost ACK.
- **Recommendation: keep three.** The extra attempts spend most of
  their frames and all of their time on receivers that cannot answer,
  and under load that time is what runs out. What the splits need is
  not more frames to the same receiver but a cheaper way to learn which
  of the two it is, which part 3 of the storm candidate, an attempt is
  a transmission, is measuring. Kept from this work: the two constants
  of the tables, `HOP_ATTEMPTS` and `ACK_TIMEOUT`, in place of literals,
  and the simulator's histogram of the attempt at which a hand-over is
  taken.

## The mechanism

Nothing in the hand-over changes but its count. Step 6 of spec
"Hand-over": on timeout, wait the retry wait and retry, up to the
count; then the hand-over has failed, a failure is counted against the
neighbour, it is banned, and the message waits for the ban and is
offered to it again with fresh attempts. A return to the parent and a
delivery to a sleepy node have the same count, a listen-before-talk
give-up counting as one, as before.

**The retry wait would stop doubling at four units.** The wait before
the retry after the k-th unanswered transmission is uniform in
[0, 2^k × 155.5 ms], which with three attempts means 311 and 622 ms and
with eight would reach 19.9 s. The wait is meant to clear what one
attempt holds the channel for, and the longest is a frame with the long
preamble and its ACK, 389.8 ms: four units, 622 ms, exceed it and two
do not, so the window would stop doubling there. A longer window clears
nothing that one attempt holds: a channel busy for longer is held by
many senders' frames and is as busy whenever the retry lands, so what
helps then is another attempt, not a longer wait. The window's other
job, pulling apart two hidden senders that collided at one receiver, is
done at four units at each retry more often than not, 9/16
(spec "Retry wait", roadmap D22). With three attempts the cap changes nothing, so it
is not in main: the ring run of the cap's commit is main's to the byte,
and S1b, the hidden pair of roadmap D22, delivers every text over 20
seeds at three, six and eight attempts alike.

The reply reserve grows because it holds the refusals the bucket refills
while a sender retries: a relay at the reserve can refuse, from the
refill alone, as many senders as would otherwise have spent that time
retrying it (`tables/budget.rs`). A relay's room for forwarding is the
bucket less the reserve, so six attempts take 7 % of it and eight 11 %.

The sleepy node's window grows with the hand-over it must let complete:
a delivery cut short by the window leaves its message to wait a beat
period. The node listens the window after every beat, whether or not
anything is held for it, so this is the cost that scales worst: 49 s an
hour more at six attempts and 82 s at eight, 0.08 and 0.13 mA at the
5.8 mA of listening, on the 0.56 mA of spec "Sleepy nodes"; S4 measures
0.11 and 0.19 mA (the tables).

## How it was measured

Every run is the scenario file at the seed named, with `--json` and
`--messages`, release builds: main `8ff3a74`, the branch
`d98-six-attempts` at its head, and `d98-eight-attempts`, the same tree
with the constant at eight and the unit tests' numbers to match. The
figures are the JSON summary's, as in `storm-wait-2026-10-31.md`, with
three added: the attempt at which each taken hand-over was answered
(`taken_at_attempt`, this branch's histogram commit, in main's runs
absent), the sleepy nodes' listening and radio current (`sleep`), and
the hand-over counters split between the relays and the sleepy nodes,
whose own messages are half the hand-overs of the dense city. The
scenarios: `dense-power.toml` seeds 1, 2 and 7, the same city at twice
the rate (`per_leaf_per_hour` 1.917) seeds 1 and 7, `s4-capacity.toml`
seed 1 at the design rate and at three times it (`per_leaf_per_hour`
2.875) seeds 1 and 2, `ring.toml`, `dense-movers.toml`, `mesh-burst.toml`
seeds 1 and 5 and `mesh-spread.toml` seed 5. Main's runs of the dense
city at both rates, S4 at the design rate, the ring and the mesh burst
at seed 1 were made the same day by other measurements on the same
commit, from sources that match it file for file; this note's own main
build reproduces one of them on the ring to the byte. The rest of
main's runs are this note's own.

## What it shows

- **The splits fall, by a third at six and little more at eight.** On
  the dense city the hand-overs that failed with the receiver holding
  the message fall 108 676 → 72 599 → 72 022 on seed 1, 114 376 →
  75 253 → 69 006 on seed 2 and 101 670 → 70 621 → 60 929 on seed 7; of
  the relays' failures they are 24–26 % → 20–22 % → 19 %. At twice the
  rate they fall 559 603 → 179 015 → 128 454, but so does everything:
  hand-overs taken 1.94 M → 1.03 M → 0.87 M.
- **The failures that remain are the ones that were always there.**
  Three quarters of the failed hand-overs on main have a receiver that
  never had the message, and at six attempts that is four fifths. The
  histogram says what they are: 70 % of taken hand-overs succeed at the
  first attempt, 14 % at the second, 7 % at the third, 4.1, 2.8 and
  2.1 % at the next three, 1.8 and 1.5 % at seven and eight. Were the
  losses independent, each attempt would recover the same share of what
  was left; instead the yield of each attempt after the second is about
  70 % of the one before, the mark of receivers that will not answer
  while the sender keeps trying: at their cap without budget for a
  reply (spec "Duty cycle cap"), or out of reach for the minute a fade
  lasts. The fates agree: DATA lost while the receiver listened and was
  free, the weak link, rises from 43 % of the frames at three attempts
  to 51 % at six and 54 % at eight, since that is where the extra frames
  go.

## What it revealed

**Half of the dense city's hand-overs are the sleepy nodes' own.** Of
seed 1's 417 166 taken hand-overs on main, 186 187 are sleepy nodes
handing their own message to their anchor, and of the 480 395 failed,
254 611: a sleepy node's hand-over fails more often than it succeeds,
and its DATA frames, 589 730, are as many as the relays' 623 859. Their
failures cost about two frames each, fewer than three attempts allow,
so most are not three unanswered frames but a channel found busy until
the give-up, which for a node with one candidate ends the hand-over.
This is the case the storm candidate's part 3 names, a busy channel
taken for silence, and it is larger than the splits.

**A sleepy node's listening follows the attempts.** The window after a
beat is derived from the hand-over it must let complete (roadmap D50),
and so grows with the count, after every beat, for every node, whether
or not its holder has anything for it: S4 at the design rate puts a
sleepy node's radio at 0.65 mA with six attempts and 0.72 with eight
against 0.54, where the spec's budget is 0.56 mA in all. Any change to
the count has to pay this, or the window has to learn to close early
when no DATA has started within the first attempt's span.

**The attempt histogram is the figure to read a retry rule by.** The
share of hand-overs taken at each attempt tells independent losses,
which decay geometrically, from receivers that cannot answer, which
do not; it is in the simulator's summary from here on.

## What it does not show

- Other seeds of `dense-power.toml` than 1, 2 and 7.
- Four or five attempts: the yield of the fourth attempt, 4 % of
  hand-overs for 7 % more frames, says they would sit between three and
  six, and nothing measured here says the trade turns.
- A count for forwards alone, with deliveries and returns kept at
  three: it would spare the sleepy node's window but not the time a
  silent neighbour costs, which is what decided this.
- D97, a wait never idle, on top of this.
- The board: nothing here was run on hardware.

## Recommendation

Keep three attempts and the spec as it is. Six attempts are a trade,
not a gain: a third fewer splits, and three to four points where the
bucket binds and refusals replace silence, S4 at three times the rate,
against the time every silent neighbour costs its sender, which where
the channel binds is delivery, the dense city at twice the rate losing
ten points and at eight attempts twenty, and at every rate a median
text 70 % later in the city, 15 to 25 % more airtime and a fifth more
radio current at every sleepy node. The receiver that
holds the message and whose ACK was lost is one case in four of a
failed hand-over; the other three are a receiver that cannot answer,
and more frames to it are the wrong tool. What would tell the two cases
apart cheaply is a receiver that answers a retry it has seen, which it
does since D90, reached by a retry that goes to it rather than to the
next candidate, which is part 3 of the storm candidate; that is where
the splits should be taken out, and this note's histogram and split
counters are the figures to measure it by.
