# GPS on the bench, 2026-11-06

Three boards, `bae6e6cbf4f5`, `25f161d73112` and `7572482fc367`, on one
desk indoors near a window, metres apart, all on USB and so all relays.
Their serial logs run from the evening of 2026-11-05 to midday
2026-11-06, with reflashes at about 10:43, 11:12, 11:24, 12:12 and
12:21. Every true fix on all three lies within about 100 m of
59.2149° N, 18.2128° E.

## Good fixes dropped

**Seen.** All three boards logged `gps: fix … beyond every known relay;
dropped` for fixes at the desk, `bae6e6cbf4f5` 101 s after its 12:21
boot, `25f161d73112` 13 s, 37 s and 74 s after boots and at 6424 s and 8372 s
of one run, `7572482fc367` at 97 s and 117 s. After their reboots of the
day each reported its address as `0.000000 0.000000` with one or two relays
known, while before them, knowing no relay, they had taken fixes at the
desk. Their stored state read:

| board | stored place | carried |
|---|---|---|
| `bae6e6cbf4f5` | 50.4501° N, 30.5234° E, given at an earlier build, 1250 km away | yes |
| `25f161d73112` | 0, 0 | yes |
| `7572482fc367` | 59.2148° N, 18.2131° E, its first fix at the desk | yes, after far fixes indoors |

**Cause.** A node had a place when it had any stored state (roadmap
D83). But a carried node does not boot at its stored place, which is
where it stood when it was last fixed; it booted carried at the position
it was built with, 0, 0 on a board built without one. From there:

- it rebuilt every neighbour's short position near 0, 0, so each true
  fix at the desk lay thousands of kilometres from every relay it knew
  and was dropped;
- it heartbeated 0, 0, whose short form its neighbours rebuilt as a
  point up to half a period, 19.5 km, from themselves, so they dropped
  their own fixes too once those were the relays they knew;
- carried, its stored place never changed, so every reboot did it again.

A board accepted a fix only while it knew no relay, which is how
`bae6e6cbf4f5` and `7572482fc367` had addresses at the desk before.

**Fix.** A node has a place stored only as a fixed node (roadmap D133).
One stored as carried and built without a place boots with none: it
sends no heartbeat, checks its first fix against no neighbour, and takes
that fix as its place, fixed, rebuilding the neighbours it heard near it
(`bark-proto/tests/first_fix.rs`, `a_node_stored_as_carried_has_no_place`).
The three boards heal at their next boot on this firmware.

## Still boards called carried

All three boards stored themselves as carried although none moved.
Two were carried because of their wrong places above: every fix at the
desk was far from 0, 0 and from 1250 km away. `7572482fc367` is the case
the carried rule is about: its place was its own first fix at the desk,
it logged a first far fix 170 s into one run, and a later run stored it
as carried; the Carried line itself fell where its log has a gap.

**What the logs hold.** The firmware logged a fix only at a change of
the receiver's state, so each `gps: fix at` line is the first fix after
a search or a loss: 53 lines, 46 of `bae6e6cbf4f5`, 2 of
`25f161d73112` and 5 of `7572482fc367`. The fixes of each second between
are not in the logs, so no run of far fixes can be read from them.

The L76K tracks down to −162 dBm and acquires down to −148 dBm, against
−130 dBm a satellite under open sky (`docs/gps-schedule-2026-10-25.md`).
Taking the weakest satellite seen, 8 dB-Hz, as near the tracking limit,
open sky would read about 40 dB-Hz, and these satellites are 10 to 30 dB
below it.

**What it says about the carried rule.** A still board at a window does
see fixes beyond the 50 m allowance (O16), and a place that is itself
one such fix makes them more frequent. The O16 fallback, a far fix
beyond twice the allowance, would not hold it: 12 % of pairs of fixes
lie more than 100 m apart. Fixes with a mean C/N0 of 21 dB-Hz or more
all lay within 34 m of the median, but they are 10 first fixes, and the
cut is read off the same data, so it is not yet a rule. And the core
counts a run of far fixes across the gaps in which no fix comes: it ends
a run only at a near fix. The 72 s assume one fix a second, but here
fixes come in periods with gaps between, so two far first fixes 72 s
apart, with no fix between, make a still board carried.

**Every fix.** Which of these makes a still board carried needs the
fixes of every second. The firmware now logs each fix the receiver
gives, with the horizontal dilution of precision and the mode of the
fix (`E` for an estimate without satellites) beside the satellites and
their C/N0:

```
gps: fix at 59.214715 18.212816 sats=4 hdop=2.3 mode=A c/n0 n=4 min=8 mean=17 max=24 dB-Hz
```

A night of these gives, for each board against its place (its `store`
line), the runs of far fixes, whether a run that made it carried was 72
fixes in a row or spanned gaps, and whether the far fixes are told apart
by their satellites, dilution or C/N0 from the near ones.

## The carried rule

**Radius.** Against the 50 m allowance the three still boards turned
carried ten times. From a place at the wander's edge a longer run would
need over 378 s at 150 m, adding minutes to a walker's delay, and the
wander stays out for tens of minutes, so a run that must keep growing
would need the same. A radius beyond the wander's width is what
separates them: 250 m, five allowances, is 51 m beyond the farthest
distance held for 72 s and 32 m beyond the farthest two fixes. A walker
at 1.4 m/s is past it after 179 s and carried after 251 s; at 1.5 m/s
after 239 s (`tests/carried.rs`,
`a_walker_turns_carried_within_four_minutes`). The replay of the
excursion above stays fixed (`a_still_board_s_wander_keeps_it_fixed`).

HDOP separates best: no fix with HDOP under 2 lay beyond 69 m of its
run's median. It is not used: the core is handed no HDOP, the place
itself may be a poor fix, and a node in a pocket gives poor fixes as it
walks.

**Gaps.** The run ended only at a near fix, so it spanned time without
a fix. Here the receiver lost its fix 13 times, so the flaw made no
carried board; it would on a board that loses its fix often, as the
first fixes of 2026-11-05 did. The driver now tells the core when the
running GPS gives no fix, and that time does not count (roadmap D136).

**To check.** The rule is from an afternoon. A night of the same logs
is to show whether the wander stays within 250 m of any fix.
