# Bring-up steps 4 and 5 on one desk

**Status:** procedure, 2026-11-06, not yet run. Three T-Echo 433 boards
on USB on one desk, metres apart, `bae6e6cbf4f5`, `25f161d73112` and
`7572482fc367` (called by their last four digits below), and two phones
with the app. Steps 4 and 5 are those of roadmap Phase 2. A quoted name
after "spec" is a heading of `docs/protocol.md`.

## What the desk lacks

### A line

Step 4 needs three relays in a line, A and C out of each other's reach
and B within reach of both. On one desk every board hears every other.

- **Distance.** A board's 10 dBm reaches the receiver's −119 dBm over
  129 dB of path loss (spec "Physical layer"). Rooms of a flat are 5 to
  20 m apart, 39 to 51 dB at 433 MHz in free space, and each wall adds
  some 5 to 15 dB: every board still hears every other with tens of dB
  to spare, at the lowest power, −9 dBm, too. The spec's indoor range is
  300 m, so a line by distance takes boards in different buildings, each
  with a host to log it.
- **Attenuators.** The bench row "Sensitivity and the loss curve" puts
  one board behind SMA attenuators. A line needs more: A and C some
  130 dB apart while each reaches B, so each board in its own shielded
  box with the links in coax through a splitter. A board's PCB and USB
  cable leak around a coax link far above −119 dBm, and the bench has
  no such boxes.
- **A board deaf to another.** A board built with `BARK_IGNORE=<id>`
  drops every frame of that node in its driver, as if its radio had not
  heard it (`bark-fw/README.md`). A and C built deaf to each other, with
  B between, are a line to every rule of the core, which decides on the
  frames it is given: the next hop, the per-hop ACK, demotion and the
  return to the parent run as they would. What it does not reproduce is
  the radio's part: A's radio still receives C's frames, so A's
  listen-before-talk waits for C, which on a real line it could not
  hear, and the collisions at B of a hidden terminal mostly do not
  happen. That part is the simulator's (`scenarios/s1b-hidden.toml`).

The third is used here: it is honest as a test of the routing, and
says nothing about hidden terminals.

The boards also need places 400 m apart. They are built in
(`BARK_LAT`, `BARK_LON`), and a built place now wins over the one a
board stored, fixed (`bark-fw/README.md`). The places are those of
`scenarios/line.toml`, far from the desk, so a fix the GPS takes at the
desk lies beyond every relay a board knows and is dropped. A board that
knows no relay takes the fix, a far one, and a run of far fixes 72 s
long turns it carried (spec "Position"): its log then says
`Carried { carried: true }`, and `texts` below warns. That happens only
while a board hears no relay, so no board is left alone for longer
than a neighbour's entry lasts, 390 s (spec "Neighbour table").

### A sleepy node

A board on battery turns sleepy after its grace only when carried, and
a still one stays fixed until its battery is low
(spec "Sleepy nodes"); unplugged, it would also stop logging. A build
with `BARK_ON_BATTERY=1` reports battery whatever USB says and leaves the
battery levels off, so the board is sleepy from its boot, as one booted
on battery is, while it stays on the host's USB and logs
(`bark-fw/README.md`). Nothing is unplugged. USB keeps its LED and its
32 MHz crystal running, which changes its current and nothing of the
protocol; the current is measured on the battery with a meter
(`bark-fw/scripts/measure.md` 5.3), which is not part of this
procedure.

### A board switched off

A board switched off is silent and deaf. The 1200-baud touch on its
serial port resets it into its bootloader (`bark-fw/README.md`,
"Flash"), where nothing drives its radio: to the others it is off.
Copying its UF2 to the bootloader's drive starts it again. Both are
done from the host, so no one touches a board.

## The tools

`bark-fw/scripts/bench.py`, run from `bark-fw/`:

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `build line A B C` | three UF2s for step 4 in `target/bench/`: A at the line's first place deaf to C, B at the second, C at the third deaf to A |
| `build sleepy R S` | the relay R at the first place, the sleepy node S (`BARK_ON_BATTERY=1`) at the second, and the console |
| `capture DIR` | every board's log into `DIR/<id>.log`, each line after the host's clock, again whenever a board comes back |
| `off ID` | the board into its bootloader |
| `on ID UF2` | the UF2 copied to the board's bootloader drive, which starts it; the drive is told by its USB serial, the chip's device ID, and the only drive there is serves when none matches |
| `say ID 'command'` | a console command typed into a board |
| `texts DIR` | each text across the logs: where a phone handed it over, each hand-over, return and drop, and where it reached an application, timed from the take; then the demotions and any turn to carried |
| `catch DIR S K` | for each beacon run of the console K, the frames sent and those the sleepy node S counted |
| `watch DIR S` | S's anchor, its watches for the anchor's heartbeat and what it was given |

`build` and `on` take a board's ID in its twelve hex digits, the other
commands also in its last four. `--name A=f4f5` labels a board in
`texts` and `watch`.

The images, from main `f818da7` with this work, against the 401 408 B
slot: a relay with a place 390 260 B, one deaf to a node as well
390 748 B, the sleepy build 390 156 B, the console 114 548 B. A build
without either flag is the size it was, 390 420 B.

## Step 4: a line, the ACK, demotion and the return

Roles follow the phones: A is the board phone 1 is paired with, C the
one phone 2 is paired with. Below, A is `f4f5`, B `3112` and C `c367`.
The simulator's twin is `scenarios/line.toml`; its test,
`on_the_bench_line_a_text_goes_through_the_middle`, says what the logs
must show.

1. Host: `scripts/bench.py build line bae6e6cbf4f5 25f161d73112
   7572482fc367`, and `scripts/bench.py capture logs-line` left
   running.
2. Host: for each board, `off <id>` then
   `on <id> target/bench/line-<X>-<id>.uf2`. Each log shows `node id=.. at 50.450000 30.5..`, and A and C
   `bench build: deaf to ..` the other.
3. Wait for each board's `boot relays=.. first_rotation_left_s=None`,
   the end of its first rotation, at most 195 s: A and C know one
   relay, B two.
4. Person: on phone 1, Settings, make the connected node the home if it
   is not; on phone 2 the same. A home whose address changed, here by
   its new place, sends the card to each contact, a text each way; if
   the first screen says the card changed, **Send my card**. About a
   minute.
5. Person: three texts from phone 1 to phone 2, a few seconds apart,
   and two back. About two minutes. `texts` shows for each the path
   A → B → C (or back), two hand-overs, each answered at its first
   attempt, about 0.1 s a hop and about 0.2 s from the take to the
   application on an idle desk (0.190 s in the simulator; a busy
   channel adds its listen-before-talk waits). Phone 2 shows each text,
   and phone 1 two checks once phone 2 acknowledges it.
6. Host: `off c367`. Person: two texts from phone 1, a minute apart.
   For the first, A forwards to B; B's three hand-overs to C go
   unanswered, B logs `Demoted` for C within 14.2 s of the take (spec
   "Bounds"; 7.4 s in the simulator) and returns the text to A; A logs
   the copy coming back and drops it, `NoRoute`. The second goes the
   same way after one unanswered hand-over, C being demoted already.
   Phone 1 shows one check, taken, and never two.
7. Host, within a minute: `off 3112`. Person: two texts from phone 1, a
   minute apart. A's hand-overs to B go unanswered, A demotes B within
   14.2 s and drops the text, `NoRoute`; the second is dropped after one
   unanswered hand-over. Within 5 minutes of `off 3112`, before B's
   entry at A expires (above):
8. Host: `on 25f161d73112 target/bench/line-B-25f161d73112.uf2`, then the
   same for C.
9. Host: `scripts/bench.py texts logs-line --name A=f4f5 --name B=3112
   --name C=c367`.

The person's part is steps 4 to 7, about five minutes of the phones
over some twenty.

## Step 5: a sleepy node

R is A's board, so phone 1 keeps its node and its home's address, S is
C's, with phone 2, and the console K is B's. Below, R is `f4f5`, S
`c367` and K `3112`.

### The long preamble caught

`scripts/measure.md` 5.1: a frame with the long preamble sent 100 times
to a board that receives duty-cycled, every 238.9 ms since roadmap D92
(spec "Duty-cycled receive and the long preamble"), at offsets spread
over the cycle. With no relay in reach S has no anchor and receives
duty-cycled at all times but its own beats (spec "Sleepy nodes alone"),
and only K transmits on its channel, so the frames its driver counts
are K's. Nothing is done by hand; about 30 minutes.

1. Host: `scripts/bench.py build sleepy bae6e6cbf4f5 7572482fc367`,
   and `capture logs-sleepy`.
2. Host: `off 3112`, `on 25f161d73112 target/bench/console.uf2`;
   `off c367`, `on 7572482fc367 target/bench/sleepy-S-7572482fc367.uf2`;
   `off f4f5`, so that R is silent until the next part.
3. S listens two rotations, 390 s, hears no relay, and receives
   duty-cycled from then on; its log says `usb=false`, and names its
   channel h in its `stored` line or a later `HomeChannelMoved`, which
   `catch` prints.
4. Host, h being that channel's number: `say 3112 'preamble long'`,
   `say 3112 'ch h'`, `say 3112 'beacon h 5000 36'`, after 510 s
   `say 3112 stop`; then the same with `preamble short`; then a
   two-minute wait for S's next `driver` line.
5. Host: `scripts/bench.py catch logs-sleepy c367 3112`. Expected: of
   the 100 long ones, all but a frame that meets one of S's own beats,
   249 ms on the air every 34.6 s on average, so 1.5 in 100: 98 or more
   is a pass. Of the short ones, about 2 (`scripts/measure.md` 5.1); far
   more says S listened all the time.

### The anchor, its watch, the window and the queue

About 20 minutes, four of them the person's.

1. Host: `on bae6e6cbf4f5 target/bench/sleepy-R-bae6e6cbf4f5.uf2`. R's
   log names its home channel r, in its `stored` line or a later
   `HomeChannelMoved`; `say 3112 'ch r'` sets K listening there, where
   it prints every frame, S's beats among them once S anchors.
2. Host: `off c367`,
   `on 7572482fc367 target/bench/sleepy-S-7572482fc367.uf2`. S boots,
   hears R's heartbeat within one of R's rotations, takes R as its
   anchor at once and listens a rotation more (spec "Relay choice"):
   `watch` shows `anchor R` within 6.5 minutes.
3. Wait two of R's rotations, 13 heartbeat intervals of 14.6 s each
   for R's ID, 190 s: S's log shows an `AnchorWatch` with `heard: true`
   once a rotation, and K's an `rx` of a 13-byte heartbeat with S's
   `id`, its beat, every 150 to 300 s (spec "Beats").
4. Person: phone 2, Settings, home: none. Its card goes to phone 1:
   S's own message, which S hands to R, and R's log has
   `message id=..`.
5. Person: three texts from phone 1 to phone 2, sent one after the
   other. R queues each for S (`Queued`) and delivers the first at once
   with the long preamble, `Delivered { .., by: Long }`, and the others
   while S listens after taking it, `by: Window` (spec "Delivery",
   "Delivery queue"); S logs `message id=..` for each and phone 2 shows
   them.
6. Host: `off c367`. Person: one more text from phone 1. R's delivery
   with the long preamble goes unanswered: `DeliveryWaits { .., tried:
   true }`, and the text waits for S's next beat. Host, half a minute
   later: `on 7572482fc367 target/bench/sleepy-S-7572482fc367.uf2`. S
   boots with its stored anchor and beats to it at once; R delivers in
   the window after that beat, `by: Window`, within seconds.
7. Host: `scripts/bench.py watch logs-sleepy c367 --name R=f4f5` and
   `texts logs-sleepy --name R=f4f5 --name S=c367`.

Afterwards phone 2's home goes back to its node once that node is a
relay again, and each board takes its usual build.
