# No time to live: measured, and what pushback needs instead

**Status:** research note, 2026-11-02. Roadmap D104. Measured against main
`c0ec923` (D98 in), paired by seed; D102, merged since, is not in these
runs. Spec references are to `protocol.md`.

## The question

A message the network has taken ends when its time runs out, 600 s after
its origin took it from the application (spec section "Time to live" (before D113), roadmap
D44). The position put to this note: nothing accepted from the
application should be dropped by age; overload should be refused at the
origin, where the application sees it; twenty minutes of latency is
acceptable if every accepted message is likely to arrive. The delivery
queue at a sleepy destination's owner keeps its own age rule (spec
"Delivery queue").

The protocol already has the pushback that this needs (roadmap D29 to
D36, D42): a relay takes a packet to send on only if its bucket holds the
airtime of everything it has queued (spec "Receiving DATA"); fresh work,
a sleepy node's own message or the relay's own application's, is also
refused while the neighbour it would go to is banned, or every live
neighbour is (spec "Receiving DATA", roadmap D34); a refusal tells its
sender how long to wait, and the sender bans the refuser for that long
(spec "Bans"); a message whose candidates are all banned waits where it
is (spec "Overload waits"). The time to live was added on top of this
because past the cap messages lived 48 to 58 minutes in the network and
kept the relays' buckets dry (`ttl-2026-10-16.md`). The question is
whether, with the pushback as it now stands, the time to live can go,
and if not, what the pushback lacks.

## What was measured

Two regimes, each with a time to live (main) and without:

- **Bucket-bound:** S4 (`scenarios/s4-capacity.toml`, 13 channels) at
  three times the texting rate, where the relays are at their duty cap
  a quarter of the time.
- **Channel-bound:** the dense city (`scenarios/dense-power.toml`, 270
  relays, 20 000 sleepy nodes) at twice the texting rate, where the
  relays are at the cap under 3 % of the time and the frames are lost to
  each other (`storm-2026-10-30.md`).

Plus both at the design rate, where nothing should change.

**Without a time to live** means a two-line change to the core for the
measurement only: a packet taken to send on and a node's own message
never expire (`relay/forward.rs` `verdict` and `originate` set `expires`
to the end of time); the wire byte stays.

On top of that, four forms of pushback, each described with its numbers
in "The prototypes" below: the regular room of the send queue forced to
3 and to 6 places instead of 39; fresh work refused while the send queue
holds a line of 19; fresh work refused while the queue could not be
served within the longest wait at the hand-over pace the relay measures;
and a refusal that tells that time instead of the bucket's refill.

The simulator counts, per relay and minute, the next-hop candidates and
how many are banned, the count-0 DATA answered taken and refused, and,
for the pace prototypes, the pace measured (`--series`); the counts change
no behaviour.

The columns, over the settled hour (texts originated from 2700 to
6300 s; S4 runs use the same window):

- *accepted*: taken by the origin's core and not given back to the
  application for good;
- *refused at origin*: never taken, or given back for good (the
  application's retries ran out of run);
- *within 20 min* and *by end*: delivered, of the accepted (the former
  of those with 20 minutes of run left);
- *lost*: accepted and dropped anywhere but the origin;
- *in flight at end*: accepted and neither delivered nor dropped when
  the run ends, the backlog;
- *p50*, *p90*: latency of the delivered;
- *airtime*: all nodes, the whole run;
- *at cap*: the share of relay-seconds with less than the reply reserve
  and one full frame in the bucket.

## Why the origin sees one regime and not the other

Every quantity the pushback uses is derived from the bucket. Room is
airtime queued against the bucket's content; a refusal's told wait is
the bucket's refill of what is queued; the shortest ban is the refill of
one frame; the silence ladder resets on any ACK. Where the bucket is what
the queue drains by, that is exact; where the channel is, it is blind.
The per-minute series of the runs without a time to live show the two
regimes side by side:

| | S4 ×3 | city ×2 |
|---|---|---|
| relay-minutes at the cap | 45 % | 4 % |
| relays whose fresh work is refused: bucket p50 | 0.30 s | 0.31 s |
| relays whose fresh work is taken: bucket p50, queue p90 | 5.4 s, 4 | 6.0 s, 1 |
| fresh work taken at queues under 5 | 94 % | 99 % |
| fresh taken : refused per 600 s | 5300 : 36 000 | 2000 : 2000 |
| queued in all, start → end of the hour | 663 → 778 | 1498 → 13 064 |
| of it in hop places at the end | 723 | 11 503 |
| relays holding 19 or more at the end | 0 | 273 |
| those relays: channel busy p50, at cap | – | 24 %, 67 % |

**S4 at three times the rate.** The relays spend 45 % of their minutes
at the cap, and a refusing relay's bucket sits at the reserve, 0.30 s.
Its hand-over pace is the bucket's: 1.37 s at the median against the
1.58 s a full frame's refill takes. So the bucket refuses fresh work
seven times in eight at the relays sleepy nodes hand to, the refusal's
told wait is the time the line really takes, the backlog stays under
1000 queued packets, and the origins see it.

**The city at twice the rate.** The relays are at the cap 4 % of the
time. Fresh work enters at the edge: 99 % of it is taken at a relay with
fewer than five packets queued and a full bucket, the anchor of the
sleepy node that sent it, which has room by every rule the protocol has.
The backlog sits elsewhere, at the 273 relays that hold 19 or more at the
end, hubs whose channel is busy a quarter of the time: 11 500 of the
13 000 packets queued are in hop places, taken by the rule that a packet
already in the network is never refused while a hop place of its count
is free (spec "Send queue", roadmap D31), and the regular room, bucket-
gated, is full at those hubs two thirds of the time. A hub refuses a
forward only once the hop places its count may use are taken, and then
tells its sender the bucket's refill of what it holds, a second or two,
after which the sender comes back and is refused again, or goes round by
another hub. Meanwhile that sender's own bucket is full and its queue
short, so it takes fresh work.

The told wait is not what stops the pressure there. A hub's refusal
bans it at the sender for the wait told, 25 to 49 s before the pace,
50 to 100 s with it; but a refused forward chooses its next hop at once
(spec "Bans"), and an edge relay in the city has 61 live candidates at
the median, so the forward goes round by another hub and the ban diverts
the traffic rather than stopping it. Fresh work at the edge is refused
only while the neighbour it would go to for its own destination is
banned (roadmap D34): with one or two of 61 banned at a time, two at the
90th percentile with the pace, that is seldom the one. The ban is a
signal about one neighbour; the congestion is at the city's centre,
several hops on, and every hop dissipates the signal across dozens of
alternatives. In S4 the relays that take from the sleepy nodes are the
hubs, and the signal has no distance to travel. Nothing carries the
centre's state to the edges, where the taking is done: the pushback
stops hops short of the origin, and the queue it should have stopped
grows all hour.

## Pushback from the pace the relay achieves

What is scarce at a hub in the channel-bound regime is its time: a
hand-over that goes unanswered takes its three attempts and two retry
waits, up to 3.15 s, and ends in a ban; one held for the bucket takes
the bucket's refill. A relay can measure that, and it sees both regimes
in one number.

**The pace.** Each hand-over's span, from when it is
started to when it ends, taken, refused, seen or failed, every wait inside
it included, goes into a window; the pace is the sum of the spans over
the count. One hand-over runs at a time, so the sum is the time the relay
spent handing over, and the pace its throughput while it had work: an
idle relay measures nothing, and is not slow. The window is the longest
wait, 61 s, the refill of a whole bucket (spec "Duty cycle cap"): the
pace over the time a queue is allowed to take, just past, is the estimate
of the pace over that time to come. It is counted in bans, 1.58 s, the
unit a refusal tells its wait in and the shortest anything waits (spec
"Bans"): ⌈61 / 1.58⌉ = 39 bins of a count and a sum, 312 bytes, no
allocation.

**Admission.** Fresh work is refused while the queue's length times the
pace exceeds the longest wait: taken, it would wait here longer than
anything waits for a bucket. Without a measurement the bucket's rule
alone decides, as today.

**The told wait.** A refusal for want of room asks for the bucket's
refill of everything queued or the queue's length times the pace,
whichever is longer, in whole bans as before, at least one and at most
63 (spec "Bans"); the senders refused still stand one ban apart, and a
pushback passed on is still passed on as it is. Told the line's real
length, the sender bans the refuser for as long as the line is, and
refuses its own fresh work for the way through it (roadmap D34): the
pressure travels upstream without any new gate.

**The fixed line**, the alternative measured first: fresh work refused
while the queue holds 19 or more, the longest wait over the longest
hand-over, 61 s / 3.15 s, the pace a relay has when nothing answers it.
The pace rule contains it: at that pace the two refuse at the same
length; where hand-overs are answered the pace admits more.

## The prototypes

Each was built on the core without a time to live and run paired by
seed with the runs above; the told wait was also run with the time to
live kept, against main. None is adopted, and the code is removed. The
pace rules were built in `efefa50` (the window, `tables/pace.rs`, and
admission) and `ad25375` (the told wait) on the branch
`worktree-agent-ttl`; the others are described here closely enough to
rebuild: the room by the regular-room constant of the send queue, the
line of 19 by a test of the queue's length beside the bucket's in the
fresh-work admission of `relay/forward.rs`.

**The regular room at 3 and 6.** Room 6 never binds. Room 3 changes
nothing but the seed's spread in either regime, with or without a time
to live: a packet the room refuses takes a hop place, so a shorter room
moves the backlog, it does not shorten it.

**The line of 19.** Byte-identical to no TTL on S4 at the design rate
and at three times it, and in the city at the design rate: the bucket's
rule fires first at every relay, and no admitting relay reaches 19. In
the city at twice the rate it fires a few times an hour, the relays that
take fresh work holding 21 at most: accepted 35 708 → 35 711, refused at
the origin 1.8 → 1.7 %, by the end 82.8 → 83.5 %, the backlog 5954 →
5755, airtime −0.6 %, the queued packets 13 064 → 12 854 at the end: the
seed's spread, and the same growth.

**Admission by pace.** Byte-identical to no TTL on S4 at three times the
rate, as the series explain: at the relays that take fresh work the
queue times the pace is 5 s at the 90th percentile and 30 s at most;
at those that refuse it, by the bucket, 17 and 60 s. The rule never
fires, and the bucket already refuses what it would; byte-identical at
the design rate too. In the city it can hardly fire: the relays that
take fresh work hold one packet at the 90th percentile and measure a
pace of 0.7 s, the queue times the pace 42 s at most in the minutes the
series samples; the relays
that refuse fresh work, the hubs, measure 1.8 s at the median and 74 s
at the 90th percentile for their queue, and the bucket refuses there
first. In the city at the design rate the run is byte-identical to no
TTL too; at twice the rate it is identical for the first 80 minutes and
then fires a few times between the series' samples: accepted 35 708 →
35 691, refused at the origin 1.8 → 1.8 %, by the end 82.8 → 82.8 %, the
backlog 5954 → 5955, the queued packets 13 064 → 13 306 at the end. The
rule asks the right question at the wrong relays: fresh
work is admitted where it enters, at the edge, whose queues are short
and whose hand-overs are answered; the slow relays are hubs several hops
in, which take no sleepy node's message and whose own fresh work the
bucket already refuses. It was removed before the told wait was
measured further; the told wait's runs carry it, and in their series
the queue times the pace at the admitting relays stayed under 49 s.

**The pace in the told wait.** S4 at three times the rate: accepted
35 176 → 35 656, refused at the origin 25.9 → 25.2 %, by the end
77.3 → 78.0 %, lost 0.6 → 0.4 %, the backlog 7788 → 7695, airtime −1 %,
every line a little better and all within a seed's spread. The city at
twice the rate: the waits told grew as meant, from 16 to 31 bans for
most refusals to 32 to 63 (the ACK's six bits hold 63; `refusal_steps`
in the run's JSON: 37 073 of 74 417 refusals in the 16-to-31 bin before,
42 207 of 59 191 in the 32-to-63 bin after), so the senders came back
less often, 151 046 → 124 029 refusals taken, and the count-0 messages
were refused less often, 11 175 → 7716; but as many were taken, 18 042
→ 18 067, the backlog grew as before, 1498 → 12 427 packets queued
against 13 064, and the hour's figures are the same to the seed's
spread. The waits told were not short to begin with: a hub holding 40 to
80 packets in hop places already told the bucket's refill of their
airtime, 25 to 49 s. The pressure the longer ban adds goes nowhere, for
the reason "Why the origin sees one regime and not the other" gives.

## What it revealed

The time to live does two jobs, and the pushback does only one of them.
Where the bucket binds, the pushback carries the load back to the
origin, and the time to live only throws away what the network would
have delivered: without it the loss goes from 8 to under 1 %, and the
origins refuse what the network cannot carry. Where the channel binds,
no quantity the pushback has reaches the origins: the relays that take
fresh work are at the edge, idle by every measure they have, and the
hubs that are slow are several hops in, behind dozens of alternatives
each. There the time to live is the network's only load shedding: the
4 % it drops is what keeps the backlog flat at 2600 messages, and
without it the backlog grows by 800 every ten minutes, the airtime it
spends rises by 43 %, and each group of texts does worse than the last.

Two facts make it hard to replace from inside a relay. First, a
relay's own measure of how slow it is, bucket or pace, is accurate,
but only for itself; the slow relays are not where the work enters.
Second, a ban is a statement about one neighbour, and in the city a
relay has 61 of them: a refusal sends the work round, not back. What
would replace the time to live is a signal that carries the centre's
state out to the edges, toward the origins, that a relay's fresh-work
rule can read; nothing in the protocol does that today, and this study
did not build one.

Were the time to live removed, two consequences would follow that the
measurement shows. Under a long overload the traffic already admitted
outranks the new: the origins refuse new texts while old ones, some
older than twenty minutes, are still carried (S4 ×3: p90 618 → 734 s).
And a sleepy node's own message, retried after each unanswered
hand-over to its anchor and waiting for a live neighbour when it has
none until its time runs out (spec "A sleepy node's own message"),
would need an end of another kind, or it would be retried for ever.

## What it does not show

One seed of each scenario; the city's spread between seeds at twice the
rate is about a point of delivery (roadmap D100). The simulator's
application offers a refused text again until it is told to wait past
600 s from when it was written, then gives up: a real app may be more or
less patient, and the "refused at origin" column moves with it. No
scenario with carried nodes was run. The pace's window and bins are
derived, not tuned; no other width was measured. The city's runs with a
room of 3 at twice the rate ran to their end, but their queue wrapper
was stopped before it wrote its mark; the tables use their output as
written.

## Recommendation

Keep the time to live. Removing it is safe and an improvement where the
bucket binds, and at the design rate it changes nothing, but in the
channel-bound city it turns a flat backlog into a growing one: delivered
by the end 90.0 → 82.8 %, in flight at the end 2166 → 5954 and rising
all hour, airtime +43 %, p90 374 → 693 s, while the origins refuse only
1.8 % instead of 0.6 %. None of the four forms of pushback measured
changes that, since none reaches the relays where the work enters.

Adopt neither pace rule: admission by pace all but never fired, and the told
wait at the pace changes nothing past the seed's spread, with or
without the time to live. Their code is removed.

The removal can be asked again once there is a pressure signal that
travels from the congested centre to the edge relays that take fresh
work. With it, the work left for a removal is the spec's "Time to live"
rewritten as what replaces it (pressure to the origin, the hop cap, no
route, and the delivery queue's own rule), the sleepy node's own
message given an end without a clock, the trade that old admitted
traffic outranks new stated, and the wire byte dropped or kept.
