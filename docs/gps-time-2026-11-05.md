# The GPS on the board, and the time of day

**Date:** 2026-11-05. **Status:** built and checked on the host; not run
on a board (below, "On the bench"). Roadmap D129 and D130. Spec
references are to `protocol.md`.

Until this note the firmware left the L76K alone: powered on the switched
rail with the radio, running at 29 mA from boot, its fixes never read, and
`Action::Locate` only logged. The core already said when the GPS is to run
(spec "Position", "Beats"; D80, D82): this note is the driver that obeys
it, and the time of day the node can show, from the GPS or from the phone.

## The receiver on the board

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UART | NMEA arrives on P1.09, the nRF52840's RX; P1.08 is its TX | `docs/implementation-research-2026-10-14.md` §2, `bark-fw/src/board.rs` |
| Rate | 9600 bps, 8N1, by default | the Waveshare L76K GPS Module wiki, read 2026-11-05 |
| Standby | WAKEUP low: RF off, core and RAM on, 20 µA; high: running, 29 mA | L76K Hardware Design V1.0, Table 2, §3.3.3 (`docs/gps-schedule-2026-10-25.md`) |
| WAKEUP | P1.02 | `board.rs` |
| Reset | P1.05, active low; not to be held low on USB | `board.rs` |
| Power | the switched rail, `PWR_EN AND (PWR_ON OR VBUS)`, up whenever the radio is | `bark-fw/README.md`, Radio power |
| Hot start | 2 s with orbits under four hours old, kept through standby; cold 30 s | Table 2; IS-GPS-200 §20.3.3.4.3.1 |

The driver drives P1.02 high to run the receiver and low for standby,
from boot, and never drives the reset line. The UART (UARTE1) is on only
while the receiver runs; it reads until the line is idle for two
characters (TIMER2 and two PPI channels), so a second's sentences come in
a few reads rather than one wake per byte. Its interrupt runs at
priority 2, with every other interrupt of ours, below the MPSL's 0. The
receiver is never sent anything: its default sentences carry all the
node reads.

## What the driver does

`bark-driver` tells the application after every call to the core what the
GPS is to do (`bark_driver::Gps`): rest, search until an instant, or
track. It takes `Action::Locate` and `Core::gps_tracks` as
`docs/core-interface.md` §4 says, and ends a search at the first fix it
hands. The firmware's task (`bark-fw/src/gps.rs`) follows it.

From each second's sentences (`bark_driver::gnss`) the node reads RMC's
status, position, date and time, GGA's count of satellites, GSA's
satellites used and GSV's C/N0 of each satellite in view, and keeps of
the C/N0 those of the satellites used: their count, lowest, mean and
highest. A fix is handed to the node if its satellites' C/N0 lies in the
band of a real sky; the node hands it to the core if it is within the
hearing radius of a relay it knows (`Core::fix_in_reach`, spec
"Position"). The band is open item O18: until the bench measures it, it
is the receiver's whole scale, 0 to 99 dB-Hz, and the log gives the
C/N0 of every first fix for the bench.

The log has one `gps:` line at each change: `searching`, `fix at …` with
the satellites and the C/N0, `no fix, searching`, `fix dropped` for the
C/N0, and `rest` (with `without a fix` when a search found nothing). A
fix beyond every known relay is a `gps:` warning from the driver.

## The schedule and what it costs

The schedule is the core's (D80, D82); the cost is the receiver's, from
the figures above, on the 850 mAh cell of spec "Sleepy nodes". On USB
the receiver runs all the time and the cell pays nothing.

| On battery | Receiver | Per day | Of the cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| any node, between searches | standby, 20 µA | 0.48 mAh | 0.06 % |
| sleepy, with an anchor | no search | 0.48 mAh | 0.06 % |
| sleepy, anchorless, under the sky | a hot start, 2 s, once a delivery TTL, 600 s: 144 a day | 2.3 mAh more, 2.8 mAh | 0.33 % |
| sleepy, anchorless, no sky | 60 s, then nothing for 9 h: 2.7 a day | 1.3 mAh more, 1.8 mAh | 0.21 % |
| fixed relay at rest, no sky | 60 s at each sign of a move, at most 0.42 an hour: 10 a day | 4.8 mAh more, 5.3 mAh | 0.62 % |
| fixed relay at rest, under the sky | 2 s at each sign | 0.16 mAh more, 0.64 mAh | 0.08 % |
| a relay just unplugged | runs through the grace, an hour | 29 mAh once | 3.4 % once |

Before this driver the receiver ran at 29 mA whenever the board was on,
696 mAh a day: the whole cell in 29 hours, whatever the radio did. The
nRF52840's UART and its clock, on only while the receiver runs, are not
counted; the bench measures the board's current in each state
(`bark-fw/scripts/measure.md`).

## The time of day

A fix's RMC sentence gives the UTC date and time. The node keeps it on the
embassy clock, RTC1 on the 32.768 kHz clock (`lfclk.rs`), as UTC
seconds and the clock's instant they were true at (`bark_driver::utc`).
The protocol reads none of it (spec "Position"); the display and the
phone do. The screen's last line is the time when the picture was
drawn, `UTC 12:35` or, once a phone gave its offset, the local time and
the offset, `14:35 +02`; with no time, no line. The screen is redrawn
seldom (`bark-fw/README.md`, Display), so the line can be up to its
longest wait old: ten minutes on USB, an hour on battery.

A time is kept while its error can be under a minute, what a clock that
shows minutes needs: the second its source may lag by, a sentence coming
within the second it reports, and the clock's drift since. The tolerance
taken is the one the MPSL is told (`ble.rs`):

| Clock | Tolerance | Drift a day | Keeps the minute for |
|---|---|---|---|
| the crystal, as the firmware takes it | ±250 ppm, the most the nRF52840 Product Specification lets a BLE stack's crystal be | 21.6 s | 236 000 s, 2.7 days |
| the RC oscillator, calibrated | ±500 ppm (Product Specification, LFRC) | 43.2 s | 118 000 s, 1.4 days |
| a watch crystal, as spec "Heartbeat" assumes | ±50 ppm | 4.3 s | 1 180 000 s, 13.7 days |

So a fix every 2.7 days keeps the minute. A relay on USB has one every
second, a resting relay at boot and at each sign of a move, an anchorless
sleepy node once a delivery TTL under the sky; a sleepy node with an
anchor never searches, and its time comes from the phone. Past the bound
the node says it has no time rather than show a wrong one.

## The phone's clock (D130)

The phone writes its UTC time in seconds and its UTC offset in minutes to
a new characteristic, Clock, at every connection (spec "Phone link"). The
node keeps the offset in its flash page beside the core's records
(`bark-fw/src/store.rs`), so a reboot keeps it, and takes the phone's time
only while it has no GPS time that keeps the minute. A read of Clock
gives what the node has: its UTC seconds, its offset and where the time
came from, none, GPS or the phone. The app shows it on its debug screen.

*Why the phone's time only without the GPS's.* A fix gives the second
the satellites keep; a phone's clock is usually set from its network to
the same second, but not always, and the GPS needs no trust in the
phone's settings. Without a GPS time the phone's is the best there is.

*Why the offset from the phone.* The node knows where it is, but turning a
position into a time zone takes the zone boundaries and their rules,
megabytes the node has no room for and which change by law; the phone
has them and the person has set them.

*Why a new link version.* The contract adds characteristics only with the
version (spec "Phone link", D117): Node says 2, and an app writes Clock
only to a node of version 2 or later. An app of version 1 meets a node
of version 2 and asks the person to update it.

## Does it fit

The node image with the driver, the time and Clock, release, built with
`BARK_LAT=50.4501 BARK_LON=30.5234`, `size -A`:

| | before | with the GPS, before dropping the host's log | as built |
|---|---|---|---|
| flash | 351 232 | 363 440, over the slot by 2 992 | 353 540 |
| static RAM | 217 600 | | 218 784 |

The slot is 360 448 B, which leaves 6 908 B. The driver, the parser, the
time and Clock took 12.2 KB; the host's own log lines (trouble-host's
`log` feature) cost 9.9 KB and were dropped to make room
(`docs/phone-link-2026-11-05.md`, "The firmware's side"). Optimising
trouble-host for size alone made the image 576 B larger under the whole
program's link-time optimisation. The position is turned from NMEA to
counts in integers, since the processor has no double-precision unit and
the software's arithmetic would have cost 1.9 KB. Static RAM grows by
the GPS task, 1 184 B, which leaves 42 336 B for the call stack.

On the e-paper status screen, with the time as its last line: flash
358 380 B, 2 068 B left; static RAM 219 008 B, 42 112 B for the call
stack. The deepest stack frames, the largest stack adjustments in the
disassembly (`llvm-objdump`): the main task's 29 312 B, the screen
task's 960 B, the GPS task's 428 B. The GPS runs as a task of its own,
its 256-byte read buffer in that task, so the main task's frame does not
hold it.

## On the bench

Build the bench image without a place, or with the place the boards
stand at: a fix farther than 3 km from every relay the node knows is
dropped, and boards built with a place elsewhere know only relays there
(`bark-fw/README.md`, on the place). A built place is not a map point and
does not stop the GPS; a map point the phone set, stored across reboots,
does, and the `boot:` line's `map_point=true` says so. The boot lines
come before a host has the port open; the first 10 s report's `boot:`
line repeats the GPS's state as `gps=`.

1. **Boot on USB.** `gps: searching`, then within a cold start's 30 s
   under the sky `utc: N s from the gps` and `gps: fix at <degrees> sats=N
   c/n0 n=… min=… mean=… max=… dB-Hz`; indoors a cold start took 87 s
   on the first bench run (2026-11-05). If `n=0` while satellites are
   used, a warning follows once with how many were listed and in view and
   the last GSA and GSV sentences as received, to fix the parser by. The
   10 s line ends in
   `utc_s=… offset_min=… from=Gps`. A board indoors logs no fix; at a
   window, a fix now and then. `bad_sentences` in the `rest` line stays
   near 0; a high count means the rate or the line is wrong.
2. **Standby.** Built with `BARK_ON_BATTERY=1`, a board with an anchor
   logs `gps: rest` and nothing more; the board's current falls by about
   29 mA from what it drew before this driver. If it does not, P1.02's
   polarity is the other way, or a low WAKEUP alone does not enter
   standby, and the driver changes.
3. **Hot start.** A board with a fix, left resting under the sky for an
   hour and then rebooted: the fix within about 2 s of `gps: searching`.
4. **C/N0.** The `gps: fix` lines over a day, open sky, windowsill,
   indoors, give O18's band.
5. **Clock.** With nRF Connect, write to `28bc0008-…` six bytes, the
   time and an offset of 60 (`bark-link`'s test `vectors`): the log shows
   `utc: phone … offset 60 min, time taken` without a GPS time, `kept from
   the gps` with one; a read gives seven bytes, the time, the offset and
   1 (GPS) or 2 (phone). After a reboot a read gives offset 60 again.

## Unsure

- WAKEUP's polarity on this board, and whether a low WAKEUP alone puts the
  receiver in standby or it also needs a command: bench step 2.
- Whether the receiver keeps its orbits through standby so that a search
  starts hot: bench step 3.
- The C/N0 band (O18): until measured, no fix is dropped by it.
- The rate, 9600 bps, is from a secondary source; the sentences parse or
  `bad_sentences` climbs.
