# Fast boot

**Date:** 2026-10-23 and 24, roadmap D73. **Status:** built and measured,
not yet decided. Spec references are to `protocol.md`. The base is main
`5cdcee0` with the simulator's boot metrics (`a1c6f09`, no core change),
run as its own binary on the same scenario files; the branch is
`a576047`.

The question, from the user: a node is silent and takes no traffic for
its whole boot listen. A relay listens 195 s, a worst-case rotation, then
sends its first heartbeat within a slot, and its neighbours learn it one
channel a slot over the next rotation; a sleepy node listens until the
first relay it hears, at most 390 s; the node refuses even its own
person's text while it boots. After a power cut every node is silent for
195 s. Nodes are to take part within seconds.

## Summary

- **Built:** a node heartbeats, sweeps and takes and forwards traffic
  from its boot. A relay's first 13 heartbeats, its **burst**, go 1.2 s
  apart with the anchorless flag; a running relay answers each burst
  heartbeat it hears once, with the ordinary preamble; every answer goes
  within a backoff of what it answers. A node keeps its home channel, the
  place it stands, its carried flag and a fixed sleepy node's anchor
  across a reboot (`Action::Store`, `Config::stored`), in a page of flash
  on the board. The application hears how the first rotation goes
  (`AppEvent::Boot`).
- **A relay planted in S4's settled hour** (12 per seed): its own text
  reaches its destination in 2.9 / 2.3 / 1.9 s at the median instead of
  195.4 / 195.3 / 195.5 s; half the relays in its range hold it after
  13.5 / 13.6 / 14.8 s instead of 274 / 269 / 277 s, all of them after
  263 / 277 / 370 s instead of 439 / 506 / 685 s.
- **A relay restarted with its stored state:** half its relays in range
  hold it with the right channel after 0.5 / 0.4 / 0.6 s, since they kept
  its entry and it comes back on its channel, instead of 262 / 266 /
  277 s; its text arrives in 1.9 / 5.7 / 3.0 s instead of 199 / 196 /
  196 s. Wiped, half hold it after 10–12 s and its text arrives in 3–5 s.
- **A power cut ending for the whole domain** (S4 with every node switched
  on in one second): a relay knows its first neighbour after 1.5 / 1.4 /
  1.1 s instead of 196 s, a sleepy node has its anchor after 5.6 / 5.4 /
  5.4 s instead of 212 / 212 / 211 s; texts sent before the settle time
  are delivered 89.6 / 89.6 / 89.1 % instead of 82.6 / 82.1 / 81.8 %, and
  the airtime per delivered text falls from 2.74 / 3.08 / 3.49 s to 1.95
  / 2.14 / 2.22 s. The bursts are 5 % of the first minute's airtime, and
  92 % of the heartbeats in it arrive, against 94 % in the settled hour.
- **Nothing measured gets worse in S4 as it is:** delivered of the texts
  of the settled hour 94.02 / 94.47 / 94.12 → 94.11 / 94.38 / 93.93 %;
  airtime per delivered text 2.26 / 2.21 / 2.35 → 1.96 / 2.12 / 2.26 s;
  a sleepy node's current 0.630 / 0.634 / 0.640 → 0.629 / 0.636 /
  0.636 mA. `dense-power.toml`: 98.03 / 98.29 / 98.04 → 97.97 / 98.45 /
  98.05 %, 8.96 / 8.96 / 8.76 → 8.73 / 8.54 / 8.45 s a text.
- **Writes of stored state:** 5.3 / 5.5 / 5.4 a day for a sleepy node on
  average in S4's settled hour, at most 9 in that hour (216 a day), and
  none for a relay that stays put; 1.8 a day for a node in
  `dense-power.toml`, where a fifth of the people's nodes switch power. The firmware appends 32-byte records to
  a 4 KiB page and erases it once in 128 writes, so the busiest node
  erases it 1.7 times a day: 10 000 cycles last 16 years.
- **Where this differs from the request,** each measured or argued below: a
  sleepy node that hears no relay still listens two rotations; a sleepy
  node does not burst (a research switch measures it); the anchorless flag
  is on all 13 burst heartbeats and marks them as in no slot; at the end
  of its first rotation a node moves only if its home is not among the
  quietest; the noise floor is not stored, nor a carried sleepy node's
  home and anchor; a node in its first rotation owns no position but its
  own.

## The design, and where it differs from the request

**The burst.** A relay's first 13 slots go a burst gap apart, then every
interval T, the channel stepping on as always. The gap is how late the
answers to one burst heartbeat can come: an answer goes within a
listen-before-talk backoff, 200 ms, may then wait behind its slot as long
as any heartbeat, 992.86 ms (spec "Heartbeat"), and is on the air 11.84
ms: 1204.70 ms (`node::burst_gap`). The next burst heartbeat then goes
when the node is back on its home for them. The first slot is at a random
phase within T of boot, as before, so that nodes switched on together do
not burst in step; the burst ends within T + 12 × 1.2 s ≈ 30 s of boot.
The 13 heartbeats are the node's first rotation, sent sooner; they cost
no airtime a rotation would not.

**The flag on the burst.** The first build set the anchorless flag on a
relay's heartbeat literally, while it knows no relay that holds mail, so
that it would drop at the first answer. That broke the sleepy nodes: a sleepy
node that anchors at a burst heartbeat times its anchor's slots from it,
and the burst is in no slot, so it misses its anchor's next two
heartbeats and checks it (`tests/sleepy.rs` caught it). With the flag on
all 13, as the request has it, the flag says two things a receiver needs:
"my table is new, answer me", and "this heartbeat is in no slot". A
neighbour times a relay's slots only by its heartbeats without the flag,
and a sleepy node whose anchor's last heartbeat heard was a burst one
watches from the next it hears while it chooses, or checks the anchor
(spec "Relay choice", Asleep). A running relay never sets it, even if it
knows nobody: a sleepy node anchored to it could then never time it.

**Answers.** A running relay, not carried and not past its grace on
battery, answers a burst heartbeat it hears once, with the ordinary
preamble, on the burst's channel. A node in its first rotation does not
answer; nodes switched on together learn each other from their bursts.
Every answer, also the long ones to an anchorless sleepy node, now goes
at a random time within a backoff of the heartbeat it answers: every
relay on the channel hears the same heartbeat, and checking the channel
in the same instant they would all find it free and send together. In S4
the answers to bursts are about 35 s of airtime in a run of about
50 000 s.

**A sleepy node does not burst.** The request has a booting sleepy node
burst with the sleepy and anchorless flags. Each running relay that
hears one answers it as it answers an anchorless node, with the long
preamble, 366 ms, at once and again a minute later. `sleepy_burst`, a
research switch, builds it (the burst with the ordinary preamble, since
the node listens through its first rotation). Measured against the same
branch without it (the one-rotation variant below, `s4-boot.toml` and
`s4-mass-boot.toml`, seeds 1 / 2 / 3):

| | without | with `sleepy_burst` |
|---|---|---|
| planted sleepy node, to its anchor, p50 | 19.2 / 12.8 / 8.1 s | 4.3 / 3.4 / 4.2 s |
| planted sleepy node, p90 | 59.8 / 55.9 / 26.1 s | 11.1 / 20.5 / 11.4 s |
| answers' airtime, `s4-boot` | 252 / 261 / 267 s | 7558 / 7694 / 7268 s, 12 % of all |
| delivered in the settled hour, `s4-boot` | 93.82 / 94.00 / 93.96 % | 94.17 / 94.08 / 94.02 % |
| mass boot: delivered before the settle time | 89.5 / 89.5 / 89.3 % | 83.8 / 84.0 / 84.5 % |

In a running network it cuts a sleepy node's wait for an anchor from
about 15 s to 4 s at the cost of 12 % of the run's airtime, most of it
while people switch on; after a power cut, when every relay heartbeats from its boot
anyway and a sleepy node has its anchor in 5.5 s without it, it fills the
first minutes with long answers and makes 9000 texts wait. It stays off.

**A sleepy node that hears no relay listens two rotations.** The request
drops the second rotation, since a relay switched on beside the node is
no longer silent. The first build did so (`ab2af2d`, the "one-rotation
variant" here). In S4 it left more sleepy nodes without an
anchor in the settled hour, 40 / 37 / 58 at 3600 s against 27 / 24 / 35
on main, in longer anchorless spells, and the long beats to and from
them, a sleepy node's copies of its beats on its anchorless neighbours'
channels, 10.2 / 8.0 / 12.1 ks against main's 8.1 / 5.6 / 6.9 ks, and
the long DATA, raised the airtime per delivered text to 2.40 / 2.38 /
2.72 s against main's 2.26 / 2.21 / 2.35 s. The second
rotation had a second reason, which stays: a listening receiver loses
about one heartbeat in ten (roadmap D47), so one rotation misses a lone
relay one time in ten, more at the edge of reach, and two one time in a
hundred; listening finds a relay at no cost to anyone, and anchorless
mode finds it by long beats and long answers. With two (`a576047`), 20 /
28 / 36 nodes at 3600 s, anchorless spells after the first rotation 75 /
88 / 112 against main's 459 / 426 / 435, the copies of beats 5.1 / 5.4 /
7.4 ks, and 1.96 / 2.12 / 2.26 s a text. A sleepy node that hears a relay still ends its first rotation at
once, so this costs nothing where there is one.

**The end of the first rotation.** A node without a stored home chooses
then, as before, but keeps its home if it is within 0.01 of the
quietest: the random boot channel already spreads nodes that hear
nothing, and a move costs the neighbours that learned it in the burst a
rotation of a stale channel. In S4 all nodes' changes of home channel, sleepy nodes' included, fell
from 63 900 to 46 600 a run. A move made then is announced on the old channel as any
other (spec "Channel moves").

**Traffic in the first rotation.** A booting node forwards with the
neighbours it knows. Two rules hold for it: it owns no position but its
own, since its table may not yet hold the relay within 50 m that is
nearer (spec "Owner"), and it holds its own person's text while it knows
no relay, until it hears one or its first rotation ends
(spec "Return to parent"). Without the second, a relay's text offered in its first second
would find no neighbour and be dropped at once, untold (`NoRoute`); a
sleepy node's waited 60 s. Nothing is dropped by age.

**Stored state.** Home channel, the place a fixed node stands, the carried
flag, a fixed sleepy node's anchor (ID and position). Left out, and why:

- time: the board has no clock that runs while it is off; a restored
  carried flag is still since the boot, and a relay on battery begins its
  grace again;
- the noise floor: the sweep takes 13 readings a second and their 10th
  percentile is at the floor within seconds, and kept it would be written
  whenever it crossed a 1 dB step;
- a carried node's place, and a carried sleepy node's anchor and home:
  they change at every fix and every relay it passes. Before `7585e59`
  these were most of the writes, 12–14 a day for a sleepy node on
  average and up to 312.

A relay restarted with its home makes no choice at the end of its first
rotation, so its neighbours, which still list it, reach it at once. A
sleepy node restarted with its anchor takes it at once, beats to it at
once and once more within an interval, and listens a rotation for it as
when it checks its anchor (spec "Relay choice"). The record is 32 bytes,
a version byte and an FNV-1a checksum among them (`bark-proto/src/store.rs`);
a record cut short by a power cut is not taken.

## What was run

`cargo build --release -p bark-sim` at each commit: `sim-base` (`a1c6f09`),
the one-rotation variant (`ab2af2d`, `sim-v2`), the branch (`a576047`,
`sim-v4`, the same core as `7585e59` but the two rotations). Seeds 1, 2
and 3 of each, at most six at once, about 4 GB each.

- `s4-boot.toml`: S4 with, in its settled hour from 2750 s, a dozen
  windowsill relays and a dozen indoor sleepy nodes planted at random
  points (`start`), a dozen windowsill relays restarted with their stored
  state and a dozen wiped, one of each kind every 300 s. The request asked
  for a plant at 600 s; S4's relays switch on until 600 s and its sleepy
  nodes until 1800 s, so a plant then would land among booting nodes, and
  the settled hour is the running network the question is about.
- `s4-mass-boot.toml`: S4 with every node switched on at 0 s, 4500 s.
- `s4-capacity.toml` and `dense-power.toml` as they are.

Times are from the boot, p50 / p90 / largest over the boots of one seed,
and "n of 12" says how many of the boots reached the event. Airtime per
delivered text is the whole run's airtime over every text delivered in
it, boots included, not the settled hour's of D70 to D72.

## Left out, and why

- **The firmware has not run.** `bark-fw/src/store.rs` is built and
  linted against embassy-nrf's NVMC driver; the bench row in roadmap
  Phase 2 says what to check. The erase stops the CPU for up to 85 ms
  once in 128 writes, which the radio's own buffer rides out but the
  driver's "time away" counter will show.
- **No GPS yet in the firmware**, so a fixed node's stored place is the
  build-time position or the map point until the GPS lands; the core
  takes the stored place over `Config::position`.
- **The noise floor** is not stored (above).
- **`sleepy_burst`** stays a research switch, off.

## To check

- The flag on the burst now means "first rotation, in no slot" for a
  relay; the spec's flag text says so (spec "Packet formats"). A relay
  that knows nobody later does not set it.
- `Relay` grew 24 B to 167 032 B: two copies of `Option<Stored>` (handed
  back, and last kept, to emit only changes). The size test now allows
  them on top of the 1 KiB.
- Tests whose timing assumed a silent first rotation were adjusted:
  `tests/routing.rs` waits for the bursts to end before a test starts and
  `fill` waits for the refusal rather than 200 ms; `bark-sim`'s
  determinism test slows its relays' heartbeats to a day so that no stub
  leaf anchors and beats into its scripted frames; `tests/mac.rs`,
  `tests/power.rs`, `tests/carried_sleepy.rs` and `tests/sleepy.rs` say
  the burst and the anchor taken in it. Each says why in place.
- The timing test `capacity_scenario_runs_a_minute_quickly` (bounds 90 s
  debug, 20 s release, unchanged): 39.5 s debug and 14.7 s release at a
  load of 9.5 on the 32-core machine, against 65.5 s debug for main
  alone. Its minute switches every node on at once: the sleepy nodes now
  hear the relays' bursts and anchor in seconds, and stop their boot
  sweeps, 13 readings a second each, so the minute has 6.3 million events
  against main's 47.5 million.
