# The ring jam

Research note, 2026-10-18, roadmap D53. Measured against main `d7d1800`
(D51).

## Summary

The ring scenario (`scenarios/ring.toml`) with its sleepy nodes
listening all the time (`sleepy_listens`, as the ring test runs it) jams
on some seeds: a backlog of a few hundred texts builds up late in the run
and is still in flight at its end. It is not the ring's cap. The ring is
at its cap on every seed, and its relays hold texts back at the origin as
they should; a jam is a holder that stops delivering to a sleepy node that
is in reach and taking messages, so that the messages for that node fill
the delivery queue, are refused, and wait at the relays before it until
those are full too. Two rules made a holder lose a node that was there:

1. **A lapse.** The heard table kept a node for two beat periods after
   its last beat *heard*, and nothing else counted. A relay at its cap
   misses one beat in five, so on half the seeds some holder missed every
   beat of a delivery TTL from a node that was sending it a text and
   taking one every two seconds, and forgot it.
2. **A move.** When a holder moved its home channel, a node listening on
   the old one followed it at once, but the holder kept the old channel
   for the node until its next beat, up to 300 s later, and sent to it
   there.

Every jam of the 120 runs measured (40 seeds, three start times) follows
from one of the two. One seed more falls below the test's bound without a
jam: a relay that hears neither neighbour's heartbeat until 540 s drops
its leaf's texts for want of a route early in the settled phase.

The fix, in the core: the heard table keeps a node for two beat periods
after the last frame heard from it, beat, own DATA or ACK, and takes its
home channel from its DATA; and a node that moves its home takes along
the sleepy nodes of its table that listen at that moment. With both, no
seed of the 120 jams, the ring test's flows start where the scenario
starts them again, and its bound stays 0.98. Neither rule costs a frame.

Both matter only to a node reached outside the windows its beats open:
listening all the time, as the research switch has it, or kept awake by a
flow for longer than a TTL. A sleeping node is reached only after a beat
its holder heard, which renews the entry and names the channel, and the
standard and loaded suites, where sleepy nodes sleep, deliver exactly as
on main, and S4 0.05 points more. A third rule, measured and not built: a sleeping node that
follows its anchor to a new channel beats there at once, so that its
holder can deliver before its next regular beat. That gains 0.5 points
in S4 and up to 1.5 in the loaded rings with sleeping nodes; beating once
at a random time within a heartbeat interval instead gains the same in
S4 for a fifth of the airtime.

## What was found

### The ring is at its cap, and that is not the jam

Each relay carries, per 2 s, its own leaf's text and the texts of the two
leaves behind it on to the next relay, and delivers the text of the leaf
three behind to its leaf: four DATA frames of 62 bytes (a 40-byte
payload and the 22-byte header), 38.72 ms each at SF5, and four ACKs it
sends for what it takes, 14.40 ms each (`radio.rs` `time_on_air`). That is
212.5 ms in 2 s, 10.62 % of the time, and the heartbeats add 0.12 %:
10.74 %, against the 9.83 % the duty cycle bucket refills
(spec "Duty cycle cap"). So even if every hand-over succeeded at the first attempt
the ring could carry 9.83 / 10.74 = 91.5 % of the texts it is offered.

Measured, fixed, flows from 800 s, seeds 1 to 40, from 900 to 1700 s:
the relays transmit 9.95 % of the time on average, 10.51 % at most (the
bucket's 6 s depth over 800 s allows 10.58 %). Each delivered text costs
the relays 284.6 ms of air: 5.52 DATA frames, 4.37 ACKs that take, 0.29
that refuse and 0.31 heartbeats. Retries on shared channels, and texts
that go the long way round after a refusal, are the 72 ms above the
212.5 ms of a first-time path. The ceiling that cost gives is
8 × 9.83 % / 284.6 ms = 2.76 texts a second; the ring delivered 2.80
(main 2.77). Of the 4 texts a second offered, the origins took 72 % and
held the rest back (spec "Addresses"). That is pushback doing its job, on
every seed, and the test's delivery ratio counts only what the origins
took.

A lapse is common and mostly cheap: some holder forgot a node in 26, 22
and 20 of the 40 seeds at the three starts. While it has forgotten it the
holder delivers nothing; the next beat it hears brings the node back, and
the backlog drains if there is time. A long lapse, or one that lasts to
the end of the run, is a jam.

Why a holder misses beats: in the 40 runs from 800 s on main, the holders
heard 1133 of the 1401 beats their nodes sent after 800 s, 81 %; before
the flows began, 99 %. Of the 268 lost, 176 came while the holder was
transmitting, to a neighbour on that neighbour's channel or an ACK, and
the rest met another frame at the holder. Beats come 150 to 300 s apart,
so a delivery TTL holds two to four of them, and at one in five lost some
holder loses all of them on about half the seeds.

Seed 27 at 2 s is the other loss: R7's neighbours' heartbeats came to its
channel at 306, 366 and 460 s, the first while its leaf's DATA was on the
air there and the others while R7 was transmitting, so it knew no
neighbour until 540 s and dropped its leaf's texts at the origin with no
route. It happens with the flows already running as the relays meet, and
neither rule touches it.

## The trace: seed 38, flows from 800 s

Homes after the boot listen: R0 7, R1 12, R2 5, R3 8, R4 12, R5 8, R6 2,
R7 6; each leaf on its relay's. L6 beats at 758 s (heard by R6), 1051 s
(R6 sending an ACK on channel 8), 1274 s (R6 sending a DATA on channel 6)
and 1556 s (R5's DATA to R6 on the air there). L6 sends R6 a text every
2 s throughout and takes one from it every 2 s until 1357 s.

1. 1357.03 s: R6 delivers its last text to L6. At 1358.3 s, 600 s after
   the last beat it heard, R6 forgets L6. With no entry it has nobody to
   send the queue to, and texts for L6 go on arriving from R5, one every
   2 s.
2. By 1441 s the delivery queue holds its 41, and R6 refuses each text
   for L6 (`DeliveryFull`), 70 to 76 a minute, from R5 and from R7, which
   has some coming the long way round. Each refused text waits at its
   sender for the 1.56 s ban and is offered again
   (spec "Overload waits"); none is dropped.
3. R5's send queue fills with texts for L6, so from 1500 s R5 forwards
   nothing and refuses its own neighbours' texts (`SendQueueFull`, 40 to
   75 a minute). Its own deliveries stop too, since they need a place in
   the send queue, and from 1740 s its delivery queue for L5 refuses as
   well. R4 follows, its forwards down from 64 a minute to 2.
4. The origins behind are refused more and more: L3 hands its relay 30
   texts a minute at 1260 s, 8 at 1740 s. At 1800 s 361 taken texts are
   in flight: 148 from L3 to L6, 68 from L2 to L5, 68 from L1 to L4, 52
   from L4 to L7, all held at or before R4, R5 and R6. None was dropped,
   and none was older than its 600 s: had the run gone on, the next beat
   R6 heard from L6 would have started the drain.

The settled phase delivered 0.858 of what it took, and took 2545 texts
where the median seed took 2890.

Seed 39 from 700 s is the move. At 1610.43 s R6 moves its home from
channel 7 to 6 and says so on 7; L6, listening, follows 15 ms later. R6's
heard table still has 7 for L6, so its DATA to L6 go on 7, unanswered,
each marks L6 asleep, and R6 tries again every 60 s (`sleepy_listens`),
again on 7, until L6's next beat, which came after the end of the run.
R6 delivered nothing after 1608 s.

## What was built

In the protocol core (spec "Heard table", "Channel moves"):

- `tables/heard.rs`: an entry's `last_heard` is the last frame heard from
  its node. `took`, its ACK of a delivery, renews it; `sent`, its own
  DATA heard, renews it and sets the node's home from the DATA's `aux`.
  DATA alone does not make an entry: only a beat says a node is sleepy.
  Sending says nothing of whether it listens, so `sent` opens no window.
  `moved` sets a node's home.
- `node/relay.rs`: every DATA heard calls `sent`, beside the neighbour
  table's `home_heard`. A node that moves its home by the busy estimate
  sets the home of each sleepy node of its table that was on the old
  channel and listens now to the new one (`take_listeners`).
- Tests: `tables::heard::tests::any_frame_from_a_node_keeps_it`, and
  `a_holder_that_moves_sends_on_its_new_channel_to_who_listens` in
  `tests/sleepy.rs`, which fails without `take_listeners`.
- The ring test (`bark-sim/tests/scenarios.rs`) starts its flows as the
  scenario does again, and keeps its bound, 0.98 on seeds 1 to 3: 0.994,
  0.999, 0.998.

No wire change, no new constant, no frame added.

The heard table rule alone fixes every jam of the ring, since there every
destination also sends, and its DATA names its new channel. The move rule
matters where a destination only receives. In a ring where only the even
leaves send, each to the leaf three on, every 1 s, sleepy nodes
listening, seeds 1 to 40: main 4 seeds below 0.98 (1, 4, 14, 40; mean
0.9963); the table rule alone 3 (1, 4, 14); both 1 (4; mean 0.9977).
Seeds 1 and 14 are moves: 0.969 and 0.972 on main and with the table rule
alone, 0.981 and 0.997 with both. Seed 4 is the early route failure of
seed 27 above. Seed 1's 0.981 is a relay's own move (below).

## Measured and not built: a beat after following a move

A sleeping node follows its anchor to a new channel at the anchor's next
heartbeat it wakes for, and its holder learns of it at its next regular
beat, 150 to 300 s later; until then the holder has no window to send in.
Two ways to shorten that were measured:

- **F**: the node beats on the new channel at once and once more within a
  heartbeat interval, as after choosing an anchor.
- **G**: it beats there once, at a random time within a heartbeat
  interval, so that the nodes that follow on one heartbeat do not beat
  together.

On the loaded rings with sleeping nodes, against main: F +0.0150 ± 0.0067
(ring, 10 seeds), +0.0105 ± 0.0048 (ring at 2 s, 30), +0.0033 ± 0.0104
(ring at 1 s, 30), +0.0055 ± 0.0035 (burst mesh ×64, 30); G +0.0035 ±
0.0087, +0.0044 ± 0.0049, +0.0034 ± 0.0128, +0.0026 ± 0.0034. In S4 ×1,
seeds 1 and 2, both gain the same, F +0.0054 ± 0.0010 and G +0.0054 ±
0.0004, with the 90th percentile of latency 14 s shorter; F costs 744 s
of airtime a run, +2.0 %, G 158 s, +0.4 %. Every node anchored to a
relay wakes for the same heartbeat, so under F all of them follow and
beat in the same instant, about 74 at a relay in S4, on top of each
other; G spreads them. The F runs carry the heard table rule and the G runs
both rules; the gains are the beat's, since the rules alone change
nothing with sleeping nodes. Not built: it is a different question from
the jam, about latency after a relay moves, and two S4 seeds are too few
to set its cost.

## What it does not show

- It is the simulator's deafness and collisions that lose one beat in five
  at a busy holder; the hardware may lose more or fewer. The rules do not
  depend on the figure.
- The jam needs a node reached outside its beat windows. With sleeping
  nodes a lapse costs nothing measured here; a flow that keeps a node
  awake for longer than a TTL, which the rule also covers, did not occur
  in the suites.
- Two other ways a busy ring loses texts remain, both as the spec
  describes them and neither a jam of this kind. A relay's own move
  reaches the neighbours that send to it only with its next heartbeat on
  their channel, up to a rotation: in the even-leaves ring, seed 1, R6
  could not reach R7 for 72 s after R7 moved, and 79 texts were in flight
  at the end (0.981). And a relay that misses its neighbours' heartbeats
  for a few rotations under load, as R7 in seed 27, has no route until it
  hears one.
- With `sleepy_listens` a node whose delivery went unanswered is tried
  again every 60 s; a retry that finds no room in the send queue waits
  for the next 60 s rather than for room. This touches only that switch
  and was not changed.

## Recommendation

Adopt both rules: they are what the heard table is for, a holder knowing
which nodes it can reach and where, and they cost nothing. Put the ring
test's flows back at the scenario's start: with the fix none of its 40
seeds jams at any start time, and the 800 s start measured nothing about
the ring. Whether a sleeping node should beat after following a move (F
or G) is a separate decision, about latency after relay moves, and wants
S4 over more seeds first.
