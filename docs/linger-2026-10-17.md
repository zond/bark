# A sleepy node listens a hand-over after every message: measured

**Date:** 2026-10-17. Roadmap D48, measured, awaiting the decision.
Branch `research/linger`, based on main `2ace68e` (D47) and measured
against it.

D47 made a sleepy node sleep as soon as it has answered a DATA without
the "more follows" bit, and listen one hand-over after one with it. Flows
of a text every 0.5 to 2 s to one node then lost a quarter to a third of
what D46's 9.25 s window carried: the node slept when its holder's queue
emptied, the next text waited a beat period, and the holder's delivery
queue of 41 overflowed (`docs/sleep-window-2026-10-17.md`, "The window
and 'more follows'"). This note measures the alternative that note left
open: the node listens one hand-over after every DATA it answers, and the
bit goes.

## Summary

- **At the texting rate it costs almost nothing.** In the capacity domain
  of spec "Capacity and sizing", S4 seeds 1 and 2, the network delivers
  92.4 % either way (+0.0 ± 0.2 points), and a sleepy node listens 175 s
  an hour on average against 171 s, 117 s at the median against 106 s:
  0.28 mA on average either way at the 5.8 mA of listening, 0.19 mA
  against 0.17 mA at the median. A node takes 0.8 messages an hour there,
  2.4 s of listening at 3.13 s each.
- **DATA lost to a node asleep halves**, 1 281 and 1 591 to 622 and 968
  a run: a holder's retry after a lost ACK now finds the node listening.
- **Flows to one node recover most of what they lost.** The corridors at
  1 s and 0.5 s deliver 99.1 and 100.0 % against 78.6 and 74.6 %, as the
  9.25 s window did; the rings at 1 s, 2 s and their own rate 76.9, 81.3
  and 83.5 % against 57.4, 67.9 and 68.7 %, half to three fifths of the
  way back to the 9.25 s window's 90 to 94 %. The mesh burst ×64 is
  unchanged, 74.5 against 74.7 %.
- **The standard suites deliver what main does**, S1, S1b, S2, the
  corridor and the meshes alike.
- With nothing left for it to do, the bit goes: the lowest spare bit of
  `dst_pos` is spare again.

## What was built

DATA loses the "more follows" bit, the core its bookkeeping, the simulator its count. |
| `a70ab1b` | The spec says so ("Sleepy nodes", "Delivery", "Delivery queue", "Packet formats"). |

The hand-over is D47's, unchanged: three attempts of 738.56 ms and the
retry waits of 305.92 and 611.84 ms (spec "Hand-over", "Retry wait"). It
is the longest the holder's next message can take to reach the node once
the holder has the ACK, so nothing is under way ahead of it. The node
renews it on every DATA it answers, a repeat included.

`tests/sleepy.rs`: `a_sleepy_node_sleeps_between_its_windows` now checks
that the node listens a hand-over after each answer, and allows that time
in its bound; `a_burst_goes_on_one_beat_and_the_node_sleeps_a_hand_over_after_the_last`
replaces the check of the bit with one that the node listens through the
hand-over after its last answer and sleeps within 50 ms after it.

The firmware image shrinks by 280 bytes of code and stays at 306 KiB of
flash and 170 KiB of RAM (`bark-fw/README.md` unchanged).

## What was run

Two simulators: **H**, main `2ace68e` as merged, and **L**, `26083c0`.
H's runs are those of the D47 note's H4, built from `bbbc4aa`, whose code
main has unchanged; rebuilt from main, three suite runs (ring, S2, the
corridor at 1 s, seed 1) came out byte for byte the same.

The set is D47's: S4 (`scenarios/s4-capacity.toml`) at one and two times
the spec "Capacity and sizing" rate, seeds 1 and 2; the standard and
loaded suites of D42, with the seeds of the D47 note. Delivered counts
reachable texts originated from the settle time to the end less a
delivery TTL. Listening counts the time a sleepy node's radio was not
asleep after the settle time, 900 s in S4; in the suites, whose settle
time comes before most sleepy nodes have chosen an anchor, it includes
the choice and is comparable only between H and L. Current is listening
time over the hour times the 5.8 mA of listening. No more than four S4
runs went at once.

At the rate a sleepy node takes about 0.8 messages an hour, so a

## Flows to one node

The loaded suites send a text every 0.5 to 2 s to one node. M and Oa are
the D47 note's: main `4397e5f` with its sleepy nodes listening, and the
9.25 s window renewed by every message.

A sender offers its next text once its last is taken, so where L carries
more, it also originates more: the corridor at 0.5 s originated 23 874
window texts in L against 9 582 in H. Carried a second is the fairer
measure there; it grows by 60 to 230 %. Latency at the median falls from
400 to 500 s to 90 to 230 s.

| Listening an hour | H | L |
|---|---|---|
| Rings | 496 to 615 s | 701 to 798 s |
| Corridors at 1 s, 0.5 s | 590, 603 s | 1 357, 1 556 s |
| Mesh, burst ×64 | 197 s | 223 s |

The 9.25 s window listened 1 940 to 2 610 s an hour in these scenarios.

**What the rings still lose.** A ring's relays carry their cap (D46), and
deliveries to one node come in bursts: half the gaps between two
deliveries to a node are under 0.12 s, but 1.4 to 1.6 % are longer than a
hand-over (0.8 % in the corridor at 1 s). After such a gap the node
sleeps, a flow of a text a second fills the delivery queue of 41 within
its next beat period, and what is refused waits at the relays behind
until its time runs out: Expired 798 and QueueExpired 729 in the ring
against 1 005 and 1 000 in H. The 9.25 s window bridged most such gaps.

## What this does not show

- Two seeds of S4 at each rate; at twice the rate their spread hides any
  difference between H and L.
- A longer listen after a message, two hand-overs or the 9.25 s window,
  would bridge more of the rings' gaps; it was not measured, and every
  second of it is paid on each message at the texting rate.
- What D47's note lists still holds: no node moves, clocks do not drift,
  the firmware does not sleep its radio yet.

## Recommendation

Adopt L for every node: one rule, no bit, no setting. At the texting rate
of spec "Capacity and sizing" it delivers what main delivers for 4 s more
listening an hour on average and 11 s at the median, 0.28 mA either way,
and it halves the DATA lost to a sleeping node. Flows of a text every
0.5 to 2 s to one node gain 13 to 25 points: the corridors all of what
the 9.25 s window carried, the rings half to three fifths of it. The
rings' remaining loss is that of a node that sleeps between bursts more
than a hand-over apart; a node that must carry such flows whole can be
set not sleepy.
