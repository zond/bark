# The next hop's margin: why a quarter of the city's DATA arrives too weak

**Date:** 2026-11-02. **Status:** diagnosed and measured in the simulator;
two rules prototyped and not adopted. Roadmap D103. Spec references are
to `protocol.md`. Measured on main `c0ec923` with a simulator that also
logs, for every DATA frame, what its sender knew of the receiver when it
chose it and what the link was (`bark-sim/src/margin.rs`, `--margin`).
The log changes no behaviour: the instrumented build reproduces main to
the byte on the ring and on `dense-power.toml` seed 1.

## Summary

- **The question.** `slots-2026-11-02.md` (D101) found that in the dense
  city a quarter of the DATA frames reach their receiver below what it
  can demodulate with nothing else on the air. Would a better reading of
  a link's margin, a better next-hop choice, cut the frames a text costs
  and so widen the headroom before overload?
- **The diagnosis.** The sender almost always chose a neighbour its table
  said had margin (99 % of the frames lost to noise), and the estimate was
  wrong by 5.9 dB on average for those frames. The error is not the fade
  at the frame, which is 0.5 dB of it: it is the average itself, 5.4 dB
  above the link's mean level. For 82 % of the frames lost to noise the
  link's mean level was *below* sensitivity; the sender had heard that
  neighbour only in its strong moments. Three things make the average
  optimistic: a heartbeat near sensitivity is heard only when its fade
  is up; a relay walking outdoors is 14 dB louder than at its stop and
  nearer than it will be; and the rule takes, of the neighbours with
  margin, the one nearest the destination, which is the one whose
  estimate is luckiest. Against that, the sender's channel differing
  from the receiver's (D38's reason for the 4 dB) and the age of the
  estimate matter less: the fade at the frame averages −0.3 dB over all
  frames, and the two channels' fades are uncorrelated as the model
  draws them (0.04). Links are symmetric, and every node's floor is the
  same, so asymmetry and floors contribute nothing.
- **The larger loss is not noise.** 25 % of the relays' DATA reach
  *nobody*: the frame's receiver, or its sender, has walked out of reach
  since the entry was made. 85 % of the dense city's relay-to-relay DATA
  have a node that walks at one end, because a walker is on USB at its
  stops and so a relay, and stays a relay through its walk under the
  hour's grace (spec "Nodes"). Between two nodes that stay put, 61 % of
  the DATA arrive and 0.3 % reach nobody.
- **The bound.** From the log, a threshold of 8 to 10 dB above the floor
  in place of 4.1 would take the chance that a frame is not lost to
  noise from 43 to 58 %, for hops 12 % shorter: 17 % fewer DATA frames
  per text, 9 % fewer frames with the ACKs, 15 % less airtime, in the
  dense city. Choosing the neighbour with the most margin instead takes
  the nearest relay and costs 12 % more.
- **Two rules prototyped, neither adopted.** A threshold derived for the
  spec's own standard, one loss in a hundred, over both processes the
  estimate faces, 9.2 dB in place of 4.1: in the dense city 2 % fewer
  frames per text for twice the hops and 7 % more airtime in all, and
  the losses to noise 26.7 → 24.7 %, because the bar moved and the
  luckiest estimates followed it, 9.8 dB above their links' mean; S4 at
  the design rate 59 % more frames per text, `dense-movers.toml` three
  and a half times as many. A ranking of the candidates with margin by
  the expected progress of one frame: 7 to 8 % fewer frames per text
  and 7 to 8 % less airtime per text in the dense city on three seeds,
  8 % and a point of delivery in the storm at twice the rate, 2 to 3 %
  in S4 and the movers, two texts of a hundred lost on
  `mesh-burst.toml`, the losses to noise unchanged on every run. Below
  the tenth the study was to clear, consistent, and for the reason the
  bound gave: it cannot see past the estimate it is given.
- **Recommendation.** Leave the next-hop rule. The loss to noise is an
  estimate that cannot be trusted near the bar, which any rule reading
  the same estimate inherits, and the larger loss is a neighbour that
  has moved. Two things to study instead: counting a heartbeat that was
  due on our channel and not heard as a reading at the floor, so that
  the average is of what was sent, not of what got through; and a
  carried neighbour (spec "Nodes") as a next hop only while its last
  heartbeat is fresh, or as a last resort, since 25 % of the dense
  city's relay DATA go to a node that has walked out of reach.

## The question

D101 classed every DATA frame addressed to a relay in the dense city by
what the receiver faced when it was lost: 26.6 % of the frames relays
send to relays, and 29.1 % of those from the people's nodes, are lost to
noise alone, the link too weak at that moment with no frame within
7.5 dB of the noise at the receiver. The rule that chose that receiver
(spec "Next hop") asks that its averaged RSSI stand 4.1 dB above the
floor, a full frame's level after a fade of 4 dB
(spec "Neighbour table", roadmap D38), and then takes the candidate
nearest the destination. The user asks what the sender knew when it
chose, whether the loss is the estimate being stale or wrong, the rule
preferring progress, the links being asymmetric or the floors
differing, how many of the lost frames a better choice would have
carried and at what cost in hops, and whether a rule derived from the
radio would pay.

## What the sender knows

A relay's estimate of a neighbour is `rssi_ema`, the average of every
frame heard from it, each new frame counting half
(spec "Neighbour table"). Every frame it hears is on its own home
channel: the neighbour's heartbeats as their rotation passes that
channel, once a rotation (65 to 195 s, mean 130 s), and the neighbour's
ACKs, which come back on the sender's channel. The DATA goes out on
the receiver's home channel. In the simulator a pair's fading is one process per
channel, a Gauss-Markov offset with a standard deviation of 4 dB and a
correlation time of 60 s, independent across channels and shared by
both directions (`bark-sim/src/links.rs`; `dense-power.toml`
`[links.fading]`). So the estimate averages samples of one process, a
rotation apart and so nearly independent, which gives it a variance of
σ²/3 (the weights ½, ¼, ⅛ … squared sum to ⅓; `relay/gps.rs` uses the
same figure), and the frame meets another process entirely, variance
σ². The spec's 4 dB covers one standard deviation of one of them.

The log (`--margin`) records, per DATA to a relay after the settle time:
the sender's entry for the receiver at the moment the frame reaches the
air (its average, its floor, whether it has margin, the entry's age,
demotion, failures); the link's base power both ways, its power at the
receiver on the frame's channel with the fade, the fade the sender
would see on its own channel at the same moment, and the receiver's
floor; the receiver's state; the alternatives the sender had; and the
frame's fate from D101's classing. Rows are classed by what the sender
and the receiver *are* at the time, a relay or sleepy, not by what the
scenario placed them as: in `dense-power.toml` roles follow power, and
nearly all of the DATA that D101 called "from sleepy nodes" is from
people's nodes that are relays on USB at the time. 1 240 326 DATA from
relays to relays in the settled hour of seed 1, and 10 211 from sleepy
nodes to their anchors.

## The diagnosis

Of the 1 240 326 DATA from relays to relays:

| fate | share | of the frames with a link |
|---|---|---|
| received | 32.1 % | 42.6 % |
| lost to noise alone | 25.9 % | 34.4 % |
| no link at the moment: the frame reached nobody | 24.8 % | |
| lost otherwise: a collision, a deaf or busy receiver | 17.3 % | 23.0 % |

**What the sender knew, for the frames lost to noise.** The receiver
was in the table in 99.9 % of them, with margin by the rule in 99.0 %,
demoted in 0.3 %. The sender's estimate stood 6.0 dB above the floor at
the median (p10 4.5, p90 11.0), that is 1.9 dB above the rule; 55 % of
the lost frames went to a neighbour within 2 dB of the rule and 77 %
within 4 dB. The entry was 86 s old at the median, against 55 s for the
frames received. The true power at the receiver was 5.7 dB *below*
sensitivity at the median (p10 −11.4, p90 −2.3).

**Where the error is.** Estimate minus truth, both as packet RSSI, for
the frames lost to noise with a link: 5.85 dB (sd 3.6). Split into the
estimate against the link's mean level and the mean level against the
power at the frame:

| | mean | sd |
|---|---|---|
| estimate − base link | 5.36 dB | 3.8 |
| base link − power at the frame (the fade on the receiver's channel) | 0.49 dB | 0.9 |

The fade did little: for 82 % of the frames lost to noise the base link
itself was below the full frame's level, 4.4 dB below sensitivity at
the median (base −123.4 dBm against −119). Packet RSSI compresses
levels near the floor, so the same split in signal terms is 13.9 =
11.6 + 2.3 dB. The sender believed in a link whose mean cannot carry a
frame.

Over all the frames sent with a link, estimate minus truth is 3.3 dB
(sd 4.9): the estimate against the base link 3.4 dB (sd 4.5), the fade
at the frame −0.3 dB (sd 4.1), and the fade the sender's own channel
shows at the same moment 0.1 dB (sd 4.2), correlated 0.04 with the
frame's. On first attempts alone the optimism is 2.0 dB; the retries,
64 % of the frames, go again to the receiver that failed and inflate it.

Why the average is optimistic, in the order of the evidence:

1. *A heartbeat near sensitivity is heard when its fade is up.* A
   13-byte heartbeat is lost 3.1 dB below a full frame's level half of
   the time (spec "Neighbour table"), and a link whose mean is at or
   below sensitivity delivers it only on the positive excursions of a
   4 dB process; the average is made of those. The margin rule was
   built to answer this (D38) and sets its bar 4 dB up; the survivors'
   average clears that bar for links 4 dB and more below it. Of the
   first attempts to a neighbour within 1 dB above the rule, 37 % went
   over a base link below sensitivity.
2. *Walkers.* A relay walking is outdoors, 14.4 dB louder than at its
   stop in this scenario (`indoor_loss_db`), and it is heard where it
   is, not where it will be. Pairs with a walker at either end are
   85 % of the DATA; of those with a link, 38 % arrive and 38 % are lost
   to noise, against 61 % and 20 % between two nodes that stay put.
3. *The winner's curse.* Of the candidates with margin the rule takes
   the nearest to the destination, which is the farthest from the
   sender and so the faintest; among several equally faint neighbours
   it is the one whose average happens to be highest. Selecting on a
   noisy estimate biases the selected estimate up.

A hand-over costs 2.81 frames on average (442 005 hand-overs). Of the
hand-overs whose first frame was lost to noise, 39 % get a later
attempt through; of those whose first frame met a collision or a deaf
receiver, 78 %; of those whose first frame reached nobody, 0.2 %. A
fade lasts a minute and the three attempts fall inside it
(spec "A sleepy node's own message", why a fade), so a weak choice
costs its three frames.

**By the entry's age.** Received 56 % when the entry is under 15 s old,
43 % at 15 to 60 s, 31 % at 60 to 130 s, 18 % at 130 to 195 s, 7 % at
195 to 390 s. The loss to noise barely moves with age (19 → 26 → 28 →
27 → 22 %); what grows is the frame that reaches nobody (1 → 9 → 24 →
42 → 63 %). Age is the walker's distance, not the fade's.

**The four causes, quantified.** (a) The estimate wrong: 5.4 dB of the
5.9 dB error of a frame lost to noise is the average standing above the
link's mean, 0.5 dB the fade at the frame; stale in time only through
the walkers. (b) The rule preferring progress: it sets the chosen
neighbour's estimate 1.9 dB above the rule at the median, where the
cause in (a) has its full effect; 55 % of the losses are within 2 dB of
the rule. (c) Asymmetry: the base link back minus forward is 0.0 dB at
every percentile; the scenario's nodes transmit at one power and the
fading is reciprocal. (d) Floors: the sender's and the receiver's floor
are −117 dBm in every row, and the physical floor is −116.5 dBm.

**The frames to sleepy nodes' anchors** (10 211): 84 % arrive, 3 % are
lost to noise, 3 % reach nobody. The node listens on the anchor's
channel, so it judges the anchor by the channel the frame will take
(the two fades correlated 0.99), and it took the strongest relay it
heard (spec "Relay choice"): its estimate stands 19 dB above the floor
at the median. Of its few losses to noise, 65 % went to an anchor whose
estimate had fallen below the rule.

## The bound

For each DATA from a relay the log holds, besides the chosen receiver,
the sender's other live, unbanned, undemoted relay entries nearer the
destination than the sender and no nearer than the chosen one (a nearer
one with margin would have been chosen unless tried, set aside or the
parent, which the simulator cannot see): the one with the highest
average, and for thresholds of 6, 8, 10 and 12 dB above the floor the
nearest whose average clears it, each with its link's true power now on
its home channel and whether it is listening there and free. The chance
that a frame at a given power is lost to noise is read from the log's
own frames (0.45 at −120.5 dBm, 0.03 at −118.5, 0 above), the receiver
must be listening and free, and a rule is credited with the progress of
the frames it would land: distance gained times that chance, averaged
over the 1 239 448 frames. Frames per text follow as one DATA per
attempt and one ACK per DATA that lands.

| most margin among those with progress | 61 % | 66.3 % | 58.9 % | 145 | 86 | 1.12 | 1.29 | 1.17 |
| threshold 6 dB, nearest first, the chosen kept if none clears it | 24 % | 52.5 % | 45.7 % | 242 | 110 | 0.86 | 0.92 | 0.88 |
| threshold 8 dB | 37 % | 58.1 % | 50.6 % | 228 | 115 | 0.83 | 0.91 | 0.85 |
| threshold 10 dB | 41 % | 60.2 % | 52.5 % | 218 | 114 | 0.84 | 0.93 | 0.86 |
| threshold 12 dB | 42 % | 60.5 % | 52.9 % | 211 | 112 | 0.86 | 0.95 | 0.88 |

The alternatives' links are what their estimates say, mostly: the
nearest neighbour clearing 8 dB stands 12.0 dB above the floor at the
median by its estimate and 11.3 dB above sensitivity in truth, and a
frame to it is not lost to noise 83 % of the time, against 43 % for
the chosen; but it gains 227 m against the chosen's 255 at the median
of the hops taken, and in 29 % of the frames no candidate clears 8 dB
and the chosen stays. The
neighbour with the most margin is the nearest relay of all, 78 m of
progress at the median: the hops multiply faster than the losses fall.

So the bound in the dense city is about a sixth of the DATA frames, a
tenth of all frames and a seventh of the airtime per text, at a
threshold of 8 to 10 dB, with hops 12 % more. It cannot touch the
quarter of the frames that reach nobody: a walker's entry is as
confident as any.

## The rules

**A threshold for both processes.** The spec's margin covers a fade of
4 dB, one standard deviation of one process, and asks for one loss in a
hundred of the symbols. Covering what the frame faces, the fade on the
receiver's channel and the average's own error, at the same one in a
hundred: 2.33 × 4 dB × 2/√3 = 10.7 dB of signal, so the signal must
average −2.07 + 10.7 = 8.7 dB above the floor and the packet RSSI
9.2 dB. Built as `radio.rs` `fade_budget_db` in the prototype; the rest
of the rule unchanged, a neighbour without margin still the last
resort.

**Expected progress.** D38 left open a ranking by the distance gained
times the chance the link carries a full frame. Built as: within the
tiers the spec orders (margin, then not demoted), the candidates sorted
by (distance of the sender to the destination − distance of the
candidate to it) × P, where P is the normal chance that a signal at the
estimate's level, less the two processes' spread of 4.6 dB, reaches the
full frame's level (`radio.rs` `full_frame_chance`); ties by distance
and ID as before. Nearest-first where links are strong, since P is near
1 for all; reliability-first where they are faint.

stands 9.8 dB above their link's mean (5.4 dB at 4.1 dB): the bar
moved, and the luckiest of the faint entries, the walkers heard

last resort, to a neighbour below the bar, against 0.5 %. In S4, where
the relays stand still and a 4 dB fade is the only error, the bar costs
hops and buys little; in the movers it drives the relays to their cap.

frames, 8 % fewer hand-overs and 8 % fewer failures, from the few
places where a near, strong neighbour outweighed a far, faint one.
The rule cannot see past the estimate it is given.

## What it does not show

- The bench. The 4 dB fade, its 60 s correlation and its independence
  across channels are the simulator's assumptions (D38). On the bench
  the noise floor read 40 dB above the thermal floor and differed by
  10 dB between channels (`bench-2026-10-30.md`); a two-board survey
  over days exists in the firmware (`bark-fw/README.md`, `survey`) but
  its result is not yet in a note. Channels 150 kHz apart within
  1.5 MHz are inside the coherence bandwidth of most urban multipath,
  hundreds of kHz to a few MHz (`simulator-design.md`), so the real
  fades on the sender's and the receiver's channels are likely more
  alike than the model's, which would make the estimate better than
  measured here and the survivor's bias the same.
- The scenario. `dense-power.toml` puts every walker on USB at its
  stops and keeps it a relay through its walk under the hour's grace;
  85 % of the relays' DATA have a walker at one end. A city where a
  carried node is sleepy would lose far fewer frames to nobody and
  somewhat fewer to noise.
- Other seeds of the dense city for the diagnosis; the prototypes were
  measured on seeds 1, 2 and 7.

## Recommendation

Leave the next-hop rule as it is: margin first, then the nearest to the
destination. The bound from the log was a sixth of the DATA frames at
best, a tenth of all frames; the two rules built on it measured 8 %
and lost it elsewhere, because the estimate they read is optimistic by
selection, by 5 dB and more exactly where the choice is made, and no
threshold or weighting of that estimate corrects a bias that moves
with the bar. The frames go missing for two reasons the rule does not
see:

1. **The average is of what got through.** A neighbour at or below
   sensitivity is heard on its good moments and averaged on those. The
   table knows when each neighbour's heartbeat is due on this channel
   (`last_beat`, spec "Heartbeat"); a heartbeat due and not heard, with
   nothing on the air, is a reading at the floor and could enter the
   average as one. That makes the estimate the average of what was
   sent, takes the survivor's bias out of it, and costs no byte on the
   air. Its effect on the 4.1 dB bar, which was set against the same
   bias, is the first thing to measure.
2. **A neighbour that walks.** In this scenario a quarter of the relays'
   DATA reach nobody and another quarter go to a walker's link that
   cannot carry them. A carried relay (spec "Nodes") is a next hop
   like any other; the heartbeats it sends while walking outdoors are
   the loudest its neighbours hear and the first to be stale. A carried
   neighbour as a candidate only while its last heartbeat is younger
   than its interval, or as a last resort, is the bound to measure
   next: it reaches the 25 % no rule of margin can.

Neither is an access rule, and neither is a change to the margin. The
expected-progress order is a one-line change that paid 7 to 8 % on
every dense run and a point of delivery in the storm; it is set aside
rather than refused, to be measured again once the estimate it reads
is of what was sent, when its weighting would mean what it says.

## How it was measured

`bark-sim` built from this branch, release, on main `c0ec923`.
`dense-power.toml` seed 1 with `--margin`, analysed by `analyse.py` in
the scratchpad's `margin/`; the attempt within a hand-over is read from
