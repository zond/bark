# Realism: switch-on, node IDs, clocks, walkers, and a strip of domains

Research note, 2026-10-18, roadmap D52. Measured against main `d7d1800`
(D51). Spec references are to `protocol.md`.

## Summary

Every earlier measurement switched every node on in the same second, kept
every node still, ran every clock true and gave nodes IDs from a fixed
sequence, and nothing larger than one collision domain was simulated.
The simulator now models each of these, each a scenario parameter that
is off by default, and S4 has them all on.

- **Switch-on over a window is the largest effect.** Relays switched on
  over 10 minutes and sleepy nodes over 30 deliver 92.5 % at the spec
  "Capacity and sizing" rate against 91.3 % switched on together, and at
  twice the rate **82.5 % against 31 %**: main at twice the rate
  collapses during its own boot (four seeds, 21 to 35 %) and never
  recovers within the run. Anchorless spells fall from 650 to 470 a run,
  most of them of nodes switched on in the first four minutes, before most
  relays heartbeat; a node switched on among running relays goes without
  an anchor about one time in 300.
- **Random IDs** give relays in reach a shared heartbeat interval at the
  chance rate, 34 and 42 pairs a run against 1 and 2 with the sequence;
  heartbeats lost to another heartbeat stay at 0.30 to 0.32 %, and
  delivery within the seeds' spread. DATA sent to a sleeping node by a
  node that is not its anchor doubles, untraced.
- **Drifting clocks** (±50 ppm) find a flaw in the anchor watch (D47).
  A sleeping node's anchor heartbeats that reach it asleep go from 2.4 %
  to 5.5 %, missed watches from 15.9 to 18.8 %, anchor checks up a
  quarter, and a sleepy node's radio current up 15 %, 0.344 to 0.399 mA.
  The window opens one drift before the time expected, but that time can
  itself be a drift late, after a heartbeat that a busy anchor sent late.
  Opening it three drifts early, 59 ms over a worst-case rotation instead
  of 20, brings misses to 15.0 %, heartbeats reaching the node asleep to
  1.5 %, and the current to 0.333 mA, below the true-clock figure. The
  change is a separate commit for the user to take or leave.
- **Walkers.** With a fifth of the sleepy nodes walking, 3 % of all of
  them in the street at a time, delivery falls 2 points (92.5 → 90.5 %).
  Texts to a node walking when they were sent arrive 59 % of the time,
  to a walker at a stop 87 %, to the rest 92.4 % as before. A walker
  changes anchor 2.7 times as often and draws 0.72 mA against 0.40 mA for
  a node that stays. The check after moving 0.67 r runs 7 400 times a run,
  two per walk.
- **The strip**, three domains with a centre and texts to anyone in it,
  does not carry the spec rate: 26 and 40 % delivered over the settled
  hour, after 84 to 86 % in its first minutes. The middle domain carries
  its own texts, a doubled centre and the transit between the ends; its
  relays are at their duty cap 38 to 47 % of the time against 7 to 14 %
  at the ends, and nearly three quarters of the lost texts die there. At
  half the rate the strip delivers 91 % at every distance and the middle
  relays use 1.4 % of their duty cycle; across the strip, 16 km, a text
  then takes 9 hand-overs at the median.
- **What remains of the regression at twice the rate.** It is not a
  number but a slide. The code of the collapse note (`f1fa059`), run for
  two hours instead of one, delivers 93 to 96 % for an hour and then
  collapses in both seeds, 80, 57 to 66, 29 to 32, 16 to 18 % in
  successive ten minutes; its 94 to 95 % was the hour before its own
  slide. Now, with a realistic
  boot, S4 at twice the rate runs at 79 to 86 % for 20 to 40 minutes and
  then slides, in four seeds of four: 79.6, 52.3, 53.5, 41.4 % over the
  settled hour. Of the plateau's 8 points below the old code's, the
  sleep model (D47 to D50, switched off with `sleepy_listens`) accounts
  for about 6 and the walkers for 2. Run for three hours it swings
  between 35 and 84 % rather than falling to nothing, as the time to live
  of D44 clears the centre. At the spec rate nothing slides.

## What was built

Five models in the simulator, each a scenario parameter that is off by
default, so every other scenario and test runs as before
(`docs/simulator-design.md` §3.7 and "Scenarios in the repository"):

- **Switch-on** (`[boot] relays_s`, `sleepy_s`). Each node is off until a
  time drawn uniformly within its kind's window; off, it hears, sends and
  originates nothing, and its dark spells start at its switch-on. The run
  has settled `settle_s` after the last switch-on: `settle_s` keeps its
  meaning, how long one node takes to settle, 900 s in S4 (a relay's
  first channel move can come 795 s after it is switched on), and the
  last node is switched on at the end of the longer window. S4 switches
  its relays on over 10 minutes and its sleepy nodes over 30: a network
  comes up when a power cut ends or in the morning, relays as their
  district's supply returns or light reaches their panels, people as an
  alert or the morning reaches them. These are the short end of that, the
  hardest boot short of all at once. S4 settles at 2700 s and runs to
  6300 s, one settled hour as before.
- **Random IDs** (`ids = "random"`). Each node's ID is drawn from the
  run's seed, as a chip's factory ID is random; a zero or repeated draw
  is drawn again, since the simulator keys its tables by ID.
- **Clocks** (`[clocks] drift_ppm`). Each node's clock runs off true time
  by a rate drawn uniformly within ±50 ppm, the crystal's tolerance (spec
  "Heartbeat"); uniformly because the tolerance bounds the error and says
  nothing of its spread, and a uniform spread puts more clocks near the
  bound. Each core is handed the time its own clock reads; its wakeups
  and sleeps are taken as the first true microsecond its clock reads them.
- **Walkers** (`[movement]`). A fifth of the sleepy nodes walk: a stop
  indoors of an hour on average, then a walk outdoors at 1.4 m/s, a
  kilometre on average in a straight line, and so on; so 3 % of all
  sleepy nodes are in the street at any moment, about the half hour a
  day of waking time a person spends walking. A walker's links are worked
  out per frame (`walk.rs`): the same path loss and cut-off as stored
  links, shadowing of 6 dB varying over 50 m of its way (3GPP TR 38.901,
  urban, not in line of sight), fading of 4 dB varying over the 60 s
  correlation time at a stop and over 0.21 s, the coherence time at
  walking speed, on the move. Its core gets a GPS fix with each beat (spec
  "Beats"). With the walkers not moving, the per-frame links give S4's
  results within 0.02 points (92.75 and 92.23 % against 92.73 and
  92.25 %).
- **The strip** (`scenarios/strip.toml`). Three S4 domains side by side,
  16 km by 5.3 km, 807 relays and 64 829 sleepy nodes, with a centre of
  2.6 km by 2.6 km in the middle one holding twice the people. The file
  argues density over rate for the centre: what makes a centre hot is
  the people in it by day, while how often a person texts does not depend
  on where they are. A text goes to anyone in the strip, as spec
  "Capacity and sizing" has a text cross the city: two in three leave
  their domain, two in nine go from one end to the other.

New counts in the summary: the windows a sleeping node woke for its
anchor's heartbeat in, heard or not, how early and how long it listened
(`Trace::AnchorWatch`, a new trace of the core that changes nothing it
does); its anchor's heartbeats that reached it asleep; stops, walks and
the checks a fix started; per text its distance when sent and whether its
destination was walking; per node whether it walks.

## What was run

S4 at the spec "Capacity and sizing" rate (×1) and twice it (×2), seeds 1
and 2, each model added in turn to the one before: **M**, main as it is;
**B**, switched on over a window; **I**, B with random IDs; **C**, I with
drifting clocks; **W**, C with walkers, which is S4 as it now is. W also
at three times the rate, at twice it with seeds 3 and 4 (and M with
them), with `sleepy_listens` (**W-L**: sleepy nodes listen all the time and
never sleep, the research switch), and for three settled hours. The code
of the collapse note (`f1fa059`), simultaneous boot, at twice the rate
for two hours (**O**). The anchor watch opening three drifts early (**C3**)
on C's scenario with and without drift. The strip at the spec rate and
at half of it, seeds 1 and 2. Delivered counts reachable texts sent from
the settle time to the end less a delivery TTL, as in the earlier notes:
900 to 3900 s for M and O, 2700 to 5700 s for the others. No more than
eight S4 runs, or two strips and four S4 runs, at once.

An S4 run takes 4 to 6 minutes and 3.8 GB with every model on; a strip
takes 55 minutes and 12.3 GB, nearly all of it the cores, 160 KiB each.

A sleeping node expects its anchor's heartbeat a rotation after the
last it heard, and keeps that time from the earliest heartbeat heard,
moved later only by the drift, since a late heartbeat says only that the
anchor was busy (spec "Relay choice"). The window opens the drift of one
rotation before the time expected. But the time expected is a drift late
whenever the last heartbeat came late, held up by a hand-over, while its
slot was as early as the drift allowed, so the slot can be two drifts
before the time expected and the next heartbeat three drifts before the
window. Two such misses in a row start a check, a rotation of listening.
True clocks show the same flaw at a smaller scale, since the core allows
for drift either way. Opening the window three drifts early costs 20 ms
more per watch and saves more than it costs, in checks avoided; it is
built and measured as its own commit, on which the rest of this note
does not rely.

A text goes to the address its sender knows: the walker's anchor when
the text was sent. A walker out of its anchor's reach takes another
anchor within minutes, by the check after a move or by two missed
watches, but the text waits at the old one, which no longer hears its

(spec "Delivery queue": the sender's own retry covers a node that moved,
and the simulated application does not retry). Walkers listen more: each
check is a rotation, 195 s, and there are two per walk.

What this says of the twice-the-rate regression, from 94–95 % in the
collapse note to 79–86 % in D49 and D50 and 21–35 % on main now:

1. **None of those numbers is the domain's steady state.** At twice the
   rate the domain slides into collapse after 20 to 60 minutes of full
   load, in every run of every version measured here; a one-hour
   measurement gives whatever share of its hour came before the slide.
   The collapse note's code slid too, an hour in, just after its
   measurement ended. Twice the spec rate is beyond what the domain
   carries for long, before D46 as now.
2. **The boot surge decides main's number.** Switched on together, main
   collapses in its boot at twice the rate and does not recover (four
   seeds, 21 to 35 %). Switched on over a window it runs at 79 to 89 %
   before it slides. D49's earlier start and D51's anchorless nodes in
   the boot make the surge worse; with a realistic boot neither shows:
   D51's long frames are 7 % of the airtime at twice the rate, from the
   nodes switched on before the relays.
3. **Before the slide, the sleep model costs about 6 points and the
   walkers 2.** W-L, the same network with sleepy nodes listening, holds
   90 to 92 % where W holds 79 to 86 %, and slides later; at the spec
   rate the sleep model costs 2.7 points (93.2 against 90.5 %). W-L still
   has the walkers, which cost 2 points at the spec rate; that accounts
   for the rest of the gap to the old code's 93 to 94 %.

## Adopted rules under a realistic boot

- **D51, a two-rotation boot listen and anchorless mode.** Its cost in
  S4, 1.3 points, and in S2, 2.0, came from the simultaneous boot. With
  relays switched on over 10 minutes, the nodes that miss every relay are
  those switched on before most relays heartbeat; a node switched on
  among running relays is without an anchor one time in 300. The rule is
  not wrong; the window chosen here makes the first four minutes look
  like the old boot for the nodes switched on in them.
- **D49, the first relay heard at once.** Its costs in the rings and
  corridors, and the S4 ×2 seed at 47 %, came from texts starting 200 s
  sooner in a simultaneous boot. With a realistic boot S4 shows no such
  seed before the slide; the slide itself is not tied to the boot. Not
  separable further without a switch for it, which the code does not have.
- **D47, the anchor watch, is wrong with drifting clocks**, as above: its
  window opens too late by up to two drifts. The fix is a separate commit.

## What this does not show

- The windows and the share of walkers are argued, not measured. A
  longer switch-on window, or relays running long before people switch
  on, only makes the boot easier; the 10 and 30 minutes are its hard end.
- Walkers walk in straight lines through buildings, at one speed, and
  stop indoors; vehicles are not modelled. Their links' shadowing and
  fading are a model of the stored links' model. The simulated
  application never retries a text, so what a walker misses is lost;
  a real sender may send again.
- Clock rates are fixed for a run; temperature moves a watch crystal by
  tens of ppm over a day. The radio's own duty-cycle timer, which sets the
  anchorless receive windows, keeps true time here.
- Two sleepy nodes never share an ID: the simulator draws again, where
  one run in twenty of a domain would have a pair.
- Why random IDs double the DATA sent to sleeping nodes by nodes that are
  not their anchor was not traced.
- The slide at twice the rate is seen and timed, not explained: its onset
  varies from 20 to 60 minutes with the seed, and what turns a stable hour
  into a collapse is the collapse question of D44 and D45. Four seeds at
  twice the rate, two elsewhere; two strips at each rate.
- The strip has three domains, not the city's fourteen, and one centre.
  Traffic from outside the strip, and transit through it, is not
  modelled.

## Recommendation

Keep the five models; S4 now runs with them, and earlier results stand
for what they measured. Take the anchor watch's wider window: it removes
a flaw that drifting clocks expose, and lowers a sleepy node's current
below the true-clock figure. Stop reading one-hour runs at twice the rate
as a capacity figure: the domain slides from about 85 % into collapse
within an hour of full load in every version, so the question for twice
the rate is the collapse question, not a regression of D46 to D51, of
which the sleep model costs about 6 points before the slide. The
strip says the spec's 23 texts a day are not carried when texts cross the
city through a centre: the middle domain, carrying its own traffic, a
denser centre and the transit, binds at its duty cap within half an hour.
Either "Capacity and sizing" should count transit through the centre, or
texts are mostly local, which the scenarios have assumed without saying.
