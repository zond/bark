# The dense-power storm

**Date:** 2026-10-30. **Status:** measured; a decision candidate at the
end. Spec references are to `protocol.md`. Measured on main `1bce3d6`
(D86, D87), on its ancestor `d054cea` (the bench's radio costs alone),
on main `da979a9` (the D77 merge), on main `82e0249` and, at seed 7,
on main `7fff055` (another agent's run); all release builds of
`scenarios/dense-power.toml` at seed 1 unless a table says otherwise.

## Summary

- **The storm is a chain reaction of split copies.** A hand-over that
  fails after three unanswered transmissions, or that gives up on a busy
  channel on a retry, leaves the message with a neighbour that took it
  while the sender carries it on to another: two searchers. Each copy
  that reaches a relay that has already handled the message reopens the
  search there (spec "Receiving DATA" case 5), so every split starts a
  depth-first search again, and each search splits in its turn. Above
  an edge of channel load the copies breed faster than the delivery
  TTL and the hop cap end them: one text reached 2 905 holders and
  97 566 DATA frames.
- **It is reachable on main by seed alone and by cost alone.** Main at
  the old costs storms on seed 7 (91.9 % in the settled hour, airtime
  1 004 612 s) and on seed 1 at the measured costs (78.9 %, 2 027 573
  s); main `da979a9`, the D77 merge, never stormed at seed 1, so D77's
  storm was its branch's timing and main's history carried no fix.
- **What starts it** at the measured costs, seed 1: a calm network at 5
  to 6 % busy with 2 800 hand-overs a minute and four in ten failing
  already, tips at minute 43; busy doubles every two minutes to 50 %
  by minute 50 while the texts offered stay at 300 a minute. Nothing
  external changes; ten texts whose searches wandered, 40 000 to 97 000
  frames each, carry the first minutes.
- **What sustains it:** 41.8 % of hand-overs fail after three
  transmissions; 803 462 of those receivers had ACKed, 245 277 more
  forwarded the message without any ACK ever sent, since an owed ACK is
  dropped when its backoff passes the sender's deadline; 36 % of the
  failures demote the neighbour and move the message to a second one at
  once; case 5 reopened 3.06 M searches in the hour against 442 031
  copies merged.
- **Two of D77's telltales read wrong:** "frames to sleepy nodes" counts
  frames to every node placed as a person's, sleepy or on USB; and the
  move rule, the cause of the 2026-10-18 collapses, moves relays only
  24 % more here.
- **Candidate:** absorb copies; a take is final only when its ACK has
  gone out, the ACK sent without listen-before-talk; an attempt is a
  transmission; a wait is never idle. Each measured alone first.

## The question

Roadmap D77 found `dense-power.toml` seed 1 in a forwarding storm on
its branch and not on the main of that day: 97.97 → 90.95 % delivered,
the run's airtime 272 306 → 1 006 409 s. Seeds 2 to 7 did not storm on
either build, and nobody knew what started the storm or what kept it
going. By 2026-10-30 main at `82e0249` no longer stormed at seed 1
(96.5 %), so some commit among the 66 after the merge had ended it, also
for no known reason. On the same day the simulator took the radio's
costs as the bench measured them (D86, D87), and seed 1 stormed again on
every build with those costs, main included: 78.9 % at the old sweep
rate, 80.9 % at D86's, 2 030 000 s of airtime against 295 000
(`docs/busy-rate-2026-10-30.md`).

Three questions, then: what starts the storm, what sustains it, and
whether the commit that ended D77's storm removed a cause or only moved
the timing of one seed. The last is answered before it is asked: main storms today,
so it was the timing.

## How it was measured

Every run is `bark-sim scenarios/dense-power.toml --seed 1`, which is
deterministic by seed, with `--json`, `--messages`, `--csv` (the
observers' busy fraction and the frames on the air each second),
`--series` (every relay's counters and load each minute) and
`--message-minutes` where the text says so. One run of the storm at
`d054cea` was traced from the settle time with a scratch copy of the
tracer that keeps only DATA and ACK transmissions and the routing
events (forwarded, returned, dropped, refused, queued, delivered): the
full trace would have run to hundreds of millions of lines.

The numbers of a run are the JSON summary's: delivered of all texts
sent, and of those sent in the settled hour; airtime by frame kind over
the channels; the cores' counters summed over nodes (hand-overs taken,
failed, failed with the receiver having the message, copies taken,
merged, held back by the bucket, neighbours demoted); drops by reason;
and each relay's load samples at its duty cap.

Two of the summary's figures do not mean what D77's row took them to
mean, and the note corrects them before using them:

- **"DATA to sleepy nodes"** counts DATA addressed to any node the
  scenario placed as a person's node (`bark-sim/src/radio.rs`,
  `sleepy_index`), whether it is sleepy at the time or a relay on USB:
  a quarter of the people's nodes are relays throughout, and a fifth
  switch. It rises with DATA as a whole and says nothing about sleepy
  nodes. The nodes that were sleepy at some time in the run are told
  apart below by their duty-cycled receive time instead.
- **"Frames to sleepy nodes ×9"** in D77's row is this counter.

## What sustains it

From the trace of the settled hour, 2700 to 6300 s: 22 914 679 DATA and
7 666 523 ACK transmissions. A hand-over is one sender's consecutive
DATA transmissions of one message to one neighbour at one hop count,
and it ends with the sender's own trace, or, with none, when the sender
next transmits the message to another neighbour.

| how the hand-over ended | count | share | receiver had transmitted an ACK that takes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| forwarded (the ACK came) | 5 848 046 | 55.0 % | 5 828 253 |
| failed, three transmissions unanswered | 4 446 484 | 41.8 % | 803 462 |
| gave up on the channel after 1 or 2 transmissions (next hop chosen, no failure counted) | 234 900 | 2.2 % | 57 755 |
| refused | 13 899 | 0.1 % | 5 |

Forwarded on the first transmission 3 411 351, on the second 1 618 432,
on the third 818 253. Of 22.9 M DATA transmissions 15 323 004 (67 %)
had no ACK transmitted at all, 7 078 378 an ACK in time, 497 229 an
ACK after the sender's 100 ms timeout (479 911 of them within 50 ms of
it), 16 068 a refusal.

Among the hand-overs that failed, the receiver transmitted DATA of the
message within 3 s, so it held it: 269 288 after an ACK the sender did
not hear and **245 277 without any ACK having been transmitted**; among
the give-ups 18 050 and 4 431. Of the failures, 1 594 862 (36 %)
demoted the neighbour at the failure, 223 215 of them after the
receiver had ACKed; a demoted neighbour sends the message to the next
candidate at once (spec "Hand-over" step 6), the rest wait for the ban
and offer it to the same neighbour again.

The copies: 3 060 986 DATA taken for a message the node had a record of
and did not hold (case 5), which reopen the search there, against
442 031 merged with a copy held (case 4), which end; 680 211 of the
3.5 M were returns from a child. Over the hour 18 479 texts were
transmitted; the most nodes transmitting one text within one 30 s
window was 990, the top ten 494 to 990, the median text 21; the nodes
that ever transmitted a text, at most 2 905, median 71; 13 631 texts
had more than 10 holders at once and 1 961 more than 100. On main at
the old costs the same seed's settled hour has a median text of 51
frames and its largest 5 655, so a text of a few thousand frames
exists on a healthy main too; the storm is that tail grown until the
TTL binds.

The give-ups on the channel, from the second run with a give-up trace
(2700 to 3700 s): 90 877 on a first attempt with nothing sent, 52 787
on a retry after one unanswered transmission, 10 561 of those with the
receiver having ACKed, 35 084 after two, 10 363 with an ACK; 57 not
forwards. Those 1 000 s are the storm's hottest; over the hour the
reconstruction above finds 234 900 give-ups after a transmission
against 1.6 M demotions and 4.4 M failures: the rule's give-up moves a
message its neighbour may hold, but it is a part, not the bulk.

What this adds up to: a hand-over fails 42 % of the time, a failure
leaves the receiver holding the message about one time in four
(803 462 ACKed plus at least 245 277 silent, of 4.45 M), and each such
copy searches on, costing hand-overs that fail at the same rate. The
copies end at the TTL, 600 s, or the hop cap, 255, both sized for one
search, and above the edge the copies breed faster than those end:
209 396 copies expired and 35 448 hit the cap in the hour, against 544
and 4 428 on main.

## The storm in D77's history

Main at `da979a9`, the D77 merge, does not storm at seed 1: it
delivers 96.5 % with 294 762 s of airtime, byte for byte the run of
main `82e0249` and of today's main at the old costs (26 613 texts,
25 671 delivered, 374 dropped, 568 in flight, p50 10.8 s). So nothing in
the 66 commits after the merge ended the storm; it was never in main's
history. D77's row measured its branch before the rebase onto the
loader and the GPS schedule (D79, D80), against main `2ee8b2d`, and
that branch is gone. The nearest thing that can be built is `2ee8b2d`
with D77's five code commits cherry-picked onto it; that build and `2ee8b2d` itself were prepared and not
run, since the question they answer is closed by the paragraph above.

What the history does show: the battery, GPS and relay-GPS decisions
between the merge and `82e0249` (D81, D82) leave this scenario's run
unchanged to the last frame, since it sets no battery levels and the
GPS changes alter no radio event in it; and the two mains differ from
D77's day only by D80's GPS schedule and the version byte, which
together move seed 1 from 97.97 % to 96.5 %. A run that storms and one
that does not are the same code at the same rate: seed 1 is a seed on
the edge, and the margin to the edge is what D86's costs removed.

## What this does not show

- The spread over seeds at the measured costs: one seed was run. The D86
  note's seeds 2 and 3 of S4 at three times the rate fell as seed 1 did,
  and the D85 branch's seed 2 of this scenario at the old costs already
  shows the beginning (airtime 384 000 against 295 000 s, 243 relays
  ever at their cap, waits told up to 18 bans), so the edge is near on
  other seeds too; where it lies was not measured.
- The first-attempt give-ups: they are counted (90 877 in the second run's 1 000 s)
  but not what each led to, since nothing was sent and the trace shows
  only the next hop chosen.
- Which attempts were not received and which were received and not
  answered: the trace kept transmissions, not receptions. The summary's
  own count of frames addressed to a node that is not sleepy gives the
  split for the whole run: of the DATA from such nodes, 42 % were
  received, 26 % lost while the receiver listened and was free (weak or
  collided), 16 % while it received another frame, 15 % while it was
  deaf (checking, transmitting, retuning), 1 % while it was on another
  channel; on main 47, 38, 9, 5 and 2 %.
- The D77 branch's own storm, which cannot be rebuilt from main's
  history (above).
- Any of the candidate's parts: nothing was built or measured; the
  numbers say what each part would touch, not what it would deliver.

## Decision candidate

One decision in four parts, ordered by leverage, each a rule of spec
"Receiving DATA", "Hand-over" or "The ACK" with its reason. Each part
is to be measured alone on this seed before they are measured together,
since the first may end the storm by itself. Nothing below was built.

**1. A copy is absorbed.** A relay that has already handled a message
and receives another copy of it from a node it did not hand it to ACKs
the copy and drops it. A return from a child, a node in the record's
`handed` set, is handled as today (spec "Receiving DATA" case 5): the
relay goes on with its search from its record.

*Why.* Case 5 today reopens the search for every copy, not only for
returns: the relay takes it and goes on to its next candidate, so the
two searchers of a split, and then their splits, each run the same
depth-first search again from every relay they reach. In the hour 3 060 986 copies reopened a search and 442 031 merged.
Absorbed, a copy can go only to relays that have never seen the
message, so a text can have at most as many holders as relays its first
copy has not reached, and the chain ends where the search has been. The
risk to measure: a split whose first copy died on a dead link leaves
its second copy unable to re-enter the relays the first had crossed, so
some searches that finish today would end early. Whether that costs
delivery at the design rate is the measurement. Measured 2026-10-31
as roadmap D89: `docs/storm-absorb-2026-10-31.md`.

**2. A take is final only when its ACK has gone out, and the ACK goes
out without a listen-before-talk.** A node that takes or refuses a DATA
answers at once on the sender's channel, without the CAD and the
reading: the sender is listening for it and nothing else is expected on
its channel in the 100 ms it waits. An owed ACK is never dropped for a
deadline; if a deadline still exists, a receiver that could not answer
must not keep the message as taken, so that the sender's retry takes it
afresh and there is one holder.

*Why.* This is the receiver's side of one principle: being unable to
send is not the same as having answered. Today the receiver takes the
packet on reception, into its record and its queue, then waits for a
clear channel to answer, backs off, and `Replies::checked`
(`bark-proto/src/node/transfer.rs`) drops the reply when the backoff
would pass the sender's timeout while the message stays taken: a split
that no sender's rule can prevent, since the sender hears nothing
either way. In the settled hour of the trace,
245 277 hand-overs that failed after three unanswered attempts
had a receiver that forwarded the message within 3 s without any ACK
having gone out, a lower bound, and 497 229 ACKs went out after
the sender's timeout, most within 50 ms of it. An ACK sent without
listening collides with whatever is on the sender's channel; that is
the cost, and the listen-before-talk the ACK does today only moves the
collision to its own backoff.

**3. An attempt is a transmission.** The three attempts of a hand-over
are three frames that went out unanswered. The sender keeps listening
before talking, with its backoff, until the frame goes out, then waits
for the ACK; a channel that stays busy never moves the message to
another neighbour by itself. On a first attempt nothing has been sent,
so the give-up may choose the next hop as today. On a retry the
neighbour may hold the message, so the give-up parks this hand-over,
with its neighbour, its attempt count and its timer, and the relay
serves the next packet of its send queue; it comes back to the same
neighbour when the wait ends.

*Why.* The sender's side of the same principle: a busy channel and a
missing ACK are two different facts, and spec "Hand-over" step 2 treats
them alike, so a retry's give-up after a lost ACK is a split made by
the rule itself, exactly when the channel is fullest. The three
attempts still bound a dead neighbour, since parking counts no attempt
and the ban's ladder still runs on the transmissions that go
unanswered; the park is bounded by part 4, so that a channel an
interferer or a crowd holds for seconds does not stall the relay's
whole queue. In the trace the give-up fired 90 877 times on a first attempt
and 87 871 times on a retry in 1 000 s, 20 924 of the retries with the
receiver having ACKed: the retry half of the rule addresses those, the
first-attempt half stays as it is.

**4. A wait is never idle.** Whenever a hand-over waits, on a busy
channel or in its retry wait after an unanswered transmission, the
radio serves the next packet in the send queue whose neighbour is on
another channel; the waiting hand-over keeps its state and resumes when
its wait ends.

*Why.* A relay at its cap in the storm spends 15.1 % (543.8 s of the hour) of its
time between a hand-over's attempts, in retry waits and backoffs, with
its radio idle while its queue holds hop places, returns and deliveries
for other channels. At the cap the bucket binds, not the radio, so this
part recovers queue time and latency rather than airtime; but a queue
that drains sooner holds copies for less time. Two risks the simulator
must measure: a late ACK missed while the sender serves another
hand-over on another channel, which today's rule catches during the
retry wait (`transfer.rs`, "late, during the retry backoff: still the
receiver's ACK"); and the tail latency of a packet to a channel that
stays busy, which no longer blocks the queue but may wait behind it.

**What the parts do not reach.** Most splits in the trace are a
hand-over that fails after three transmissions although the receiver
sent an ACK the sender did not hear, 18 % of the failed
hand-overs, after which the message goes to a second neighbour at once
where the failure demoted the first (36 % of the failures).
These are ACKs lost on the sender's home channel to the storm's own
frames; parts 2 to 4 reduce them only by reducing the frames, and part 1
ends the copies they make.

**Considered and not adopted.**

- *More attempts per hand-over.* 67 % of the DATA transmissions in the
  trace never had an ACK transmitted, so the DATA did not reach its
  receiver (the summary's count of frames addressed to a node that is
  not sleepy: 42 % received, 26 % lost while the receiver listened and
  was free, 16 % while it received another frame, 15 % while it was
  deaf); more attempts add frames to a channel that is already losing
  them.
- *Records kept longer.* No record was lost in the run
  (`records_lost` 0 on main and in the storm); the records already
  outlive the storm by far, and a lost record is not what splits a
  message.
