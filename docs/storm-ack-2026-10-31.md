# A take is final when its ACK has gone out

**Date:** 2026-10-31, the measurements; the write-up finished
2026-11-02. **Status:** measured; roadmap D93 and D94, the user
decides. Spec references are to `protocol.md`. Part 2 of the four-part
candidate of `docs/storm-2026-10-30.md`, built on main `09b7d73`,
where D90 is adopted, and measured against it; all release builds, the
same scenario files and seeds, one binary for every form, which with
its switches off reproduces main's ring run to the byte.

## Summary

- **The forms.** A: a relay acts on a DATA it answers taken only once
  the ACK has left the antenna, and the ACK goes at once, without
  listening first. B1: the take at the ACK alone, the ACK listening
  first as today. B2: the sender's listen-before-talk put to the
  receiver as a probe it answers, the frame following the answer. A+B2:
  both.
- **Today's main does not storm.** `dense-power.toml` seed 1 on
  `09b7d73`, with D90, D91 and D92, delivers 97.8 % of the settled hour
  at 299 754 s of airtime, and seed 7 98.0 % at 305 040 s; the D90
  note's 89.3 % at 1 162 844 s was on a base before D91 and D92. No form
  is judged here by ending a storm.
- **A is worth most where it matters.** At the design rate every form
  delivers the same: S4 93.4 → 93.6 %, the dense city 97.8 → 97.8 %,
  the walkers 96.4 → 96.5 %. At three times the rate A delivers
  63.6 / 61.1 / 77.5 % against main's 51.3 / 52.1 / 48.8 % on seeds 1 to
  3, with 11 / 10 / 23 % less airtime and the median latency 158 / 162 /
  197 → 83 / 94 / 32 s; on the dense city it spends 6 to 11 % less
  airtime for the same delivery. B1, the take at the ACK with the ACK
  still listening first, gets about half of that: 57.0 / 55.8 / 64.8 %.
- **The probe halves delivery under load.** B2 delivers 26.6 / 26.1 /
  25.2 % at three times the rate, with 10 % more airtime, a third of it
  probes and answers, and hand-overs held back by the bucket twenty
  times main's: at the cap the bucket binds, not the channel, and every
  probe and every answer is paid from it. On the dense city, where the
  channel binds, B2 spends a quarter less airtime than main for the
  same delivery, 231 861 against 299 754 s, with a third of main's
  DATA frames and no relay ever at its cap; A+B2 the same.
- **The split rate falls under A.** Hand-overs that failed with the
  receiver holding a record of the message: 125 885 → 108 676 on the
  dense city, 44 738 → 28 661 at three times the rate. Under B1 the
  packets left untaken because the ACK never found the channel, which
  main takes silently, are 4 299 in the dense city's hour and 14 557 to
  20 252 under load; under A they are 20 to 500, for budget alone.
- **Recommendation: adopt A**, both halves. The ACK at once is what
  pays; the take at the ACK is the principle that makes an unsent ACK
  harmless, and costs nothing. B2 is not adopted as stated; what it
  showed about the frames nobody receives is in "What it revealed".

## What today's code does

A relay that receives a DATA addressed to it decides at once whether it
takes or refuses the packet (spec "Receiving DATA"), owes the ACK, and
then acts: a packet with work goes into its send queue or its delivery
queue and its record is made or updated, before the ACK has gone out
(`bark-proto/src/node/relay/forward.rs`, `data`). The ACK goes when the
radio is next free, first of the jobs (`relay.rs` `JOBS`): the relay
retunes to the sender's channel, listens before talking, and if the
channel is busy waits a random 0 to 50 ms and listens again
(spec "The ACK"; `transfer.rs` `Replies::checked`). It owes one reply at a time
(roadmap D29), and a DATA that arrives while one is owed is neither
taken nor refused.

The ACK can fail to go out in three ways, and in each the packet stays
taken:

- **The channel stays busy.** When a backoff would end after the
  sender's timeout, 100 ms, the reply is dropped (`Replies::checked`,
  `forget_late`): the sender hears nothing, retries up to twice, and
  the retransmissions are answered as ACK only (case 3), each with the
  same chance of the same fate. After the third silence the sender
  counts a failure and bans the receiver; if that failure demotes it,
  the message goes to the next candidate at once, and the receiver's
  copy searches on beside it. This is the silent split: in the storm's
  settled hour 245 277 failed hand-overs had a receiver that sent the
  message on within 3 s without any ACK having gone out
  (`docs/storm-2026-10-30.md`, "What sustains it").
- **The budget is spent.** A reply needs the heartbeat reserve left in
  the bucket (spec "Duty cycle cap"). The relay checks that before it
  owes the ACK, and a packet it cannot answer is not taken (`data`, the
  `Budget` refusal with no reply); but the check is made again when the
  reply starts and when its check clears (`start_reply`,
  `reply_check`), and a reply abandoned there leaves the packet taken.
  Nothing spends the budget between the two but a held check of the
  heartbeat (core-interface §3 rule 8), so this is rare.
- **The reply is late.** An ACK that goes out after the sender's
  timeout but before its retry still answers the hand-over
  (`transfer.rs`, "late, during the retry backoff"); one that goes out
  after the retry's frame is answered by the retransmission's ACK, so
  the late one costs the sender a frame, not a split. 497 229 ACKs in
  the storm's hour went out after the timeout, 479 911 of them within
  50 ms of it.

## The forms

Four forms, each a switch in `Config` so that one simulator binary runs
them all from a scenario's `[protocol]` section; with every switch off
the binary is main. Separate branches were not needed: the forms do not
touch each other's code, and one binary checked against main's output
to the byte is a surer base for a comparison than four builds.

**A. The take is final when the ACK has gone out, and the ACK goes at
once** (`take_at_ack` on, `ack_listens_first` off). The relay decides
as before, and owes the ACK carrying the DATA; it acts on the packet,
into the send queue, the delivery queue and its record, when the ACK's
transmission completes (`forward.rs` `taken`). An ACK that cannot go
out leaves the packet untaken, reported as refused for `Unanswered`,
and the sender's retry brings it again, as a new packet. The ACK is
transmitted without listening first: the sender is listening for it on
its home channel and expects nothing else there for the 100 ms it
waits, and the listen-before-talk only moved the collision to the
reply's own backoff (`docs/storm-2026-10-30.md`, part 2). A packet that
is no work, cases 1, 3, 4 and 6, is acted on at reception as before:
nothing is lost if its ACK never goes.

What each half removes: the take at the ACK removes the first and
second ways above, since a reply that lapses or is abandoned takes
nothing with it; the ACK at once removes the first way's cause and the
third, since a reply that never waits for the channel is never late
and never lapses, and leaves only the budget, which the relay checks
before it owes the reply. So in form A the take at the ACK is a
principle with almost no occasion in the simulator; its occasions are
in form B1.

**B1. The take is final when the ACK has gone out, and the ACK listens
first as today** (`take_at_ack` on, `ack_listens_first` on). The same
as A but the ACK keeps its listen-before-talk, so the first way above
happens as often as on main and ends differently: the copy is not
taken, and the sender's retry carries on. The count of packets left
untaken under B1 is the count of silent takes main makes.

**B2. The sender's listen-before-talk is put to the receiver as a
probe, which it answers** (`probe`). The form of "a listen-before-talk
on the home channel answered by the listening receiver" (roadmap, the
bench list), derived here since no document fixed it:

1. The sender checks the receiver's home channel as today (step 2 of
   spec "Hand-over": the CAD with the reading, a random 0 to 50 ms
   backoff if busy, the give-up after 500 ms).
2. Clear, it transmits a probe there, 14 bytes, 15.7 ms: the kind, the
   format, the receiver's ID and its own; and listens on that channel
   for the answer, 18.5 ms, the answer's airtime and one turn of each
   radio from listening to transmitting and back, 1.42 ms each at most
   (spec "Heartbeat", the transmission's preparation and start), with
   no retune and no listen-before-talk on either side.
3. The receiver, which is listening on its home channel, answers a probe
   addressed to it at once, without listening first, with the same
   frame marked as the answer, if it owes no other reply and can afford
   it from the heartbeat reserve; the answer is a reply like an ACK,
   one at a time.
4. On the answer the sender transmits the DATA at once, without another
   check, retunes home and waits for the ACK as today. The frame is
   stamped at the probe, its count and time left as if it went then.
5. No answer in time is one unanswered attempt: the retry wait, then
   the next attempt's check and probe, three in all, then the failure
   and the ban, as for a frame that went unanswered. A frame with the
   long preamble, to a node receiving duty-cycled, is not probed: the
   probe would need the long preamble too and cost most of what it
   saves.

What it is for: a DATA is sent only to a receiver that has just said it
is listening, so a frame is not spent on a receiver that is
transmitting, checking, retuning or away, or whose channel is busy at
its end where the sender cannot hear it; the hidden terminal is met
with a 15.7 ms probe instead of a 138.6 ms frame. What it costs: a probe
and an answer on every hand-over, 31.4 ms, a fifth of a full frame and
its ACK, both paid from the duty-cycle bucket, the answer from the
receiver's. The sender rests on the receiver's channel between the
probe and the answer, so the simulator's count of resting-channel
changes, "home moves" in its summary line, includes two per probe under
B2 (4.4 M on the dense city against 34 000); the core's own count of
moves, in the per-node counters, is the right one.

**A+B2.** Both.

## How it was measured

Every run is the scenario file at the seed named, with `--json` and
`--messages`; the figures are the JSON summary's: delivered of the texts
sent in the settled hour and of all texts, latency, the run's airtime
and its DATA, ACK and probe parts, the cores' counters summed over the
nodes, drops and texts lost by reason, the relays whose load sample ever
found the bucket under its reserve, and the largest settled airtime of a
node. Main's figures are runs of `09b7d73` made for this note; each
form's are the branch's binary at `bc29067` with the form's switches in
the scenario's `[protocol]` section. The three-times-rate S4 is
`s4-capacity.toml` with `per_leaf_per_hour` 2.875.

Two of the rows read differently since D90. "Failed, receiver had it"
counts a failed hand-over whose receiver has a record of the message,
which under D90 a relay keeps after answering seen or passing the
message on, holding no copy; so it bounds the splits from above. "Copies
answered" are the copies answered seen, merged or taken that were not
returns; nearly all are seen now.

## What it shows

- **At the design rate the forms are neutral in delivery.** The dense
  city, S4, the walkers, the ring and the mesh move by a point or less
  under A and B1, within the seeds' spread; A spends 6 and 11 % less
  airtime on the dense city's two seeds and 2 % less on S4.
- **Under load A is worth 9 to 29 points, and the ACK at once is most
  of it.** At three times the design rate A delivers 63.6 / 61.1 /
  77.5 % against main's 51.3 / 52.1 / 48.8 %, with 11 / 10 / 23 % less
  airtime, the median latency halved or better, a third fewer texts
  lost at the time to live and a quarter fewer waiting at their
  origin. B1 gets 57.0 / 55.8 / 64.8 %: taking nothing an unsent ACK
  would have left behind is worth about half, and sending the ACK at
  once, so that it is not left behind at all, the rest. Hand-overs that
  fail fall from 184 900 to 139 390 on seed 1, and those that fail with
  the receiver holding a record from 44 738 to 28 661.
- **The silent take is common, and A ends it.** Under B1, which counts
  them, main leaves an ACK unsent after the take 4 299 and 4 181 times
  in the dense city's hour and 14 557 to 20 252 times under load; under
  A the ACK never waits for the channel, and the packets left untaken,
  20 to 500 a run, are those the budget would not answer.
- **The probe halves delivery under load.** B2 delivers 26.6 / 26.1 /
  25.2 % at three times the rate and A+B2 33.0 / 33.0 / 34.8 %: the
  probes and answers take 57 604 of 186 457 s, hand-overs held back by
  the bucket go from 1.0 M to 22 M, and texts lost at the time to live
  from 6 603 to 17 246. There the relays sit at their cap on every form
  (9.9 % of airtime), so the bucket binds and not the channel; a probe
  spends the sender's bucket and its answer the receiver's reserve on a
  hand-over the receiver may then refuse for want of budget, and a
  frame that waits for the bucket waits longer with the probe's airtime
  added to it.
- **Where the channel binds, the probe is worth a quarter of the
  airtime.** On the dense city B2 delivers the same as main at 231 861
  against 299 754 s on seed 1 and 224 803 against 305 040 s on seed 7,
  with a third of main's DATA frames, half its hand-overs held back by
  the bucket and no relay ever at its cap against 108; on the walkers
  12 % less airtime at the same delivery. The cost is latency: the
  median on S4 rises from 1.4 to 1.8 s, and in the mesh burst by a
  fifth.
- **The mesh is whole under every form.** 98 to 100 of 100 in the
  burst, 100 % spread, the reachable texts at seed 5.

## What it revealed

- **Two frames in three on the dense city go to a receiver that cannot
  take them.** Under B2 a frame goes only after an answer: 2.39 M probes
  were sent, 1.02 M answered, and 0.51 M DATA frames followed, against
  main's 1.37 M DATA frames for the same texts delivered. So 57 % of the
  probes found the receiver deaf or busy, and half the answers did not
  reach the sender, on the receiver's channel where every sender to
  that receiver transmits. The storm note's count of frames to a node
  that was not sleepy, 42 % received, said the same from the receiver's
  side; the probe says it from the sender's, per hand-over, and shows
  that the DATA airtime of a dense network is mostly frames nobody
  receives. A probe is the wrong instrument where the bucket binds, but
  the finding stands for any rule that keeps a frame off a deaf
  receiver.
- **The take at the ACK has almost no occasion once the ACK goes at
  once.** The simulator's radio is deaf only during its own excursions,
  so an ACK sent at once goes out, and a reply fails only for budget,
  which the relay checks before it owes the reply. On the board the
  reply can still fail, for a radio fault or a held check; the rule
  makes that harmless.
- **A sender at the cap under D90 bans and demotes more than it hands
  over.** On the dense city main counts 525 536 failed hand-overs
  against 438 601 taken, and 217 639 demotions; A brings the failures
  to 480 395 and the demotions to 201 105. Most of these failures are
  frames lost on the way, not splits, as the probe's count of deaf
  receivers says; what to do about a neighbour that is heard but cannot
  be reached is the D90 note's open question, not this one's.

## What it does not show

- **Parts 3 and 4 of the candidate**, alone or with A: an attempt as a
  transmission, and a wait that is never idle. On this base the
  listen-before-talk give-up on a retry moves a message its neighbour
  may hold, as before, and the probe's own give-up does the same.
- **A storm.** No form was measured against one, since none of the
  seeds run storms on today's main; the splits are measured by their
  counts.
- **Other seeds of `dense-power.toml`** and of the mesh.
- **A cheaper probe.** One sent only when the check found the channel
  busy recently, or only for full frames, or paid outside the bucket as
  a reply is, was not built; nor a probe with the long preamble.
- **The board.** Nothing here was run on hardware; the answer's wait of
  18.5 ms assumes the measured turnarounds of `docs/lean-driver-2026-10-30.md`.
