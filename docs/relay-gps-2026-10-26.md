# How a relay runs its GPS

**Date:** 2026-10-26. **Status:** adopted 2026-10-27 as roadmap D82,
option E; spec "Position" carries it. Spec references are to
`protocol.md`. Begun on main `da979a9`, rebased onto main `7820f1f`
(D81) and measured there.

## Summary

- **Recommended (E):** a relay's GPS runs all the time on external power
  and through its first hour on battery, then rests in standby,
  searching at boot and when its fixed relay neighbours' heartbeats
  change more than fading explains; a far fix keeps it running until
  the node is carried. A map point rests it always.
- **Battery:** a relay on battery lasts 101 h on 80 % of 850 mAh under
  E, 106 h with the GPS resting at once, 19 h with it always on. In a
  day's outage (`grid-down.toml`) every relay turns sleepy under always
  on, the first after 8.9 hours, and the last hours deliver 2 to 12 %;
  under E none turns and every hour delivers 95 to 96 %.
- **Carried detection:** within the hour E is today's rule (p50 117 s
  after a walker sets off). After it the radio's sign sees 59 to 66 % of
  walks within the walk, p50 250 to 284 s; resting the GPS from the
  first second of battery costs 0.22 points of `dense-power.toml`;
  resting it with no radio sign costs 11 points.
- **Not proposed:** the radio's sign for sleepy nodes (one neighbour, a
  costly action, a small gain).

## Question

Roadmap D80 decided when a sleepy node runs its GPS. A relay's still
runs all the time, on USB and on battery. The L76K draws 29 mA while it
tracks (L76K Hardware Design V1.0, Table 2). On USB that costs nothing.
On battery it now matters: the decision being merged as D81
(`docs/battery-role-2026-10-25.md` on its branch) lets a fixed relay
that loses external power relay on until its cell reads 20 %, so a relay
can run on battery for days. That note's arithmetic: a relay's radio
draws 6.4 mA, so 80 % of 850 mAh, 680 mAh, lasts 106 h; with the GPS
tracking as well, 35.4 mA, 19 h (16 h at the median of its spread of
cells and readings).

The user's thoughts, to be tested:

- (a) a relay whose position is a map point never runs its GPS;
- (b) both relays and sleepy nodes might run the GPS again when the
  radio says they may have moved: when their fixed neighbours, or those
  with the map-point flag, and their signal strengths change more than
  fading explains.

## What a relay uses its own fixes for

From spec "Nodes", "Position", "Sleepy nodes", "Implementation notes"
and the core (`relay.rs`, `relay/motion.rs`, `relay/power.rs`,
`relay/gps.rs`):

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2. | 3. Its position while carried: each fix (spec "Nodes") | its neighbours, at each heartbeat slot | 50 m | each heartbeat, 5 to 15 s | it is routed to where it was |
| 4. Its address while fixed | senders, routing | the address is the place it stands (use 1), not a fresh fix | none | unchanged |
| 5. The place kept across a reboot (D73) | itself after a reboot | as use 1 | none: the place does not change while fixed | unchanged |
| 6. The position it takes when plugged in (`fixed_here`) | as use 1 | 50 m | at plug-in | it is the sleepy node's last fix, which D80 may have left hours old; a relay that tracks finds out within 72 s through use 2, and is then carried for four hours |
| 7. A map point (D77): set by the app, it wins over any fix | its neighbours read the flag | — | — | the driver hands no fix while a map point is set (`core-interface.md` §4), so the GPS has no use at all |
| 8. The decoding of the short positions it hears (spec "Positions on the wire") | itself | within 12.5 km | none: its own position is the place it stands | unchanged |
| 9. The fix checks: a fix 3 km from every relay it knows is dropped (D74) | its driver | — | — | not a use of fixes; a check on them |

So a fixed relay needs a fix at its first boot (use 1) and at plug-in
(use 6), and then for one thing: to see that it is carried (use 2).
While carried it needs one at each heartbeat (use 3). Nothing else a
fixed relay does reads a fix. A map point (use 7) leaves the GPS with no
use.

Use 2 on battery is new with D81. Under D71 a relay on battery turned
sleepy within the hour's grace whatever its GPS said; under D81 a fixed
relay on battery stays a relay until 20 %, and only a carried one keeps
D71's grace and turns sleepy. So under D81 the carried flag is what
turns a person's node, unplugged and put in a pocket, sleepy: missed, the
node relays at 6.4 mA from the pocket for four days instead of sleeping
at 0.64 mA for 56, and the mesh routes to the windowsill it left.

## The options

- **A. Always** (today): the GPS tracks while the node is a relay.
- **B. Battery events**: tracks on USB; on battery it rests in standby
  and searches only at the events a relay on battery has: its boot. It
  sees no motion on battery.
- **C. Map point**: as A, but a relay with a map point never runs it.
- **D. Radio**: tracks on USB; on battery it rests in standby and
  searches when the radio says it may have moved (below). A search that
  finds a far fix keeps the GPS on until the node is carried or a fix
  comes near again; one that finds a near fix ends at once.
- **D everywhere**: D on USB too, to measure the radio's sign alone,
  and as the option "a radio-triggered search for every node".
- **E. Grace**: tracks on USB and through D71's hour of grace on
  battery, then as D. Uses 1 to 3 say this is enough if the radio's
  sign is: a person who unplugs a node and walks off does so within the
  hour, and a relay left on battery past it, in an outage, moves only
  when somebody carries it.

**The radio's sign of motion.** A relay hears each neighbour's heartbeat
on its home channel once a rotation, 130 s on average, 195 s at most,
and keeps the averaged RSSI of each neighbour, each new frame counting
half (spec "Neighbour table"). Under fading of σ = 4 dB (D38,
`radio::FADE_DEPTH_DB`), with a correlation time of 60 s in the
scenarios, heartbeats a rotation apart are nearly independent
(e^(−130/60) = 0.11), so the average varies as σ²/3, and the difference
between a new heartbeat and the average before it, its **surprise**, as
σ² + σ²/3 = 4σ²/3, 4.6 dB.

Over a worst-case rotation, 195 s, the node sums the squared surprises
of the heartbeats of its fixed relay neighbours, each over 4σ²/3. For a
still node the sum of k of them is chi-square with k degrees of freedom.
The node searches when the sum passes its two-standard-deviation point,
which a still node passes in 2.3 % of rotations, the same convention as
the switching margin (spec "Relay choice"). The point is taken from the
Wilson–Hilferty approximation, k (1 − 2/(9k) + 2 √(2/(9k)))³, which is
within a few percent of the exact quantile for every k.

What moving does to it. Under the scenarios' path loss, exponent 3.6
(spec "Relay choice"), a node that moves x towards or away from a
neighbour r away changes its RSSI by 36 log10(1 + x/r) dB. A walker at
1.4 m/s moves 180 m in an average rotation; for neighbours 500 m away in
random directions the RMS change is about 4 dB, 16 dB², which raises
the mean squared surprise from 1 to 1.75; with 20 neighbours the sum's
two-sigma point is 1.63 a heartbeat, so path loss alone passes it in
most rotations. The scenarios' shadowing, 6 dB that changes over 50 m
of a walker's path, adds up to 72 dB², and the step out of a building,
14.4 dB on every link at once, more still. So a walker should be seen
within its first rotation or two.

What it costs. At 2.3 % of 18.5 rotations an hour, a still relay
searches 0.42 times an hour: 0.007 mA where it sees the sky (a hot
start), and 0.20 mA where it never does (a 60 s search each time). With
no back-off this is bounded by itself, at most 3 % of the radio's
6.4 mA, so a relay searches with no back-off, unlike a sleepy node,
whose budget is ten times smaller.

What it misses:

- a node moved while switched off: its boot is an event of its own,
  and it searches then;
- a node driven: at 10 m/s it is 1.3 km away within a rotation, few of
  its neighbours are heard twice, and few surprises come;
- a node in a quiet place with no fixed relay neighbours, or one in a
  dense place whose full table keeps replacing them: no surprises, or
  few;
- a node moved and put down among neighbours that look like the old
  ones: only the move itself is seen, while it is under way;
- a change around a still node, a lorry parked under the window or a
  new wall: a search for nothing, 0.016 mAh.

**What I expect, written before measuring:**

| Option | GPS on battery | 680 mAh lasts | a walked relay on battery turns carried | delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 29 mA | 19 h | ≈108 s after it is picked up: 36 s to leave the allowance at 1.4 m/s, then 72 s; D70 measured p50 77 s from the first far fix | as main |
| B | 0.02 mA | 106 h | not while on battery; in `dense-power.toml`, at its next stop, where it is on USB | texts to relays that walk while on battery fall towards D70's 58 % before carried existed; the whole domain loses a point or less |
| C | as A without a map point, 0.02 mA with one | 19 or 106 h | as A; with a map point never (no fix is handed) | as A: no scenario has map points |
| D | 0.03 to 0.22 mA | 103 to 106 h | within a rotation or two of leaving the allowance, then 72 s: 3 to 5 minutes | between A and B, nearer A |
| D everywhere | as D on USB too | — | as D for every relay, also on USB | `dense-movers.toml`'s walking relays a point or two below A |

The simulator cannot yet run a relay on battery past its grace (D81 is
not on main), so D and B are measured where relays are on battery today,
`dense-power.toml`'s walkers and switching nodes within their grace, and
"D everywhere" and "B everywhere" in `dense-movers.toml` and S4 stand
for a relay on battery past its grace: every relay there is treated as
on battery for its GPS alone.

**And a sleepy node.** Asleep, a sleepy node hears one relay, its
anchor, once a rotation, so its radio's sign is one surprise a rotation.
At two standard deviations it acts in 2.3 % of rotations for nothing,
0.42 times an hour; what it would act on costs it far more than a relay:
a search that finds nothing indoors is 0.48 mAh, a third of its 0.63 mA
at that rate, and a check of the anchor, a rotation of listening at
5.8 mA, 0.31 mAh, a fifth. D80's back-off of 8 h would hold such
searches back, and with them the searches it is there for. What it would
buy is the move check, which D80 measured at two points of the texts to
walkers for a fix at every beat. So the sign is not proposed for a
sleepy node, and not measured; its anchor missed twice still starts its
check (spec "Relay choice").

## How it was run

`cargo build --release -p bark-sim`, then for each option a copy of the
scenario with `[protocol] relay_gps = "…"` appended:

```
bark-sim SCENARIO --seed N --json OUT.json --messages OUT.csv [--battery OUT.bat] > OUT.txt
```

Delivered is of the texts sent in the settled hour, 2700 to 6300 s,
from `OUT.csv` (`fate` = `delivered`); "to walking relays" is those
whose destination was a relay walking when they were sent (`dst_sleepy`
false, `dst_walking` true). The GPS currents, the radio's sign and the
walks are the lines `relays' GPS`, `radio sign of …` and `walks of
fixed relays` of `OUT.txt`; the split between relays that walk and those
that do not is from the per-node `gps` of `OUT.json`. `grid-down.toml`
is read by phase, as the D81 note does: texts delivered within an hour,
the run's last hour left out; the charge from `OUT.bat`.

| walking relays on USB | `dense-movers.toml` | 1 | A, B everywhere, D everywhere |

### The surprise against the previous heartbeat

Asked at adoption: since a still relay's surprises vary less than their
derivation says, try the simpler definition, a heartbeat's RSSI less the
same neighbour's previous heartbeat's, whose variance under fading is
2σ² whatever else the average takes in. It needs the last heartbeat's
RSSI in the neighbour entry, two bytes that fit its padding.
`dense-movers.toml` seed 1 with every relay resting, both forms on the
adopted core (before the switch went), and S4 seed 1:

| | average (adopted) | previous heartbeat |
|---|---|---|
| still relays' mean squared surprise, over the form's own variance | 0.38 (S4 0.32) | 0.38 (S4 0.39) |
| still relays' rotations that fired | 0.66 % | 0.58 % |
| walking relays' rotations that fired; their mean squared surprise | 75.2 %; 3.03 | 70.4 %; 2.48 |
| walks carried within the walk; p50 / p90 after setting off | 298 of 458; 249 / 339 s | 303 of 465; 267 / 371 s |
| time walked while fixed | 27.7 h | 29.8 h |
| delivered | 95.78 % | 95.81 % |
| still relays' GPS | 0.068 mA | 0.061 mA |

Neither form meets its derivation: both measure 0.38 of it. The cause is
not how the average is kept but which frames are heard at all: a frame
in a deep fade is lost, so the heartbeats that reach the receiver are
cut off below sensitivity, and the packet RSSI adds the noise to the
signal; both shrink any difference of two readings. The previous
heartbeat sees walkers later, so the average stays, and the spec's
reason states the measured variance.

## What matched and what did not

- **Matched:** A's detection, p50 117 s from setting off (36 s to leave
  the allowance, then the 72 s, at a fix a heartbeat). B's cost, standby.
  E equal to A wherever walks end within the grace. D between A and B
  and near A in delivery.
- **B is far worse than expected.** I expected a point or less of the
  domain; it lost 11 points in `dense-power.toml` and 4 in
  `dense-movers.toml`, and the airtime per delivered text rose elevenfold.
  A relay that walks fixed keeps owning its old place, holding mail there
  and anchoring sleepy nodes there, and senders chase its old position
  for the whole walk; D70 measured the same before carried existed.
- **The radio's sign is quieter than derived.** A still relay's mean
  squared surprise is 0.3 to 0.46, not 1 (above, "What the radio's sign
  does"), so it searches for nothing a fifth to a twentieth as often as
  expected, and a walker still passes the test in 77 to 99 % of its
  rotations.
- **D is slower than expected.** p50 249 to 284 s after setting off
  against my 3 to 5 minutes' upper end; 59 to 66 % of walks turn carried
  within the walk against A's 82 to 88 %. The test closes a rotation
  only at its end, 195 s; then a search, a cold start of 30 s for a
  relay whose GPS rested indoors, and the 72 s.
- **D everywhere delivers to walking relays better than A in
  `dense-movers.toml`** (99.25 against 97.74 %, 133 texts) but worse
  overall (95.78 against 96.42 %): within one seed, and the texts to the
  walkers are few.

## Recommendation

Adopt E: a relay's GPS runs all the time on external power and through
its first hour on battery; after that it rests in standby and searches
only at boot and when its radio says it may have moved, and a search
that finds a far fix keeps it running until the node is carried or a fix
is near. A relay with a map point never runs it.

- On USB it costs nothing, and a node on USB in a car needs it.
- The hour on battery is when a person unplugs a node and walks off
  with it. E is A there: in `dense-power.toml` it turns the walker
  carried in 117 s and delivers what A delivers, where D loses 0.22
  points and doubles the time walking relays spend fixed at the wrong
  place.
- After the hour it costs 29 mAh once, then D's 0.02 to 0.12 mA: a
  relay on battery lasts 101 h on 680 mAh against 19 h. In a day's
  outage the relays end at 76 % of their charge under E; under A every
  relay reached its low level and turned sleepy, the first after 8.9
  hours, and the outage's last five hours delivered 2 to 12 %.
- The radio's sign keeps the case a person picks up a relay that has
  been on battery for hours, in an outage: 59 to 66 % of such walks are
  seen within the walk, p50 4.7 minutes after setting off. B, no
  search on battery at all, is not an option: it lost 11 points.
- A map point already stops the fixes (`core-interface.md` §4); the GPS
  then has no use, and resting it saves the 29 mA on USB too, which
  matters for a solar relay (O17).

D alone (rest from the moment the power goes) saves only the first
hour's 29 mAh, 4 % of the cell, for 0.22 points of delivery and twice
the time walking relays spend at the wrong place.

## The spec text as proposed

For spec "Beats", in place of its last sentence of the GPS paragraph, "A
relay's GPS runs all the time.":

> A relay's GPS runs all the time on external power and for the grace
> after it is lost, and rests in standby once a relay has been on
> battery longer, or whenever its position is a map point (roadmap D82).
> A resting relay searches at boot, and when the heartbeats of its fixed
> relay neighbours change more than fading explains. Over a worst-case
> rotation it sums the squares of each such heartbeat's difference from
> that neighbour's averaged RSSI, each over 4σ²/3, the variance fading of
> σ = 4 dB gives it ([neighbour table](#neighbour-table)), and searches
> when the sum passes its two-standard-deviation point as a chi-square of
> as many degrees as heartbeats. A search that finds a far fix
> ([position](#position)) keeps the GPS running until the node is carried
> or a fix is near again. A relay's search has no back-off.
>
> *Why it runs through the grace.* A person who unplugs a node and walks
> off does so then, and the 72 s of far fixes that make it carried
> (D70) need the GPS running. After the grace a relay on battery is
> one left where it stands, in an outage (roadmap D81), and 29 mA would
> take its battery to the low level in 19 hours instead of 106.
>
> *Why no back-off.* Even a relay that never sees the sky and passes the
> test in 2.3 % of rotations, which a still relay does at most, spends
> 0.2 mA on searches, 3 % of its 6.4 mA.

And under "Fixed and carried" in spec "Position", no change: the rule is
the same, fed by the fixes a running GPS gives.

## Found on the way

- **The first fix of a new board is a far fix.** Spec "Position" says
  the place a fixed node stands is its first accepted fix. The core has
  no "no position yet": it starts at the position the driver boots it
  with (`Config::position`, 0, 0 in the firmware without
  `BARK_LAT`/`BARK_LON`), so its first fix is a far fix, it turns
  carried 72 s later and is fixed again only after four hours, holding
  no mail meanwhile. The simulator never shows it, since it boots every
  node at its true place. It wants a rule of its own: a node that has no
  stored place and no map point takes its first accepted fix as its
  place.
- **The map point is never cleared by a fix.** Spec "Packet formats"
  says a node clears the map point when it takes a fix as its position;
  `core-interface.md` §4 says the driver hands no fix while a map point
  is set. So only the app can clear it, and the app has no command to.
- **At plug-in a relay keeps a sleepy node's last fix** (`fixed_here`),
  which D80 may have left hours old; a relay that runs its GPS finds out
  through the carried rule and is then carried for four hours. Nobody
  addresses a node at its own position before it is a relay, so the
  first fix after plug-in could be taken as its place at once. Not
  measured.

## What this does not show

- **Indoor reception.** Every indoor node hears no sky (`no_sky = 1`), as
  in D80. Many people's relays sit at windows; with the sky they would
  get hot starts and their searches would cost a tenth.
- **The fading of still links.** The test is derived from D38's 4 dB,
  an assumption to be replaced in Phase 2. The simulator's fading is
  that 4 dB by construction, so the false rate measured here is the
  model's. Still indoor links likely fade less, which makes the test
  quieter still; a busy street likely more.
- **Real walkers.** In the simulator a walker steps out of a building
  (14.4 dB on every link at once) and its shadowing changes every 50 m;
  both make the sign strong. A node carried within one building, or
  along a street of the same kind, gives a weaker one.
- **A car.** No scenario drives relays on battery; the sign is expected
  to see few surprises at 10 m/s (above).
- **The carried rule itself** is fed a fix a heartbeat, not a second,
  in every option.
- **One seed for the outages and for `dense-movers.toml`.**

## What I am unsure of

- **Hot start.** The recommendation leans on searches being cheap where
  the sky is in view: a 2 s hot start with orbits under four hours old
  (L76K Table 2, IS-GPS-200). After an hour on battery the orbits are
  fresh; hours later they are not, and every search is a 30 s cold
  start, 0.24 mAh, still 0.1 mA at the worst rate measured. If the
  board does not keep the L76K's backup supply (a Phase 2 bench item),
  every search is cold; D's detection then takes 30 s longer, and E's
  first hour is unaffected.
- **Standby.** 20 µA needs the WAKEUP pin or the backup supply wired as
  the hardware design says; if the board can only cut the L76K's power,
  resting costs nothing and every start is cold.
- **The grace as the window.** People may unplug a node and leave with
  it hours later. E then relies on the radio's sign, which missed a
  third of walks within the walk here.
- **The test's point.** Two standard deviations is the switching
  margin's convention, not an optimum. With the false rate measured an
  order below the derived one, a lower point would see walkers sooner;
  ending a rotation as soon as its sum passes the point it needs would
  too. Neither is measured.
