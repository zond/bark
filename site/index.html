bark

Small radio nodes that carry text messages across a city when the phone network and the internet are down.

Get the app

Android asks once to allow installs from your browser.

Then open the app and pair with a node using the code on its screen, or use a public node nearby.

Read more

A message travels toward a place: each node passes it on to the neighbour nearest to where the recipient's node is. Every node learns where it stands from its GPS, so the network needs to know a place for each person it delivers to.

A café, a library or a school can run a public node. Anyone nearby can use it with the app, without pairing, and the node's place is where messages for its guests arrive. They read them on their next visit.

An address is a node's place and its ID. There is nothing to register and no list kept by the network, so anyone who has an address can write to it whenever the network stands. The app puts a person's address on their card, which they can share or print where people see it.

A person who moves around has no fixed place, so they can make a node their home: a node at home on a charger, its place set on the map in the app, whose address goes on their card. Messages for them wait there until they come home or fetch them with the app from anywhere the network reaches.

In the same way, a police station, a fire station, a hospital or another emergency service with a node at a fixed place is reachable at its address for good.

Every message carries, encrypted with the rest, the address of the node its sender writes from. A reply sent soon after goes the shortest way to where the sender is now, not to where they were or to their home node, so a person on the move can be answered wherever they last wrote from.
